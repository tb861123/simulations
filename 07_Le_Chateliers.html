Le Chatelier's Principle:
If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to oppose the change.

↑ [Reactant] → shifts RIGHT
↑ Temperature (exothermic) → shifts LEFT
↑ Temperature (endothermic) → shifts RIGHT
↑ Pressure → shifts to fewer gas moles
Catalyst: faster rate only — does NOT change position.
Kc = [products]^p / [reactants]^r
K changes with T; not affected by P, conc or catalyst.

[4 marks] In the Haber process, N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)  ΔH = −92 kJ/mol. Explain why a temperature of about 450°C is used rather than a much higher or much lower temperature.

At low temperatures, the rate of reaction is too slow [1];
the forward reaction is exothermic, so higher temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left [1];
this decreases the yield of ammonia [1];
450°C is a compromise between an acceptable rate and an acceptable yield [1].

[3 marks] The equilibrium N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) is established in a sealed container. State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on this equilibrium.

The equilibrium shifts to the left / towards N₂O₄ [1];
because increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium towards the side with fewer moles of gas [1];
there is 1 mole of gas on the left and 2 moles on the right [1].

[3 marks] A solution at equilibrium contains CoCl₄²⁻ (blue) and Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ (pink): CoCl₄²⁻(aq) + 6H₂O(l) ⇌ Co(H₂O)₆²⁺(aq) + 4Cl⁻(aq)  ΔH = +50 kJ/mol. Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added. State the colour change and explain it using Le Chatelier's principle.

The solution turns blue [1];
adding HCl increases the concentration of Cl⁻ ions [1];
the equilibrium shifts to the left to reduce the Cl⁻ concentration, forming more CoCl₄²⁻ [1].
