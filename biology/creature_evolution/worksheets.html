Teacher notes

Investigation 1: From flailing to walking

What to look for: Usually long flat spells, then a jump when a mutation or crossover hits on a better rhythm, which then spreads through the population (watch one colour take over). The median catches up with the best a few generations later as the new design's descendants fill the population. Evolution here is blind trial and error plus keeping what works.

Investigation 2: Turn selection off

What to look for: Nothing improves: mutations still happen, but good ones are as likely to be thrown away as bad ones. Fitness wanders and even falls, because most mutations make a working creature worse. Variation alone is not evolution; it needs selection. (The batch run measures this.)

Investigation 3: How much mutation?

What to look for: With no mutation, only crossover reshuffles the starting genes: progress stops almost at once. At 0.8 the best still creeps up, because the survivors are kept unchanged, but most children are much worse than their parents: look how far the median lags behind the best. Real mutation rates are tiny (most genes are copied perfectly), so children resemble their parents (heredity), which selection needs.

Investigation 4: The environment changes

What to look for: Fitness drops at once: the champions were adapted to flat ground. Over the next generations, designs that can climb (taller, longer strides, grippier feet) are selected and fitness recovers. What counts as 'fit' depends entirely on the environment.

Investigation 5: Learning to swim

What to look for: A single muscle squeezing and relaxing pushes water one way, then exactly back the other way (the 'scallop theorem' of slow swimming): no net movement. Successful swimmers use several muscles out of step, so a wave runs along the body or a part sweeps back stiff and returns edge-on, like an oar. Evolution finds this without being told.

Investigation 6: Jumpers

What to look for: Jumpers tend to become compact, stiff bodies with a strong muscle timed to fling the body upwards, sometimes building up a bounce over several cycles (resonance with the springs). Different jobs select different shapes.

Investigation 7: Run it again

What to look for: Almost never: one run evolves a two-legged shuffler, another a tumbling wheel or an inchworm. Which design wins depends on chance (which random creatures happened to start, which mutations happened). Real evolution is also 'contingent': rerun the tape of life and you would not get the same animals.

Batch experiment 1: Walkers: fitness over generations

Each run starts from different random creatures and gets different mutations, so some runs take off early and others stall for a while. On average the best distance climbs steeply at first, then more slowly.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.369 ± 0.0367 · 5: 0.973 ± 0.201 · 10: 3.81 ± 1.09 · 15: 4.95 ± 0.856 · 20: 9.25 ± 4.5 · 30: 9.46 ± 2.81 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Is selection needed?

Without selection, the best distance after 20 generations is no better than the random starters (often worse); with it, several times further. Mutation supplies variation; selection is what turns variation into adaptation.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.148 ± 0.0518 · 1: 5.13 ± 1.54 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Mutation rate

With no mutation, crossover only reshuffles the starting genes and walkers barely improve (about 0.4 m). Any mutation from 0.05 to 0.6 per gene works about equally well here (5–7 m), because the survivors are kept unchanged, so the best design is never lost however wild the children are. Without that, very high rates would wreck good designs.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.394 ± 0.0629 · 0.05: 5.51 ± 2.45 · 0.15: 5.25 ± 1.55 · 0.3: 6.71 ± 2.1 · 0.6: 6.42 ± 1.94 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Swimmers: fitness over generations

Random swimmers barely move: back-and-forth strokes cancel out in slow, syrupy swimming. Evolution finds out-of-step muscle timings that make a wave, so distance climbs over the generations.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.497 ± 0.0643 · 10: 1.25 ± 0.368 · 20: 1.77 ± 0.403 · 30: 2.59 ± 0.655 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)
