Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Limiting factors in a glasshouse

What to look for: In dim light, light is the limiting factor: extra CO₂ hardly helps. With more light, CO₂ becomes limiting and extra CO₂ raises the yield. Growers raise whichever factor is limiting.

Investigation 2: Does it pay?

What to look for: Not always: beyond the optimum the extra yield is small but the costs keep rising. Profit is highest at a moderate temperature and CO₂.

Investigation 3: Fertiliser

What to look for: Yield rises steeply at first, then levels off (another factor becomes limiting). Beyond what the crop can take up, nitrate leaches into rivers: eutrophication.

Investigation 4: The fermenter

What to look for: Enzymes in the bacteria work fastest near 37 °C; colder is slower, and above about 45 °C they denature and the bacteria die. The fermenter has a water jacket to remove the heat the bacteria make.

Investigation 5: Aseptic technique

What to look for: About 4 in 10: other microbes compete for nutrients or spoil the product. Sterilising the fermenter and nutrients with steam, and filtering the air, keeps them out.

Investigation 6: Making human insulin

What to look for: The same enzyme leaves matching sticky ends, which pair up so ligase can join the gene into the plasmid. The bacteria then make human insulin for people with diabetes.

Investigation 7: Selective breeding

What to look for: Several hundred litres per generation at first. Selective breeding can also reduce variety (inbreeding, less resistance to disease).

Investigation 8: Heritability A level

What to look for: Higher heritability, faster progress. Over many generations the real gain falls below R = h²S × generations, because selection uses up the genetic variation (and some is lost by drift).

Investigation 9: Economic optimum A level

What to look for: No: the last few kilograms of nitrogen add too little wheat to pay for themselves, so the economic optimum (about 180 kg/ha here) is below the dose that maximises yield.

Batch experiment 1: Nitrogen and wheat yield

Diminishing returns: each extra kilogram adds less wheat, and the extra is wasted (and pollutes) beyond what the crop can use.
Theory (yield curve): 0: 5.1 · 50: 7.7 · 100: 9.04 · 150: 9.73 · 200: 10.1 · 250: 10.3 · 300: 10.4
Result from a test run: 0: 5.15 ± 0.164 · 50: 7.51 ± 0.111 · 100: 9.08 ± 0.126 · 150: 9.82 ± 0.133 · 200: 10.2 ± 0.158 · 250: 10.3 ± 0.111 · 300: 10.4 ± 0.132 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Selective breeding: does heritability matter?

Higher heritability gives faster progress, but below the breeder's equation: selection uses up the variation it works on.
Theory (breeder's equation × 10): 0.1: 840 · 0.3: 2520 · 0.5: 4200 · 0.7: 5880 · 0.9: 7560
Result from a test run: 0.1: 556 ± 36.2 · 0.3: 1690 ± 83.5 · 0.5: 2690 ± 30.4 · 0.7: 3600 ± 75.8 · 0.9: 4760 ± 50.3 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Fermenter temperature

A sharp optimum near 37 °C (body temperature for the bacteria's enzymes); above about 45 °C almost nothing is made.
Theory (model): 20: 3.92e-7 · 25: 2.22e-5 · 30: 7.95e-4 · 35: 0.592 · 37: 0.757 · 40: 0.398 · 45: 3.10e-4 · 50: 0
Result from a test run: 20: 3.92e-7 ± 0 · 25: 2.22e-5 ± 0 · 30: 7.95e-4 ± 0 · 35: 0.592 ± 0 · 37: 0.757 ± 0 · 40: 0.398 ± 0 · 45: 3.10e-4 ± 0 · 50: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Aseptic technique: how many batches are lost?

A binomial spread around 7.6. With aseptic precautions (about 1% risk) almost none are lost.
Theory (binomial mean): 7.6
Result from a test run: mean 7.5, SD 1.97, SE 0.113 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 5: Limiting factors: CO₂ in dim and bright light

In dim light (points) extra CO₂ helps little: light is limiting. In bright light (line) CO₂ is the limit, so adding it pays.
Theory (at 80% light): 200: 30.3 · 400: 51.8 · 600: 64.3 · 800: 72.4 · 1000: 78.2 · 1200: 82.4
Result from a test run: 200: 17.7 ± 0 · 400: 30.2 ± 0 · 600: 37.5 ± 0 · 800: 42.3 ± 0 · 1000: 45.6 ± 0 · 1200: 48.1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)
