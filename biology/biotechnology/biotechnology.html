How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Glasshouse: photosynthesis is limited by light, carbon dioxide and temperature together: rate = 1 ÷ (1 ÷ light term + 1 ÷ CO₂ term + 1 ÷ temperature term), so the scarcest factor matters most. Temperature has an optimum near 27 °C (enzymes); above it the rate falls fast. The crop grows logistically over 120 days. Costs (heating above the 10 °C outside, CO₂, lamps) and the tomato price are illustrative.
  • Wheat and fertiliser: grain yield = 10.5 × (1 − e^(−(60 + N) ÷ 90)) t/ha (a Mitscherlich curve: soil supplies about 60 kg N/ha); each plot differs by ±8%. Nitrate lost by leaching rises steeply above what the crop can use. Illustrative values, close in shape to UK fertiliser trials.
  • Aphids: logistic growth (×1.25 a day when few) with ladybirds eating them (predator–prey). Each spray kills 95% of susceptible aphids, 10% of resistant ones and 90% of ladybirds; resistance is one gene.
  • Selective breeding: 200 cows; milk yield = 6000 L + 50 L for each of 80 "plus" alleles above 40 (40 genes, two copies each) + environment. The environmental spread is set so that the heritability is as chosen. The best cows (and bulls) by their own yield are parents; each calf gets one random copy of each gene from each parent. Real breeding uses records of relatives, genomic tests and artificial insemination.
  • Fermenter: growth rate = 0.4 an hour × glucose ÷ (glucose + 0.5) × temperature, pH and oxygen factors, with a 2-hour lag; yield 0.5 g of cells per g of glucose. Respiration heats the broth: without the water jacket the temperature climbs. Penicillin is made when glucose runs low (a secondary metabolite); insulin is made as the GM bacteria grow (primary); yeast makes ethanol when oxygen is short. Without aseptic technique, a contaminant may arrive (5% chance an hour) and out-grow the culture.
  • Plate counts: colonies = Poisson(cells per cm³ × 10^−dilution × 0.1 cm³ × 0.9), with ±3% pipetting error at each transfer and 10% of cells in clumps.
  • Immobilised lactase: lactose converted = 1 − e^(−k × time in the column); k rises with temperature (×2 per 10 °C) until the enzyme denatures (above 55 °C immobilised, 45 °C free) and falls with bead diameter (less surface). Beads lose 0.5% activity per run.
  • Genetic modification: matching restriction enzymes leave matching sticky ends; 40% of plasmids close up without the gene; 1 bacterium in 10 000 takes up a plasmid. GM crops: yield losses to pests and weeds and the evolution of Bt resistance (one gene, partly recessive; refuges of non-GM plants keep susceptible insects) are illustrative.
  • Gene therapy: treated lung cells are replaced with a half-life of about 4 weeks, so the effect fades; edited blood stem cells last for life; germ-line changes are in every cell and inherited.
  • PCR: each cycle every target copy is copied with a chance set by how well the primers anneal (best 3–5 °C below their 62 °C melting temperature; too low and other products appear); copying slows as primers and nucleotides run out (plateau about 10¹² copies). Gels: distance ∝ (a − b log₁₀ size) × voltage × time, ±0.5 mm. Sequencing: chain-terminating nucleotides stop 1 in 20 new strands at each base.
  • Cloning: each subculture gives up to 6 shoots per explant depending on the cytokinin : auxin ratio; unsterile work loses 30% of vessels. Dolly: 277 fused eggs, 29 embryos, 1 lamb (real numbers).
  • Quality only changes the picture; the model and the batch runs are the same at every level.
