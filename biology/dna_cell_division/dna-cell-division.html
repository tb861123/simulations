How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • DNA is drawn as a flat, twisting ladder: two sugar–phosphate backbones running in opposite directions (antiparallel) joined by base pairs, A–T (2 hydrogen bonds) and C–G (3). Real DNA makes a full turn every 10 base pairs and is 2 nm wide.
  • Protein synthesis: RNA polymerase copies the template strand into mRNA (U instead of T); a ribosome reads the mRNA three bases (one codon) at a time; tRNA molecules with the matching anticodon bring the amino acids, which are joined by peptide bonds. The real standard genetic code is used. Real genes have introns that are cut out (splicing) and much longer sequences; the mRNA leaves the nucleus through a pore. The β-globin sequence is the real start of the human gene.
  • Mutations: one base substituted, inserted or deleted. Their effect is worked out with the real code: silent (same amino acid), missense (a different amino acid), nonsense (an early stop codon) or frameshift (every codon after it changes). Mutation rates are scaled up enormously so they can be seen.
  • Replication (A level): helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds; free nucleotides pair with each exposed base; DNA polymerase joins them. Left out: the leading and lagging strands, Okazaki fragments, ligase and primers.
  • Meselson and Stahl: 2000 DNA molecules, copied each generation by the chosen rule; a random half is kept (the culture is diluted), then 200 are sampled for the centrifuge. Bands are at 1.724 (¹⁵N), 1.717 (hybrid) and 1.710 g/cm³ (¹⁴N) (approximate values from the 1958 paper).
  • Cell cycle: phase lengths are approximate textbook values. Mitosis is slowed down in the animation compared with interphase; the clock shows real hours. Checkpoints (A level) are simplified to three: G1, G2 and the spindle assembly checkpoint in metaphase.
  • Root tip squash: the chance that a cell is in each phase = time in that phase ÷ cell cycle time (a steady population). Cells stop cycling between about 1.5 and 3 mm from the tip. Counters miss very early prophase (30% of prophase cells) because it looks like interphase. Real roots also vary from root to root and with the time of day.
  • Meiosis: homologous pairs line up at random (independent assortment); at A level one chiasma per pair swaps the ends of two non-sister chromatids. Left out: several chiasmata per pair, chromosomes of different sizes and shapes, and non-disjunction in meiosis.
  • Lac operon (A level): the operator switches at random between repressed and free; mRNA is made while it is free and broken down with a half-life of about 1.4 minutes (illustrative: lac mRNA is short-lived; the mean for E. coli mRNAs is about 5 min, genome-wide study, PNAS 2002); rates are approximate. The culture graph is a simple growth model.
  • Quality only changes the picture (free nucleotides, shading, detail); the model and the batch runs are the same at every level.
