Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Base pairing

What to look for: A pairs with T, C pairs with G (complementary base pairing). So in double-stranded DNA the amount of A equals T and C equals G (Chargaff's rule), though A + T need not equal C + G.

Investigation 2: Making a protein

What to look for: DNA coding strand ATG → mRNA AUG (U replaces T) → tRNA anticodon UAC, carrying methionine, the start of every protein. The ribosome reads one codon (3 bases) at a time and joins the amino acids into a chain.

Investigation 3: Sickle cell

What to look for: Codon 7 GAG → GTG: glutamic acid (charged, water-loving) becomes valine (hydrophobic). The haemoglobin molecules stick together at low oxygen, so red cells become sickle-shaped and block capillaries.

Investigation 4: Three kinds of substitution

What to look for: The code is degenerate: several codons code for the same amino acid, so many substitutions (especially in the third base) are silent. Missense changes one amino acid; nonsense makes a stop codon, cutting the protein short.

Investigation 5: Frameshift

What to look for: Inserting or deleting a base shifts the reading frame: every codon after it is read differently, so the rest of the protein is different (and often hits a stop codon soon). The nearer the start, the worse.

Investigation 6: Mitosis

What to look for: DNA is copied in S phase (interphase), so each chromosome becomes two identical chromatids. In mitosis the chromatids are pulled apart, so each new cell gets an identical, complete set (2n): used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Investigation 7: Root tip squash

What to look for: Near the tip (the meristem) about 10–15% of cells are in mitosis; 2–3 mm back almost none: the cells there are elongating and differentiating instead of dividing.

Investigation 8: Meiosis makes variety

What to look for: Each pair lines up at random, so each gamete gets one of each pair at random: 2ⁿ kinds. 2 pairs: 4; 3 pairs: 8; humans: 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million, before crossing over adds even more. Random fertilisation then multiplies this again.

Investigation 9: Crossing over A level

What to look for: No: at each chiasma, chromatids of a homologous pair swap sections, so each chromosome in a gamete can be a new mix of maternal and paternal alleles. Together with independent assortment, almost every gamete is unique.

Investigation 10: Phase durations A level

What to look for: Time in a phase ≈ (cells in that phase ÷ total cells) × cell cycle time. For example 5 metaphase cells out of 250: 5/250 × 20 h = 0.4 h (24 minutes).

Batch experiment 1: How often is a substitution silent?

About a quarter of substitutions change nothing, because the genetic code is degenerate (mostly a change in the third base of a codon).
Theory (exact): 26.1
Result from a test run: mean 26.1, SD 4.92, SE 0.348 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 2: Frameshift: where it happens matters

An insertion early in the gene changes almost every amino acid after it; near the end, only a few. (The first amino acid after the insertion sometimes stays the same by chance.)
Result from a test run: 2: 16.2 ± 0.2 · 4: 14.6 ± 0.245 · 6: 12.8 ± 0.2 · 8: 9.6 ± 0.245 · 10: 7.8 ± 0.2 · 12: 5.6 ± 0.245 · 14: 3.2 ± 0.2 · 16: 2 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Meiosis: how many kinds of gamete?

The number of kinds doubles with every extra pair. 500 gametes are enough to find all 64 kinds with 6 pairs; for humans (23 pairs, 8.4 million kinds) you would need millions.
Theory (2ⁿ): 1: 2 · 2: 4 · 3: 8 · 4: 16 · 5: 32 · 6: 64
Result from a test run: 1: 2 ± 0 · 2: 4 ± 0 · 3: 8 ± 0 · 4: 16 ± 0 · 5: 32 ± 0 · 6: 64 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Mitotic index along a root

Cell division happens in the meristem near the tip; further back the cells elongate and differentiate. Counting only 150 cells gives a few % of scatter.
Theory (model): 0.25: 14.9 · 0.5: 14.8 · 1: 13.8 · 1.5: 8.98 · 2: 2.52 · 2.5: 0.399 · 3: 0.0553
Result from a test run: 0.25: 14.7 ± 1.7 · 0.5: 13 ± 1.04 · 1: 14.3 ± 1.48 · 1.5: 11 ± 1.73 · 2: 2.33 ± 0.577 · 2.5: 0 ± 0 · 3: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: How long does each phase last?

Short phases are hard to time this way: with only about 5 cells in metaphase, chance makes a big difference. Count more fields for a better estimate.
Theory (true): 0.4
Result from a test run: mean 0.397, SD 0.184, SE 0.013 from 200 runs
