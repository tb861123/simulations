Teacher notes

The results below come from one test run with the default seed, so a class should get the same numbers. A different seed gives slightly different values within the scatter.

1 · How often do foxes and rabbits both survive?

How often do foxes and rabbits both survive? 100 runs of the classic cycles for 15 years. The equations predict both survive every time. In the model, a deep rabbit crash sometimes starves every fox, purely by chance.

2 · Is the cycle period what Lotka–Volterra predicts?

Is the cycle period what Lotka–Volterra predicts? Realistic rules. For each run, a (rabbit growth) and d (fox death) are worked out from the births and deaths it actually had, and the Lotka–Volterra period 2π/√(ad) is compared with the measured repeat time. Counted this way the two agree to within about 10–15% on average, though single runs scatter widely. Grass is the main reason realistic cycles are slow: it holds the rabbits' net growth rate a down, and the counted a includes that. (Fitting the equations to the shape of the cycles instead gives a period about 25–30% too short.) A second hump at about twice the period comes from runs where big and small cycles alternate, so the pattern only repeats every two cycles. Runs with no clear repeating cycle are left out.

3 · With Lotka–Volterra-like rules, is the period 2π/√(ad)?

With Lotka–Volterra-like rules, is the period 2π/√(ad)? 50 runs with the equations' own assumptions (unlimited grass, fixed birth and death rates, well-mixed hunting, no maturity delay) in a bigger field. Here a = 0.05 × 3 ÷ 2 per week and d = 0.04 per week straight from the rules, so the period should be 2π/√(ad) ≈ 2.2 years. The measured period comes out about 5–10% longer. The small excess is because chance pushes the cycles to large swings, and big Lotka–Volterra cycles are slower than the small-swing formula; it shrinks in bigger fields.

4 · Why are real cycles slower than Lotka–Volterra?

Why are real cycles slower than Lotka–Volterra? Lotka–Volterra-like rules, but now young foxes must wait before they can breed. The equations assume births respond to food at once, so their period (dashed line, from counted births and deaths) stays at about 2.2 years. The measured period rises with the delay, from about 2.4 years with no delay to about 3 years at 6 months and about 4 years at 9 months (with a lot of scatter): a delay in the predator's response stretches every cycle. Realistic foxes also live off fat reserves for weeks, which adds more delay.

7 · Do refuges help foxes and rabbits live together?

Do refuges help foxes and rabbits live together? Sweep the share of patches with warrens, with skilled foxes (60% hunting success). With no refuges the foxes eat the rabbits down so far that the foxes then starve. Warrens keep a reserve of rabbits safe, so crashes are shallower and the foxes more often survive: refuges stabilise the cycle. The effect needs plenty of warrens, and chance still scatters the results (20 runs per step gives about ±10%). (There is no simple formula for this, so no theory line.)
