How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Light: the lamp is a point source, so light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance² (inverse square law). Relative intensity is 100 at 10 cm (about 20 000 lux for a bright LED: approximate). Room lights add a background of about 1.5 (≈ 300 lux).
  • Rate of photosynthesis: two limits combine smoothly: a light-limited rate (proportional to light, hardly affected by temperature, because the light-dependent reactions are photochemical) and a carbon-dioxide-and-enzyme-limited rate (rises with CO₂ and levels off; rubisco and the other Calvin cycle enzymes speed up with temperature, Q10 ≈ 2, and denature above about 38 °C). Whichever is smaller is the limiting factor.
  • Bubbles: oxygen made minus oxygen used in respiration (net). Bubbles come out at random (a Poisson process), so a 1-minute count scatters by about √(count). Each new piece of pondweed differs by about ±10%. A filament bulb without a heat shield warms the water when close (up to about 8 °C over ten minutes). Bubbles also differ in size, and some oxygen dissolves: in real practicals a gas syringe or capillary tube measures volume more accurately. Plants are assumed to have adjusted to new conditions (really, wait about 5 minutes).
  • Leaf and indicator: net CO₂ uptake = gross photosynthesis − respiration, through the stomata. Petroleum jelly blocks stomata: most are on the lower surface (here 90%). Gross photosynthesis falls as the tube's CO₂ is used up and stops at about 45 ppm (the CO₂ compensation point). The indicator's colour follows the CO₂: purple below about 200 ppm, red near air (about 420 ppm), orange near 700 ppm, yellow above about 1500 ppm (a simplified scale; the real indicator follows pH). The indicator's buffer and the air space are lumped into one number.
  • Respirometer: oxygen use per gram rises with temperature (Q10 = 2) until the organisms are damaged (about 40–45 °C). With soda lime the drop moves by the volume of O₂ used; with water it moves by O₂ used − CO₂ made, so RQ = (soda-lime distance − water distance) ÷ soda-lime distance. The water bath wanders by about ±0.03 °C a minute, which makes the air in each tube expand and contract; the control tube cancels most of this (the tubes never match exactly, so about a fifth is left). The scale reads to ±0.5 mm. Volume = πr² × distance.
  • Calvin cycle (A level): three pools. Rubisco fixes CO₂ onto RuBP (rate ∝ RuBP × a CO₂ factor) to make 2 GP; GP is reduced to TP using ATP and reduced NADP from the light-dependent stage (rate ∝ GP × light supply); some TP regenerates RuBP (also using ATP) and some leaves to make glucose and other products. Turning off the light stops GP → TP and TP → RuBP, so GP builds up and RuBP falls; cutting CO₂ does the reverse. Pool sizes are relative.
  • ATP yield (A level): per glucose, glycolysis 2 ATP (net) + 2 reduced NAD; link reaction 2 reduced NAD; Krebs cycle 2 ATP, 6 reduced NAD, 2 reduced FAD; oxidative phosphorylation about 2.5 ATP per reduced NAD and 1.5 per reduced FAD, so about 32 in total (older books give 38, the theoretical maximum; the real yield is lower because membranes leak protons). Anaerobic respiration gives 2.
  • Chromatography (A level, PAG6): Rf values are typical for thin-layer chromatography with a non-polar solvent (approximate: they change with the solvent, the plate and the temperature). Each run's solvent front and spot positions vary by about ±1 mm and spots spread.
  • Quality only changes the picture (bubbles, molecules, chloroplasts, particles); the model and the batch runs are the same at every level.
  • Left out: photorespiration and C4/CAM plants, the effect of light colour (wavelength), water stress and stomata closing in the heat, the exact stoichiometry of the Calvin cycle (6 CO₂ per glucose), cyclic photophosphorylation in the pool model, and the cost of transporting pyruvate and NADH into the mitochondrion.
