Membranes and Movement · Teacher notesmembranes.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Diffusion

What to look for: Each particle moves at random, but there are more on the left to cross from there, so more cross left to right than right to left: net movement down the concentration gradient. When the concentrations are equal, particles still cross both ways, but equally: no net movement (dynamic equilibrium).

Investigation 2: What changes the rate?

What to look for: Faster when warmer (particles move faster), with more surface area, and with a steeper concentration gradient; slower with a thicker membrane (longer distance). Fick's law: rate ∝ area × difference in concentration ÷ thickness. Lungs, gut and leaves are adapted to maximise this.

Investigation 3: Osmosis

What to look for: Water moves from the dilute side (more free water molecules) to the concentrated side, so the right side swells. Sugar molecules are too big to pass through. That is osmosis: the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane.

Investigation 4: Active transport

What to look for: Active transport moves particles against the concentration gradient, using energy (ATP) from respiration. With no respiration the carriers stop, and only diffusion would happen. Root hair cells take up mineral ions this way, and the gut absorbs glucose.

Investigation 5: Surface area to volume

What to look for: The acid moves in by diffusion from every face. The small cube has a large surface area for its volume (12 : 1 for 0.5 cm, 2 : 1 for 3 cm) and a short distance to the centre, so it changes colour first. Time to reach the centre grows as the square of the distance. That is why large organisms need exchange surfaces and transport systems.

Investigation 6: Potato and sucrose

What to look for: In water and weak solutions, water enters the potato cells by osmosis: they gain mass and become turgid. In strong solutions water leaves: they lose mass and become flaccid. The line crosses zero at about 0.3 mol/dm³: there the solution has the same concentration (water potential) as the cell sap.

Investigation 7: Facilitated diffusion A level

What to look for: Facilitated diffusion is passive (down the gradient, no ATP), but limited by the number of carrier proteins. When they are all busy, the rate levels off (saturation), unlike simple diffusion, which keeps rising in proportion to the difference.

Investigation 8: Water potential A level

What to look for: Water moves from higher (less negative) to lower (more negative) water potential: out of the cell into the solution. The cell becomes flaccid and eventually plasmolysed (the membrane pulls away from the cell wall). Pure water has ψ = 0, the highest possible.

Batch experiment 1: Agar cubes: size and time

Double the size, four times as long. Diffusion is fast over short distances and very slow over long ones.

Batch experiment 3: Temperature and diffusion

Warmer particles move faster, so more reach the membrane each second. Particle speed goes as the square root of the absolute temperature, so from 0 °C to 60 °C diffusion is only about 10–20% faster here: the trend is small compared with the chance scatter, so look at the means. (In living cells, high temperatures also damage the membrane.)
Result from a test run: 0: 52.1 ± 1.89 · 10: 51.6 ± 2.63 · 20: 59.6 ± 2.32 · 30: 59.9 ± 2.97 · 40: 54.8 ± 2.43 · 50: 60.5 ± 2.49 · 60: 59.9 ± 1.57 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Membrane thickness

Thicker membranes slow diffusion (Fick's law: rate ∝ 1 ÷ thickness). The fall is gentler than that here because, as fewer cross, the gradient stays steep for longer.
Result from a test run: 0.5: 58.2 ± 1.99 · 1: 56.3 ± 3.24 · 1.5: 50 ± 1.75 · 2: 50.3 ± 1.52 · 3: 36.8 ± 1.92 · 4: 38.7 ± 1.78 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Active transport and respiration

No respiration, no transport against the gradient. As respiration rises the carriers work faster, until each carrier is working flat out.
Result from a test run: 0: 0 ± 0 · 0.1: 9.25 ± 1.25 · 0.25: 15.5 ± 1.94 · 0.5: 22.5 ± 1.26 · 0.75: 26 ± 1.08 · 1: 23.5 ± 1.66 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Facilitated diffusion saturates

The rate levels off once the carriers are all busy: the number of carrier proteins limits facilitated diffusion.
Result from a test run: 10: -0.667 ± 1.33 · 40: 6.33 ± 1.86 · 80: 11.7 ± 2.03 · 120: 13.7 ± 1.45 · 180: 26 ± 2.89 · 240: 23.7 ± 3.93 · 300: 29.7 ± 2.19 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)
