Natural Selection · Teacher notesAnswers from a test run (fixed seed)

Teacher notes

The results below come from one test run with the default seed, so a class should get the same numbers. A different seed gives slightly different values within the scatter.

1 · Does the dark moth spread as the selection equation predicts?

Does the dark moth spread as the selection equation predicts? 100 runs of industrial melanism. The mean of the runs follows the dashed theory line closely; individual runs scatter around it because of chance (drift). The mean sits a little below theory because in a few runs the first dark moths are eaten by bad luck and the allele is lost (it starts at only 1%).

How many generations until 90% of moth alleles are dark? Real records: about 50 years (1848–1895) near Manchester. The spread of results comes from chance; the theory value is the infinite-population answer.

4 · Can small populations lose a helpful allele by chance?

Can small populations lose a helpful allele by chance? A helpful allele (s ≈ 0.08, no dominance) starts at 5%. In small populations it is often lost by drift before selection can save it; in big populations it almost always spreads. The dashed line is Kimura's formula.

5 · Is a single new mutation usually lost?

Is a single new mutation usually lost? One copy of a helpful mutation (s ≈ 0.17) in 200 animals. Even helpful mutations are usually lost in the first few generations, by bad luck, because a single carrier may have no young. Theory: 1 − (1 − e^(−2s))/(1 − e^(−4Ns)).
