Natural Selection · Teacher notesnatural-selection.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Be the bird

What to look for: You did not try to 'improve' the moths. You just ate the ones you could see. The survivors were darker than average, their offspring inherited that, and the whole population shifted. That is natural selection.

Investigation 2: The Industrial Revolution

What to look for: The moths track the bark with a delay of a few generations: the population can only shift as fast as selection and mutation allow. This is the real story of the peppered moth, Biston betularia.

Investigation 3: No variation, no evolution

What to look for: Selection can only choose between differences that already exist. With no variation there is nothing to select, the population cannot adapt, and heavy predation drives it extinct.

Investigation 4: Variation without mutation

What to look for: It adapts quickly at first using the existing variation, then stalls: it cannot go beyond the darkest moth that was present at the start. Mutation is the only source of new variants.

Investigation 5: Can evolution keep up?

What to look for: If the environment moves faster than the population can follow, mismatch grows each generation, predation rises and numbers crash. More variation (bigger mutations), a larger population or faster breeding all help. This matters for species facing rapid climate change.

Investigation 6: Weak selection

What to look for: Weak selection still works, only slowly. With no selection at all the mean still wanders at random: genetic drift, which is strongest in small populations.

Batch experiment 1: Do moths match their bark?

Which moths get eaten and which mutations arise are chance events, so each population takes a slightly different path. Selection pulls them all the same way.
Theory (perfect match): 0.1: 0.1 · 0.25: 0.25 · 0.4: 0.4 · 0.55: 0.55 · 0.7: 0.7 · 0.85: 0.85
Result from a test run: 0.1: 0.103 ± 0.00559 · 0.25: 0.254 ± 0.00924 · 0.4: 0.408 ± 0.0117 · 0.55: 0.551 ± 0.00655 · 0.7: 0.695 ± 0.00962 · 0.85: 0.838 ± 0.00711 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Selection strength and speed

Runs where the population died out are left out. Adapting depends on when lucky mutations turn up, so the times vary a lot, most of all when selection is weak.
Result from a test run: 0.3: 50.5 ± 3.41 · 0.6: 31 ± 1.45 · 0.9: 20.4 ± 0.909 · 1.2: 17.2 ± 0.892 · 1.5: 15.3 ± 0.833 · 1.8: 17.3 ± 0.333 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Extinction and the pace of change

Each run is all-or-nothing, so plenty of repeats are needed to estimate a probability. Near the tipping point survival is down to luck.
Result from a test run: 0.01: 1 ± 0 · 0.014: 1 ± 0 · 0.018: 0.933 ± 0.0463 · 0.022: 0.233 ± 0.0785 · 0.026: 0.1 ± 0.0557 · 0.03: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 30 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Is mutation needed?

The final colour is that of the darkest few moths that happened to be in the starting population, which is a matter of chance. Extinct populations are left out.
Result from a test run: 0.02: 0.486 ± 0.00325 · 0.06: 0.391 ± 0.0129 · 0.1: 0.289 ± 0.0191 · 0.14: 0.174 ± 0.0145 · 0.18: 0.0958 ± 0.0148 · 0.22: 0.0407 ± 0.0142 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)
