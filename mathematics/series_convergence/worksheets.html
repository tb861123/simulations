Series and Convergence · Teacher notesseries-convergence.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: The binomial expansion

What to look for: Only for −1 < x < 1 (shaded). There the terms shrink and the partial sums settle; outside, the terms grow and adding more makes it worse. For rational n, the expansion is valid for |x| < 1.

Investigation 2: √(1 + x)

What to look for: 1 + ½x − ⅛x² = 1 + 0.05 − 0.00125 = 1.04875; √1.1 = 1.048809: correct to 4 decimal places. Small x makes the series converge fast.

Investigation 3: A whole-number power

What to look for: (1 + x)³ = 1 + 3x + 3x² + x³ exactly: every later coefficient is 0, so the series stops and is valid for every x.

Investigation 4: Standard results

What to look for: Σr³ = ¼N²(N + 1)² = (½N(N + 1))² = (Σr)², a lovely coincidence. Σr² to 20 = ⅙ × 20 × 21 × 41 = 2870.

Investigation 5: Converges or diverges?

What to look for: Σ1/r² converges (to π²/6 ≈ 1.645). Σ1/r diverges: it grows like ln N, slowly but without limit. Terms tending to 0 is necessary for convergence but not enough.

Investigation 6: eˣ, term by term A level

What to look for: x = 1: 7 terms (the error is about 1/7! ≈ 0.0002). x = 4: about 16 terms. eˣ's series converges for every x, but larger x needs more terms.

Investigation 7: sin x and small angles A level

What to look for: sin x ≈ x for small x (one term), better x − x³/6 (error about x⁵/120, less than 0.001 for |x| < 0.65). The series alternates, so the error is less than the first term left out.

Investigation 8: ln(1 + x) at the edge A level

What to look for: At x = 1 the series 1 − ½ + ⅓ − … converges, but very slowly (the error is about 1/(2n)). At x = 1.2 it diverges: the terms 1.2ᵏ/k grow. The interval of validity is −1 < x ≤ 1.

Batch experiment 1: How many terms for eˣ at x = 1?

The error is roughly the first term left out, 1/n!, which shrinks faster than any power: eˣ's series converges very quickly near 0.
Theory (first missing terms 1/n! + …): 2: -0.15 · 3: -0.664 · 4: -1.29 · 5: -2 · 6: -2.79 · 7: -3.65 · 8: -4.56 · 10: -6.52 · 12: -8.65
Result from a test run: 2: -0.144 ± 0 · 3: -0.661 ± 0 · 4: -1.29 ± 0 · 5: -2 ± 0 · 6: -2.79 ± 0 · 7: -3.65 ± 0 · 8: -4.56 ± 0 · 10: -6.52 ± 0 · 12: -8.65 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Σ1/r² closing in on π²/6

The remaining tail is about 1/N (like the area under 1/x² beyond N): slow, and 1000 terms only give 3 decimal places.
Theory (≈ 1/N − 1/(2N²)): 5: 0.18 · 10: 0.095 · 20: 0.0488 · 40: 0.0247 · 80: 0.0124 · 160: 0.00623
Result from a test run: 5: 0.181 ± 0 · 10: 0.0952 ± 0 · 20: 0.0488 ± 0 · 40: 0.0247 ± 0 · 80: 0.0124 ± 0 · 160: 0.00623 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Σr³ = (Σr)²

Exactly 1 every time: ¼N²(N + 1)² = (½N(N + 1))². Proving it for all N needs algebra (or induction), not just checking.
Theory (exactly 1): 1: 1 · 2: 1 · 3: 1 · 5: 1 · 10: 1 · 20: 1 · 50: 1 · 100: 1
Result from a test run: 1: 1 ± 0 · 2: 1 ± 0 · 3: 1 ± 0 · 5: 1 ± 0 · 10: 1 ± 0 · 20: 1 ± 0 · 50: 1 ± 0 · 100: 1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Alternating harmonic series

The error is about half the next term. This is ln(1 + x)'s series at the edge of its interval, x = 1: it converges, but painfully slowly.
Theory (≈ 1/(2N)): 10: 0.05 · 20: 0.025 · 40: 0.0125 · 80: 0.00625 · 160: 0.00313 · 320: 0.00156
Result from a test run: 10: 0.0475 ± 0 · 20: 0.0244 ± 0 · 40: 0.0123 ± 0 · 80: 0.00621 ± 0 · 160: 0.00312 ± 0 · 320: 0.00156 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Random points: is the binomial expansion any good?

About 0.31: the error is |x|¹⁰/|1 + x|, under 0.01 only for roughly −0.62 < x < 0.63, a quarter-and-a-bit of the range. Outside |x| < 1 the expansion is useless however many terms you take.
Theory (≈ 1.25/4 (−0.62 < x < 0.63)): 0.313
Result from a test run: mean 0.313, SD 0.0342, SE 0.00197 from 300 runs
