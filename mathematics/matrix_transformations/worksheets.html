Matrix Transformations · Teacher notesmatrix-transformations.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Columns are images of i and j

What to look for: The first column is where i = (1, 0) goes; the second is where j = (0, 1) goes. To write down any linear transformation's matrix, find the images of i and j.

Investigation 2: Rotation by 90°

What to look for: i → j = (0, 1) and j → −i = (−1, 0). det = 1: the area is unchanged and the F is not flipped (rotations are direct isometries).

Investigation 3: Reflection flips

What to look for: M = (0 1; 1 0), det = −1: the area is the same but the F reads backwards (orientation reversed). Every reflection has determinant −1.

Investigation 4: Determinant = area scale factor

What to look for: A stretch by k multiplies area by k = det. A shear (1 k; 0 1) has det 1: the square becomes a parallelogram with the same base and height.

Investigation 5: Squashed flat: singular matrices

What to look for: det = 1 − 1 = 0: every point lands on the line y = x/2, so the area becomes 0. Many points go to the same image, so there is no inverse.

Investigation 6: Order matters

What to look for: No: reflection then rotation gives (0 1; 1 0) (reflection in y = x), but rotation then reflection gives (0 −1; −1 0) (reflection in y = −x). Matrix multiplication is not commutative: NM ≠ MN.

Investigation 7: Invariant lines

What to look for: The x-axis is a line of invariant points (every point fixed, λ = 1). The y-axis is an invariant line but its points move along it (doubled, λ = 2).

Investigation 8: Shear: one invariant line

What to look for: Only one: the x-axis, a line of invariant points. A rotation (other than 0° or 180°) has none at all: no real eigenvalues.

Investigation 9: 3D: volume scale factor A level

What to look for: Rotation det 1 (volume kept), reflection −1 (volume kept, flipped), stretch 2 × 1 × 0.5 = 1 (volume kept, shape changed!), enlargement 1.5³ = 3.375.

Batch experiment 1: Random matrices: how often is the shape flipped?

Swapping the two columns changes the sign of det but not the chance of the matrix, so exactly half flip the plane.
Theory (½ by symmetry): 0.5
Result from a test run: mean 0.497, SD 0.0341, SE 0.00197 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 2: Enlargement and area

Exactly k² for every triangle: the determinant scales EVERY area by the same factor, whatever the shape or position. A negative k is a rotation by 180° as well.
Theory (det = k²): -2: 4 · -1.5: 2.25 · -1: 1 · -0.5: 0.25 · 0.5: 0.25 · 1: 1 · 1.5: 2.25 · 2: 4 · 2.5: 6.25
Result from a test run: -2: 4 ± 0 · -1.5: 2.25 ± 7.90e-16 · -1: 1 ± 0 · -0.5: 0.25 ± 0 · 0.5: 0.25 ± 0 · 1: 1 ± 0 · 1.5: 2.25 ± 2.56e-16 · 2: 4 ± 0 · 2.5: 6.25 ± 1.99e-15 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: det(AB) = det A × det B

Zero apart from computer rounding (about 10⁻¹⁶): area scale factors multiply when transformations are combined.
Theory (0 exactly): 0
Result from a test run: mean -4.31e-18, SD 3.99e-17, SE 2.30e-18 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 4: Random matrices: are there invariant lines?

About 68% (a computer estimate from millions of matrices gives 0.680): most random matrices have two invariant lines through the origin; the rest turn every line (complex eigenvalues, like a rotation).
Result from a test run: mean 0.678, SD 0.0346, SE 0.002 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 5: Rotating n times by 360°/n

Rⁿ = I exactly, but each step rounds a little; after 360 steps the error is still only about 10⁻¹³.
Theory (0 (Rⁿ = I)): 3: 0 · 4: 0 · 6: 0 · 12: 0 · 24: 0 · 60: 0 · 360: 0
Result from a test run: 3: 6.50e-16 ± 0 · 4: 2.45e-16 ± 0 · 6: 8.88e-16 ± 0 · 12: 1.11e-15 ± 0 · 24: 9.93e-16 ± 0 · 60: 2.71e-15 ± 0 · 360: 1.07e-14 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)
