Numerical Methods · Teacher notesnumerical-methods.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Is there a root?

What to look for: f(1) = −1 < 0 and f(2) = 5 > 0, and f is continuous, so a root lies in [1, 2]. Each step halves the interval: after 10 steps it is less than 0.001 wide. The root is 1.32472 (5 d.p.).

Investigation 2: A staircase

What to look for: It converges to 1.3247. Near the root the curve y = g(x) is much flatter than y = x (gradient about 0.19), so each step is about a fifth of the one before.

Investigation 3: The wrong rearrangement

What to look for: It diverges, even from 1.33: near the root the curve y = x³ − 1 is steeper than y = x (gradient about 5.3), so every step moves further away. The same equation can give a good or a useless iteration.

Investigation 4: A cobweb

What to look for: y = cos x slopes downwards near the root (gradient −sin 0.739 = −0.67), so the iterates jump from one side of the root to the other and spiral in. Downward slope → cobweb; upward slope → staircase.

Investigation 5: Two roots, two iterations

What to look for: x = eˣ/3 converges to 0.6191 (gentle slope there) but runs away from 1.5121 (steep slope). x = ln(3x) does the opposite: it finds 1.5121. Each rearrangement can only find the roots where it is flatter than y = x.

Investigation 6: Newton–Raphson

What to look for: 1 → 1.5 → 1.41667 → 1.4142157 → 1.41421356237: about 1, 2, 5, then 11 correct digits. The number of correct digits roughly doubles every step (quadratic convergence).

Investigation 7: When Newton–Raphson fails

What to look for: From 0 it cycles 0, 1, 0, 1, … forever and never finds the root. From −1.5 it converges quickly to −1.7693. Also try a start near ±0.816, where f'(x) = 0: the tangent is almost flat and the next value shoots far away.

Investigation 8: Change of sign without a root

What to look for: No: the interval closes in on x = 2, where there is an asymptote, not a root. The change of sign rule needs f to be continuous on the interval.

Investigation 9: A root without a change of sign

What to look for: f(0) = 2 and f(2) = 4: no sign change, yet x = 1 is a root (the curve touches the axis). A repeated root, or two roots close together, can be missed by a change-of-sign search.

Investigation 10: How fast? Errors step by step A level

What to look for: Iteration and bisection: straight lines (the error is multiplied by a constant each step: about 0.67 for x = cos x and exactly 0.5 for bisection). Newton–Raphson: a curve that plunges, because the error is roughly squared each step.

Batch experiment 1: Bisection: how small is the error?

The error never exceeds the bound, but it is not always close to it: sometimes the midpoint lands near the root by luck.

Batch experiment 3: Newton–Raphson: correct digits

Slow at first (far from the root), then the digits roughly double each step until the computer runs out of precision (about 16 digits).
Theory (eₙ₊₁ = eₙ²/(2xₙ)): 1: 0.378 · 2: 1.32 · 3: 3.11 · 4: 6.66 · 5: 13.8
Result from a test run: 1: 0.378 ± 0 · 2: 1.32 ± 0 · 3: 3.11 ± 0 · 4: 6.66 ± 0 · 5: 13.8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Random starting points for Newton–Raphson

Two piles: starts left of ln 3 ≈ 1.0986 (where f'(x) = 0) slide to 0.619; starts to the right go to 1.512. Starts very near 1.0986 get flung far away first: Newton–Raphson's 'basins' can be surprising.
Result from a test run: mean 1.02, SD 0.445, SE 0.0257 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 5: Steps to reach 6 decimal places

With |g'(α)| ≈ 0.19 each step gains about 0.7 decimal places, so roughly 8 steps whatever the start.
Theory (≈ ln(tolerance ÷ typical start error) ÷ ln|g'(α)|): 8.67
Result from a test run: mean 8.82, SD 0.548, SE 0.0316 from 300 runs
