Teacher notes

Investigation 1: A block pulled over a pulley

What to look for: B: 9.8 − T = 1a. A: T = 2a. Adding: 9.8 = 3a, so a = 3.27 m s⁻², T = 6.53 N. The tension is LESS than B's weight (9.8 N): if it were equal, B would not accelerate.

Investigation 2: Heavier hanging mass

What to look for: a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂) tends to g as m₂ grows: B almost falls freely, and the tension approaches m₁g.

Investigation 3: Atwood machine

What to look for: The 3 kg side goes down: a = (3 − 2)g/5 = 1.96 m s⁻², T = 2 × 3 × 2 × 9.8/5 = 23.5 N, between the two weights (19.6 N and 29.4 N).

Investigation 4: A rough table A level

What to look for: Maximum friction μR = 0.4 × 2g = 7.84 N < 9.8 N, so it moves with a = (9.8 − 7.84)/3 = 0.65 m s⁻². It stays still if m₂ ≤ μm₁ = 0.8 kg: then friction is less than its maximum and equals m₂g.

Investigation 5: Will it slide down the slope? A level

What to look for: It slides when tan θ > μ: θ > tan⁻¹ 0.5 = 26.6°, whatever the mass (m cancels). Below that, friction balances mg sin θ exactly and is not at its maximum.

Investigation 6: Pushing up a slope A level

What to look for: Down the slope mg sin 30° = 24.5 N, μR = 0.3 × 5g cos 30° = 12.7 N. It stays still for 11.8 N ≤ P ≤ 37.2 N: friction can act either way. Above 37.2 N it moves up.

Investigation 7: Moments: a plank about to tip A level

What to look for: It tips about D when the reaction at C becomes zero. Moments about D: 10g × 1 = 20g × (x − 3) → x = 3.5 m. Beyond 3.5 m it tips.

Investigation 8: Reactions on a bridge A level

What to look for: Their total is always the total weight (vertical forces balance). Each reaction changes linearly with the load's position (taking moments about the other support).

Batch experiment 1: Measuring the acceleration with a stopwatch

Large masses give short times, so reaction-time errors matter more: the scatter grows with m₂. A light gate would do much better.
Theory (m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)): 0.5: 1.96 · 1: 3.27 · 1.5: 4.2 · 2: 4.9 · 3: 5.88 · 4: 6.53 · 6: 7.35
Result from a test run: 0.5: 2.01 ± 0.18 · 1: 3.69 ± 0.251 · 1.5: 4.22 ± 0.33 · 2: 4.47 ± 0.314 · 3: 5.94 ± 0.951 · 4: 6 ± 0.489 · 6: 7.51 ± 0.83 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Tension in an Atwood machine

Equal masses: T = mg and nothing accelerates. As m₂ → ∞, T → 2m₁g = 39.2 N: the light side is pulled up at nearly g.
Theory (2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)): 0.5: 7.84 · 1: 13.1 · 2: 19.6 · 3: 23.5 · 4: 26.1 · 6: 29.4 · 8: 31.4 · 12: 33.6
Result from a test run: 0.5: 7.84 ± 0 · 1: 13.1 ± 0 · 2: 19.6 ± 0 · 3: 23.5 ± 0 · 4: 26.1 ± 0 · 6: 29.4 ± 0 · 8: 31.4 ± 0 · 12: 33.6 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Acceleration down a rough slope (A level)

Nothing happens until tan θ = μ (26.6°); at 90° the slope is a wall and the block falls freely (R = 0, so no friction).
Theory (g(sin θ − μ cos θ)): 10: 0 · 20: 0 · 25: 0 · 30: 0.656 · 40: 2.55 · 50: 4.36 · 60: 6.04 · 75: 8.2 · 90: 9.8
Result from a test run: 10: 0 ± 0 · 20: 0 ± 0 · 25: 0 ± 0 · 30: 0.656 ± 0 · 40: 2.55 ± 0 · 50: 4.36 ± 0 · 60: 6.04 ± 0 · 75: 8.2 ± 0 · 90: 9.8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Measuring μ by tilting (A level)

SD about 0.033: a 1.5° error in the angle gives a 6–7% error in μ. Repeating and averaging reduces it like 1/√n.
Theory (true μ): 0.5
Result from a test run: mean 0.5, SD 0.0337, SE 0.00168 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 5: Reaction at a support (A level)

A straight line: moments are proportional to distance. The other reaction is the total weight minus this one.
