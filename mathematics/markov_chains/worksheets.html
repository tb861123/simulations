Markov Chains · Teacher notesmarkov-chains.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Sunny or rainy?

What to look for: π₁ = (0.7, 0.3); π₂ = (0.7 × 0.7 + 0.3 × 0.5, …) = (0.64, 0.36); π₃ = (0.628, 0.372). The tokens (dots) agree with the matrix (lines) up to random scatter.

Investigation 2: The steady state

What to look for: π = πT gives π_sunny = 0.5/(0.3 + 0.5) = 0.625. Start in any state: the chain forgets where it began.

Investigation 3: Brand switching

What to look for: Even though A keeps 80% of its customers each month, the steady state is about (0.41, 0.36, 0.23): B gains because it keeps 70% and wins 15% from A, 20% from C.

Investigation 4: Tⁿ

What to look for: Every row of Tⁿ tends to the steady state π: whatever the starting state, the probabilities after many steps are the same.

Investigation 5: Gambler's ruin A level

What to look for: For a fair game P(ruin) = 1 − k/N = 1 − 3/6 = 0.5, and the expected number of bets is k(N − k) = 9. £0 and £6 are absorbing states: tokens pile up there.

Investigation 6: A slightly unfair game A level

What to look for: P(ruin) = (r^k − r^N)/(1 − r^N) with r = 0.55/0.45: about 0.65. A small edge per bet becomes a big edge over a whole game.

Batch experiment 1: Long-run fraction of sunny days

Close to 0.625, but successive days are linked (spells of weather), so a year's fraction scatters more than 365 independent days would.
Theory (steady state q/(p + q)): 0.625
Result from a test run: mean 0.625, SD 0.0317, SE 0.00183 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 2: Probability of ruin (A level)

A straight line: in a fair game your chance of reaching the target is your share of the total money, k/N.
Theory (1 − k/N): 1: 0.9 · 2: 0.8 · 3: 0.7 · 4: 0.6 · 5: 0.5 · 6: 0.4 · 7: 0.3 · 8: 0.2 · 9: 0.1
Result from a test run: 1: 0.922 ± 0.0111 · 2: 0.763 ± 0.0295 · 3: 0.685 ± 0.0253 · 4: 0.637 ± 0.0269 · 5: 0.505 ± 0.0166 · 6: 0.422 ± 0.0193 · 7: 0.343 ± 0.0197 · 8: 0.168 ± 0.0278 · 9: 0.11 ± 0.00913 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: How long does a game last? (A level)

Longest from the middle (25 bets from £5). Individual games vary enormously, so each mean of 100 games still scatters.
Theory (k(N − k)): 1: 9 · 2: 16 · 3: 21 · 4: 24 · 5: 25 · 6: 24 · 7: 21 · 8: 16 · 9: 9
Result from a test run: 1: 7.88 ± 0.813 · 2: 17.3 ± 1.32 · 3: 21.2 ± 1.7 · 4: 22.8 ± 0.755 · 5: 26.4 ± 0.643 · 6: 24.3 ± 0.735 · 7: 22.5 ± 0.863 · 8: 15.1 ± 1.58 · 9: 9.29 ± 0.887 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Mean return time (A level)

The mean return time to a state is 1 ÷ its steady-state probability (Kac's lemma): 1/0.625 = 1.6 days.
Theory (1/π_sunny): 1.6
Result from a test run: mean 1.6, SD 0.0509, SE 0.00294 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 5: Brand A's share after n months

Falls from 1 to the steady-state share (about 0.40) within about a year. The matrix power gives the exact expected share.
Theory (row 1 of Tⁿ): 0: 1 · 1: 0.8 · 2: 0.665 · 3: 0.576 · 5: 0.479 · 8: 0.428 · 12: 0.413 · 20: 0.41
Result from a test run: 0: 1 ± 0 · 1: 0.815 ± 0.0135 · 2: 0.663 ± 0.0038 · 3: 0.565 ± 0.00923 · 5: 0.475 ± 0.00735 · 8: 0.446 ± 0.00865 · 12: 0.397 ± 0.0122 · 20: 0.41 ± 0.0107 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
