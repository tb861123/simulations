Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Do they meet?

What to look for: At lift 0 they meet at one point. Lifted, their directions are still not parallel, but they never meet: they are SKEW. Skew lines only exist in 3D; in a plane, non-parallel lines always cross.

Investigation 2: Angle between two lines

What to look for: b₁·b₂ = 2 − 2 + 0 = 0, so they are perpendicular: 90°. The angle depends only on the directions, so lifting does not change it.

Investigation 3: Shortest distance between skew lines A level

What to look for: It is along n = b₁ × b₂ = (2, −1, −5); distance = |(a₂ − a₁)·n|/|n|, which grows in proportion to the lift. The common perpendicular is at right angles to BOTH lines.

Investigation 4: Where a line meets a plane

What to look for: Substitute: (−3 + 2λ) + λ + 2(−2 + 2λ) = 4, so 7λ − 7 = 4 and λ = 11/7. The point is (1/7, 11/7, 8/7).

Investigation 5: Angle between a line and a plane

What to look for: cos φ = (b·n)/(|b||n|) = 7/(3√6) gives φ = 17.7° between b and the NORMAL; the angle with the plane itself is 90° − 17.7° = 72.3°.

Investigation 6: Distance from a point to a plane

What to look for: |Q·n − p|/|n| = |3 + 2 + 6 − 4|/√6 = 7/√6 = 2.86. The foot of the perpendicular is Q − (7/6)n.

Investigation 7: Two planes meet in a line A level

What to look for: Direction n₁ × n₂ = (5, 1, −3) (perpendicular to both normals). Angle: cos θ = n₁·n₂/(|n₁||n₂|) = 1/6, θ = 80.4°.

Batch experiment 1: Skew distance and lift (A level)

Proportional to h: only the part of the lift along the common perpendicular n̂ counts. Here n̂ = (2, −1, −5)/√30, so the distance is 5h/√30 = 0.913h.
Theory (|h k·n̂|): 0: 0 · 0.5: 0.456 · 1: 0.913 · 1.5: 1.37 · 2: 1.83 · 3: 2.74 · 4: 3.65
Result from a test run: 0: 0 ± 0 · 0.5: 0.456 ± 0 · 1: 0.913 ± 0 · 1.5: 1.37 ± 0 · 2: 1.83 ± 0 · 3: 2.74 ± 0 · 4: 3.65 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Angle between random directions

The mean is 90°, but angles near 90° are far more common than near 0° or 180° (the density is ½ sin θ): two random directions in 3D are usually close to perpendicular.
Theory (90°): 90
Result from a test run: mean 90.7, SD 5.83, SE 0.336 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 3: Random points: distance to a plane

The distance is just |z|, uniform on 0 to 3, with mean 1.5. The formula |Q·n − p|/|n| works for any plane, tilted or not.
Theory (1.5): 1.5
Result from a test run: mean 1.5, SD 0.0917, SE 0.0053 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 4: Formula against a search

Always zero or slightly positive: no point of the plane is closer than the foot of the perpendicular.
Theory (never negative): 0
Result from a test run: mean 4.46e-4, SD 0.00156, SE 1.56e-4 from 100 runs
