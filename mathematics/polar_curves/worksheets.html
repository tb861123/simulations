Polar Curves · Teacher notespolar-curves.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Tracing a cardioid

What to look for: r = a(1 + cos θ) is largest (2a) at θ = 0 and zero at θ = π, where the cardioid has its cusp at the pole.

Investigation 2: Area of a cardioid

What to look for: ½∫a²(1 + cos θ)² dθ over 0 to 2π = 3πa²/2 = 6π ≈ 18.85 for a = 2. The sectors (thin triangles) approach it as N grows.

Investigation 3: Limaçons: dimple or loop?

What to look for: b < a/2 (here b < 1): convex, egg-shaped; a/2 < b < a: a dimple; b = a: a cardioid (touches the pole). With r ≥ 0 only, for b > a parts of θ are missing.

Investigation 4: Roses

What to look for: With Edexcel's r ≥ 0, n petals for each n (the negative-r half is not drawn). (If negative r is allowed, even n gives 2n petals.) Each petal spans an angle π/n.

Investigation 5: One petal's area

What to look for: ½∫4cos²3θ dθ from −π/6 to π/6 = π/3 ≈ 1.047. Use cos²3θ = ½(1 + cos 6θ).

Investigation 6: Spiral

What to look for: Each turn adds aπ to r, so the turns are evenly spaced (Archimedean spiral). Area grows like θ³: ½∫(aθ/2)² dθ = a²θ³/24.

Investigation 7: Tangents parallel to the initial line A level

What to look for: dy/dθ = a(cos θ + cos 2θ) = 0 gives cos θ = ½ (θ = ±π/3) or cos θ = −1 (the pole). So the top and bottom of the cardioid are at θ = ±π/3, r = 3a/2.

Investigation 8: Tangents perpendicular to the initial line A level

What to look for: dx/dθ = 0 where x = r cos θ has a turning point: at θ = 0 (the far right) and at the points on either side of the dimple, and the far left. The yellow squares mark them.

Batch experiment 1: Sectors approaching the area

The error falls like 1/N² (midpoint sectors). Area is the limit of the sum of ½r²δθ: this is where ½∫r² dθ comes from.
Theory (≈ 0.411/N²): 2: 0.103 · 4: 0.0257 · 6: 0.0114 · 8: 0.00642 · 12: 0.00285 · 16: 0.00161 · 24: 7.14e-4
Result from a test run: 2: 0.105 ± 0 · 4: 0.0258 ± 0 · 6: 0.0114 ± 0 · 8: 0.00643 ± 0 · 12: 0.00286 ± 0 · 16: 0.00161 ± 0 · 24: 7.14e-4 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Random points inside a cardioid

A Monte Carlo check of the area 3π/2 for a = 1: the fraction is the area ratio (3π/2)/16 ≈ 0.295.
Theory ((3π/2) ÷ 16): 0.295
Result from a test run: mean 0.295, SD 0.0229, SE 0.00132 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 3: Area of a rose

The same for every n: each of the n petals has area π/(4n). (Allowing negative r would double it for even n.)
Theory (π/4 for every n): 1: 0.785 · 2: 0.785 · 3: 0.785 · 4: 0.785 · 5: 0.785 · 6: 0.785 · 7: 0.785
Result from a test run: 1: 0.785 ± 0 · 2: 0.785 ± 0 · 3: 0.785 ± 0 · 4: 0.785 ± 0 · 5: 0.785 ± 0 · 6: 0.785 ± 0 · 7: 0.785 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Spiral area grows like θ³

The swept area includes the overlapping earlier turns: it counts every region once per pass of the arm.
Theory (θ³/24): 0.5: 1.29 · 1: 10.3 · 1.5: 34.9 · 2: 82.7 · 2.5: 161 · 3: 279
Result from a test run: 0.5: 1.29 ± 0 · 1: 10.3 ± 0 · 1.5: 34.9 ± 0 · 2: 82.7 ± 0 · 2.5: 161 ± 0 · 3: 279 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Average distance from the pole

The average of cos θ over a full turn is 0, so the mean r is a. (The mean of r² is 3a²/2, which is why the area is 3πa²/2.)
Theory (a): 2
Result from a test run: mean 1.99, SD 0.143, SE 0.00827 from 300 runs
