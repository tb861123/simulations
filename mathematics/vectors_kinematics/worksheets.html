Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Reading a velocity–time graph

What to look for: The gradient is the acceleration, 1.5. Area = trapezium ½(2 + 8) × 4 = 20 m = s = ut + ½at² = 8 + 12.

Investigation 2: Slowing down and turning back

What to look for: v = 0 at t = 4 s (s = 16 m). After 6 s, s = 48 − 36 = 12 m, but it has travelled 16 + 4 = 20 m. Displacement and distance differ once it turns back.

Investigation 3: Variable acceleration

What to look for: v = 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3(t − 1)(t − 3) = 0 at t = 1 s and t = 3 s. a = 6t − 12 = 0 at t = 2 s, where the v–t graph has its minimum. SUVAT does NOT apply: the acceleration changes.

Investigation 4: Adding vectors

What to look for: Yes: both routes reach the same point (the parallelogram). a + b = 4i + 4j, |a + b| = √32 = 5.66, at 45° to i.

Investigation 5: Subtracting and the vector between two points

What to look for: a − b = a + (−b). From B to A: BA = a − b, so AB = b − a. The distance between the points is |b − a|.

Investigation 6: Unit vectors and direction

What to look for: |a| = 5. Unit vector â = (3i + 4j)/5 = 0.6i + 0.8j. Angle tan⁻¹(4/3) = 53.1° above i.

Investigation 7: A projectile as a vector A level

What to look for: Only the vertical component changes (by −9.8 each second); horizontal stays 14.1 m s⁻¹. Time 2u sin θ/g = 2.89 s, range u² sin 2θ/g = 40.8 m.

Investigation 8: Which angle goes furthest? A level

What to look for: From the ground, 45° gives the greatest range, and 30° and 60° tie (sin 60° = sin 120°). From a cliff, a lower angle (about 40°) wins, because the projectile has longer to fall.

Batch experiment 1: Step-by-step v. SUVAT

Using the velocity at the start of each step always lags behind: the error is ½atΔt, proportional to the step. That is why the exact formula (an integral) is better.
Theory (½ a t Δt): 0.05: 0.225 · 0.1: 0.45 · 0.2: 0.9 · 0.3: 1.35 · 0.5: 2.25 · 0.75: 3.38 · 1: 4.5
Result from a test run: 0.05: 0.225 ± 0 · 0.1: 0.45 ± 0 · 0.2: 0.9 ± 0 · 0.3: 1.35 ± 0 · 0.5: 2.25 ± 0 · 0.75: 3.38 ± 0 · 1: 4.5 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Range against angle (A level)

Symmetric about 45°: θ and 90° − θ give the same range. The simulation stops at the first step below the ground, so it overshoots by a few millimetres.
Theory (u² sin 2θ / g): 10: 14 · 20: 26.2 · 30: 35.3 · 40: 40.2 · 45: 40.8 · 50: 40.2 · 60: 35.3 · 70: 26.2 · 80: 14
Result from a test run: 10: 14 ± 0 · 20: 26.3 ± 0 · 30: 35.4 ± 0 · 40: 40.2 ± 0 · 45: 40.8 ± 0 · 50: 40.2 ± 0 · 60: 35.4 ± 0 · 70: 26.2 ± 0 · 80: 14 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Throws are never identical (A level)

The range depends on u², so a 5% spread in speed gives about a 10% spread in range, and the average is very slightly more than for the average speed.
Theory (u²(1 + 0.05²)/g): 40.9
Result from a test run: mean 40.9, SD 4.2, SE 0.21 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 4: Adding two random unit vectors

|a + b| = 2|cos(φ/2)| where φ is the angle between them; its average is 4/π ≈ 1.27, not 2 (only if they point the same way) and not 0.
Theory (4/π): 1.27
Result from a test run: mean 1.27, SD 0.0647, SE 0.00374 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 5: Distance against displacement

Zero until the particle first turns back at t = 1 s. To find a distance, split the time at each instant the velocity is zero.
Theory (split at the turning points t = 1, 3): 0.5: 0 · 1: 0 · 1.5: 1.25 · 2: 4 · 2.5: 6.75 · 3: 8 · 3.5: 8 · 4: 8
Result from a test run: 0.5: 0 ± 0 · 1: 0 ± 0 · 1.5: 1.25 ± 0 · 2: 4 ± 0 · 2.5: 6.75 ± 0 · 3: 8 ± 0 · 3.5: 8 ± 0 · 4: 8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)
