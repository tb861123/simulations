Teacher notes

Investigation 1: cosh from two exponentials

What to look for: cosh x is the average of eˣ and e⁻ˣ: for large x the eˣ half dominates; for large negative x the e⁻ˣ half. Their product is ¼, so the sum is smallest (1) when they are equal, at x = 0.

Investigation 2: sinh and tanh

What to look for: sinh and tanh are odd (rotational symmetry), cosh is even. tanh x → ±1: horizontal asymptotes y = ±1, because for large x, sinh and cosh are both almost ½eˣ.

Investigation 3: The hyperbola and the circle

What to look for: Both shaded areas are exactly t/2. For the circle, t is the angle; for the hyperbola, t is not an angle but it plays the same role. That is why they are called hyperbolic functions.

Investigation 4: Hanging chain

What to look for: No: it is a catenary y = a cosh(x/a). Near the bottom they almost agree (cosh u ≈ 1 + u²/2), but the catenary rises more steeply near the posts. A suspension bridge's cable (carrying a level deck) IS a parabola.

Investigation 5: Inverses as logarithms A level

What to look for: arsinh 2 = 1.4436 = ln(4.236). Solving sinh y = x: e^y − e^(−y) = 2x is a quadratic in e^y. cosh y ≥ 1, so arcosh is only defined for x ≥ 1 (and we take the positive root).

Batch experiment 1: cosh²x − sinh²x = 1?

Zero apart from rounding (larger for large |x|, where cosh² and sinh² are big numbers almost equal: subtracting them loses digits).
Theory (0): 0
Result from a test run: mean 1.08e-14, SD 2.09e-13, SE 1.21e-14 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 2: Hyperbolic sector area = t/2

A straight line of gradient ½: exactly like a circular sector of angle t, whose area is t/2.
Theory (t/2): 0.25: 0.125 · 0.5: 0.25 · 1: 0.5 · 1.5: 0.75 · 2: 1 · 2.5: 1.25 · 3: 1.5
Result from a test run: 0.25: 0.125 ± 0 · 0.5: 0.25 ± 0 · 1: 0.5 ± 0 · 1.5: 0.75 ± 0 · 2: 1 ± 0 · 2.5: 1.25 ± 0 · 3: 1.5 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Length of a hanging chain

Arc length ∫√(1 + sinh²(x/a)) dx = ∫cosh(x/a) dx: the identity cosh² − sinh² = 1 makes the integral easy.
Theory (2a sinh(5/a)): 3: 15.3 · 4: 12.8 · 5: 11.8 · 6: 11.2 · 8: 10.7 · 10: 10.4 · 15: 10.2
Result from a test run: 3: 15.3 ± 0 · 4: 12.8 ± 0 · 5: 11.8 ± 0 · 6: 11.2 ± 0 · 8: 10.7 ± 0 · 10: 10.4 ± 0 · 15: 10.2 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: arsinh as a logarithm (A level)

Zero up to rounding: the logarithmic form comes from solving e^y − e^(−y) = 2x as a quadratic in e^y.
