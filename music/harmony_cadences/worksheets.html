Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Four cadences

What to look for: Perfect (V–I) sounds complete. Imperfect (ending on V) sounds unfinished, like a question. Plagal (IV–I) is the gentle 'Amen'. Interrupted (V–vi) promises I but swerves to vi: a surprise.

Investigation 2: Primary chords

What to look for: I = C E G, IV = F A C, V = G B D. Together they use every note of the scale, so they can harmonise almost any melody in the key.

Investigation 3: Pop's favourite four chords

What to look for: Hundreds of pop songs use these four chords (in some order). Hearing harmony in Roman numerals lets you recognise the pattern in any key.

Investigation 4: Breaking the rules A level

What to look for: Random four-part chords often move two parts in parallel 5ths or octaves (which makes them sound like one part), cross the parts or leave the leading note hanging. The automatic voicing searches for the smoothest path that avoids them.

Investigation 5: Inversions A level

What to look for: Inversions let the bass move by step instead of leaping. Ic before V (the cadential 6–4) is a common way to approach a perfect cadence in the Classical style.

Investigation 6: Chromatic colour A level

What to look for: The Neapolitan 6th is a major chord on the flattened 2nd (D♭ F A♭ in C), usually in first inversion before V. V/V (D F♯ A C in C) contains F♯, which leads up to G, the dominant: it 'tonicises' V.

Batch experiment 1: Random voicings break rules

Each new chord adds about one rule-break when parts are chosen without care: that is why harmony is taught as voice leading, not only as chords.
Result from a test run: 2: 1.75 ± 0.239 · 3: 3.5 ± 0.276 · 4: 4.5 ± 0.583 · 5: 4.35 ± 0.472 · 6: 6.1 ± 0.714 · 8: 11.4 ± 0.568 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Careful voicing: how far do the parts move?

Upper parts mostly move by step or stay put (common notes kept); the bass leaps more.
Result from a test run: mean 28.6, SD 6.86, SE 0.56 from 150 runs

Batch experiment 3: How rough is each kind of chord?

Major and minor triads are smoothest; diminished and 7th chords are rougher: they sound tense and want to resolve.
Theory (in tune): 1: 0.697 · 2: 0.721 · 3: 0.949 · 4: 0.856 · 5: 1.45 · 6: 1.67
