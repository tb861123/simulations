Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Tempo

What to look for: 60 BPM: one beat per second. 120 BPM: half a second. Beat length = 60 ÷ BPM. A bar of 4/4 at 120 BPM lasts 2 s.

Investigation 2: Feel the metre

What to look for: 3/4: three beats each split in two (ONE-and-two-and-three-and). 6/8: two beats each split in three (ONE-and-a-TWO-and-a). Same six notes, different groupings: simple against compound.

Investigation 3: Backbeat

What to look for: Rock and pop put the snare on beats 2 and 4 (the backbeat). On 1 and 3 it sounds like a march.

Investigation 4: Syncopation

What to look for: Accents on off-beats followed by silence on the next strong beat give syncopation (a 'push'). Putting them back on the beat lowers the score and sounds square.

Investigation 5: Swing

What to look for: It is delayed and shortened: the pair becomes long–short (like a triplet crotchet–quaver). Jazz swing is often between 1.5:1 and 2.5:1, faster tempos swinging less.

Investigation 6: Irregular metre

What to look for: Three beats of unequal length: ONE-two THREE-four FIVE-six-seven. Common in Balkan music, and in pieces like 'Money' (Pink Floyd, 7/4).

Investigation 7: Polyrhythm: 3 against 2 A level

What to look for: Once per cycle: lcm(3, 2) = 6 equal parts. 3 against 2 is the hemiola feel; 4 against 3 lines up every 12 parts and sounds much more complex.

Investigation 8: Human timing A level

What to look for: Small random timing errors (10–20 ms) sound natural; above about 30 ms the pulse gets sloppy. Drum machines 'humanise' by adding small random shifts.

Batch experiment 1: Beat length and tempo

Inversely proportional: doubling the tempo halves the beat. Largo is about 50 BPM (1.2 s), presto about 180 (0.33 s).
Theory (60 ÷ BPM): 40: 1.5 · 60: 1 · 80: 0.75 · 100: 0.6 · 120: 0.5 · 140: 0.429 · 160: 0.375 · 180: 0.333 · 200: 0.3
Result from a test run: 40: 1.5 ± 0 · 60: 1 ± 0 · 80: 0.75 ± 0 · 100: 0.6 ± 0 · 120: 0.5 ± 0 · 140: 0.429 ± 0 · 160: 0.375 ± 0 · 180: 0.333 ± 0 · 200: 0.3 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Swing ratio and the off-beat

Straight (1:1) is half way (300 ms); triplet swing (2:1) is two thirds of the beat (400 ms); 3:1 is a dotted rhythm (450 ms).
Theory (600 × r ÷ (1 + r)): 1: 300 · 1.25: 333 · 1.5: 360 · 1.75: 382 · 2: 400 · 2.5: 429 · 3: 450
Result from a test run: 1: 300 ± 0 · 1.25: 333 ± 0 · 1.5: 360 ± 0 · 1.75: 382 ± 0 · 2: 400 ± 0 · 2.5: 429 ± 0 · 3: 450 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: When polyrhythms line up

With b = 3: 3 against 2 needs 6 parts, 4 against 3 needs 12, 5 against 3 needs 15. Shared factors (6 against 3) make it simple again.
Theory (lcm(a, 3)): 2: 6 · 3: 3 · 4: 12 · 5: 15 · 6: 6 · 7: 21
Result from a test run: 2: 6 ± 0 · 3: 3 ± 0 · 4: 12 ± 0 · 5: 15 ± 0 · 6: 6 ± 0 · 7: 21 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Human timing: how far off the grid?

About 12 ms on average: below the 20–30 ms where most listeners start to hear notes as 'early' or 'late'.
Theory (σ√(2/π)): 12
Result from a test run: mean 12, SD 2.17, SE 0.109 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 5: Syncopation score

Syncopation peaks with a middling number of notes: very sparse or very busy rhythms leave few strong beats empty after an off-beat note.
Result from a test run: 2: 1.73 ± 0.0609 · 4: 2.69 ± 0.0881 · 6: 3.05 ± 0.108 · 8: 3.1 ± 0.116 · 10: 2.47 ± 0.113 · 12: 1.97 ± 0.0938 · 14: 1.2 ± 0.0715 (mean ± SE, 200 runs each)
