Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Phasing

What to look for: As player 2 creeps ahead one note at a time, the two lines make a different combined pattern at each offset. After 12 notes of drift (here 50 patterns) they line up again: Reich's 'Piano Phase'.

Investigation 2: Additive process

What to look for: 1, 1 2, 1 2 3 … up to the whole cell, each played twice, then back down. The change is gradual and predictable: a process you can hear happening.

Investigation 3: A tone row A level

What to look for: Each of the twelve notes appears once, so none dominates: no tonic, atonal. Berg's row is built from thirds, so it contains major and minor triads: serial music can still hint at tonality.

Investigation 4: Inversion A level

What to look for: In the inversion every interval goes the opposite way: up a semitone becomes down a semitone. In the matrix, rows read left to right are prime forms; columns read down are inversions; reading backwards gives R and RI.

Investigation 5: Webern's symmetry A level

What to look for: Webern's Op. 21 row is a palindrome in intervals: the retrograde is a transposition of the prime, so there are only 24 different forms instead of 48. Webern loved symmetry.

Batch experiment 1: Tonal or atonal: how even are the notes?

The tonal tune uses only seven notes, unevenly (tonic and dominant most): about 2.6 bits. Serial music uses all twelve equally: the full 3.58 bits, so no note can sound like home.
Result from a test run: 1: 2.6 ± 0.0291 · 2: 3.58 ± 1.02e-16 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Phasing: how long until the parts line up?

Halve the difference, double the time. Reich's players drift by feel; small wobbles make each realignment a little different.
Theory (100 ÷ percentage): 0.5: 200 · 1: 100 · 2: 50 · 3: 34 · 5: 20
Result from a test run: 0.5: 197 ± 1.86 · 1: 100 ± 0.653 · 2: 50.3 ± 0.3 · 3: 33.9 ± 0.1 · 5: 20.7 ± 0.153 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Intervals in a random row

Usually 6–8 of the 11 possible. 'All-interval' rows (using every interval once, like the row of Berg's Lyric Suite) are very rare: composers construct them on purpose.
Result from a test run: mean 6.97, SD 1.07, SE 0.0477 from 500 runs
