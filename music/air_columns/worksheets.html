Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Longer tube, lower note

What to look for: It halves (down an octave): f = c/2L for an open tube. That is why a bassoon or tuba is long and a piccolo short.

Investigation 2: Open and closed

What to look for: The closed tube is an octave lower: f₁ = c/4L instead of c/2L. Its lowest mode fits only a QUARTER wavelength in the tube.

Investigation 3: The missing even harmonics

What to look for: A closed tube can only have a node at one end and an antinode at the other, so it supports only odd harmonics (1, 3, 5 …). This gives the hollow, 'woody' tone of a clarinet.

Investigation 4: Finger holes

What to look for: An open hole acts like the end of the tube, so the air column is shorter and the note is higher: 1/0.75 = 1.33 times the frequency, a fourth higher.

Investigation 5: Warming up

What to look for: Sound travels faster in warm air (c ≈ 331 + 0.6θ), so every note goes up: about 3.5% here, more than half a semitone. Players tune once the instrument is at playing temperature.

Investigation 6: Overblowing

What to look for: Open tube: to the 2nd harmonic, an octave up (the flute overblows at the octave). Closed tube: to the 3rd harmonic, an octave and a fifth up (the clarinet overblows at the twelfth).

Investigation 7: End correction A level

What to look for: e ≈ 0.6r for each open end. For a 2.4 m pipe of radius 6 cm the acoustic length rises by 7.2 cm (3%), lowering the note by about half a semitone. Thin tubes barely change.

Investigation 8: Resonance tube practical A level

What to look for: First resonance: L + e = λ/4. With c = 343 m/s, λ = 0.670 m, so L ≈ 0.168 − 0.011 = 0.157 m. Find the second resonance too: the difference L₂ − L₁ = λ/2, which cancels the end correction.

Batch experiment 1: Resonance tube: frequency and length

Nearly straight, but it curves below c/4L for short tubes: the end correction matters more when L is small. Plot L against 1/f instead and the intercept gives −e.
Theory (c / 4(L + 0.6r)): 2: 168 · 3: 249 · 4: 329 · 5: 407 · 6: 483 · 7: 558 · 8: 631
Result from a test run: 2: 168 ± 0.576 · 3: 250 ± 0.751 · 4: 328 ± 1.61 · 5: 405 ± 2.03 · 6: 478 ± 1.04 · 7: 553 ± 3.41 · 8: 628 ± 5.49 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Finding c and e from L against 1/f

About ±1%. Because the end correction appears only in the intercept, the gradient gives c without needing to know e.
Theory (true c at 20 °C): 343
Result from a test run: mean 343, SD 5.56, SE 0.393 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 3: Open against closed

Dots (closed: (2n − 1)c/4L′) start an octave below the open line and climb in steps of c/2L′: only odd harmonics of a lower fundamental.
Theory (open: nc/2L′): 1: 281 · 2: 561 · 3: 842 · 4: 1120 · 5: 1400 · 6: 1680
Result from a test run: 1: 142 ± 0 · 2: 425 ± 0 · 3: 708 ± 0 · 4: 991 ± 0 · 5: 1270 ± 0 · 6: 1560 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Temperature and tuning

About 3 cents per °C: a cold flute is a quarter-tone flat. String instruments go the other way (the strings slacken), so ensembles retune.
Theory (1200 log₂(c/c₂₀)): 0: -61.7 · 5: -46 · 10: -30.6 · 15: -15.2 · 20: 0 · 25: 15.1 · 30: 30 · 35: 44.8
Result from a test run: 0: -61.7 ± 0 · 5: -46 ± 0 · 10: -30.6 ± 0 · 15: -15.2 ± 0 · 20: 0 ± 0 · 25: 15.1 ± 0 · 30: 30 ± 0 · 35: 44.8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Finger holes and semitones

Each hole must be about 5.6% further up the tube than the last. At 12 semitones the tube is (almost) half as long: the octave.
