Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Ternary form

What to look for: A B A: the first section returns at the end. B is in a contrasting key (here the relative minor) with a different tune. The self-similarity map shows A matching A as a bright square.

Investigation 2: Rondo

What to look for: A B A C A: the main theme (refrain) returns three times, with contrasting episodes (B, C) in between, like a chorus.

Investigation 3: Textures

What to look for: Monophonic: one line. Homophonic: chords moving with the tune. Melody and accompaniment: a tune over a broken-chord pattern (Alberti bass). Polyphonic: two independent lines.

Investigation 4: Canon

What to look for: The second part plays the same tune two beats later, an octave lower: imitation. A canon at a distance sounds busy where the parts overlap.

Investigation 5: Sonata form A level

What to look for: Exposition: first subject in the tonic, second in the dominant (a key contrast). Development: themes broken up and moved through keys. Recapitulation: both subjects return in the tonic, resolving the contrast.

Investigation 6: Can a computer find the form? A level

What to look for: The map compares scale degrees beat by beat. Light decoration keeps the similarity high; heavier variation drops it below the threshold and the computer calls it a new section, though a listener still hears the theme.

Investigation 7: Name that form

What to look for: Listen for what comes back and how often: AB, ABA, ABACA, AABA, verse–chorus. Check with the map, then reveal.

Batch experiment 1: Variation and automatic form-finding

As more notes change, a note-by-note comparison stops recognising the theme. (The minor-key variation is still found, because it is compared in its own key.) Listeners use rhythm, harmony and contour too.
Result from a test run: 0: 90 ± 5.57 · 0.2: 96.7 ± 3.33 · 0.4: 90 ± 5.57 · 0.6: 76.7 ± 7.85 · 0.8: 73.3 ± 8.21 · 1: 70 ± 8.51 (mean ± SE, 30 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Canon: when do the parts clash?

A one-beat delay clashes most: a tune that moves by step puts neighbouring notes a second apart. Longer delays still clash a quarter to two-fifths of the time: composers write tunes that fit against themselves, which is the skill of writing a canon.
Result from a test run: 1: 76.7 ± 2.34 · 2: 23.6 ± 2.6 · 3: 40 ± 3.23 · 4: 30.4 ± 3.1 · 6: 40.5 ± 4.56 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: How thick is each texture?

Homophony is thickest (four notes at once); monophony thinnest. Texture is about how parts relate, not only how many there are.
Result from a test run: 1: 1 ± 0 · 2: 4 ± 0 · 3: 2 ± 0 · 4: 2 ± 0 · 5: 1.75 ± 0 · 6: 2 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)
