Hypothesis Testing · Teacher noteshypothesis-testing.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Is the spinner biased?

What to look for: The critical region is X ≥ 9 (P(X ≥ 9) = 0.041 ≤ 0.05 but P(X ≥ 8) = 0.102). With a fair spinner H0 is wrongly rejected about 4% of the time: the ACTUAL significance level, not quite 5%, because X can only be a whole number.

Investigation 2: A spinner that really is biased

What to look for: Only about 40% of samples land in the critical region. The other 60% are Type II errors: the bias is real, but 20 spins is not enough to show it. Try n = 80.

Investigation 3: Fewer seeds germinate?

What to look for: P(X ≤ 16 | p = 0.8) = 0.0468 < 0.05, so reject H0: there is evidence that fewer than 80% germinate. At the 1% level it would not be significant: the conclusion depends on α, which must be chosen BEFORE looking at the data.

Investigation 4: Two tails: is the coin fair?

What to look for: Either kind of bias counts as evidence, so the 5% is shared between the two tails. For B(30, 0.5) the critical region is X ≤ 9 or X ≥ 21, with actual significance level 2 × 0.0214 = 0.043.

Investigation 5: The significance level

What to look for: A smaller α shrinks the critical region, so fewer true H0s are rejected (fewer Type I errors), but more real effects are missed (more Type II errors). You cannot reduce both without a bigger sample.

Investigation 6: Bigger samples, sharper tests

What to look for: With more data the H0 distribution narrows (SD √(np(1−p)) grows more slowly than n), so the critical region starts closer to np0 in proportion and a real difference is spotted far more often. The power curve below gets steeper.

Investigation 7: Under-filled flour bags A level

What to look for: z = (496.2 − 500) ÷ (6/√10) = −2.00, P(Z ≤ −2.00) = 0.0227 < 0.05: reject H0. The critical value is 500 − 1.645 × 6/√10 = 496.88 g.

Investigation 8: Power of the normal test A level

What to look for: Power = P(x̄ < 496.88 | μ = 497) ≈ 48% with n = 10. You need about n = 25 for 80%: power grows as the two curves separate, which needs σ/√n small compared with the shift.

Investigation 9: Two-tailed z test A level

What to look for: 250 ± 1.96 × 30/4 = 235.3 and 264.7 ms. The drink might speed people up or slow them down, and the direction was not predicted in advance, so both tails are needed.

Batch experiment 1: How often is a true H0 rejected?

About 4.1%, not 5%: the critical region X ≥ 9 has probability 0.041 because X is discrete. Each run is only 200 tests, so it scatters by about ±1.4 percentage points.
Theory (actual significance level): 4.09
Result from a test run: mean 4.07, SD 1.42, SE 0.184 from 60 runs

Batch experiment 2: Power curve: how big a bias can 20 spins detect?

At p = 0.25 the curve is the significance level; beyond it the curve is the power. Below 0.25 the test almost never rejects, which is right: it is one-tailed.
Theory (P(X ≥ 9 | p)): 0.15: 0.133 · 0.25: 4.09 · 0.3: 11.3 · 0.35: 23.8 · 0.4: 40.4 · 0.5: 74.8 · 0.6: 94.3 · 0.7: 99.5
Result from a test run: 0.15: 0 ± 0 · 0.25: 4 ± 0.365 · 0.3: 12.2 ± 1.11 · 0.35: 23.8 ± 2.1 · 0.4: 36.8 ± 2.3 · 0.5: 76 ± 2.05 · 0.6: 91.5 ± 0.619 · 0.7: 98.8 ± 0.167 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Does a bigger sample help?

The theory line wiggles because the actual significance level jumps about as n changes (discrete X). Roughly 4 times the data are needed to halve the size of effect you can detect.
Theory (exact binomial power): 10: 9.49 · 20: 23.8 · 40: 30.5 · 60: 44.1 · 100: 69.7 · 150: 84.8
Result from a test run: 10: 8.2 ± 1.36 · 20: 26.6 ± 1.54 · 40: 30.4 ± 0.927 · 60: 43 ± 2.02 · 100: 74.6 ± 2.18 · 150: 85.2 ± 1.39 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: p-values when H0 is true (A level)

Flat (uniform) from 0 to 1: every p-value is equally likely, so exactly 5% fall below 0.05. That is what the significance level means. A small p-value alone is not proof.
Theory (mean of a uniform): 0.5
Result from a test run: mean 0.504, SD 0.299, SE 0.0122 from 600 runs

Batch experiment 5: Power of the z test (A level)

Power = Φ(shift ÷ (σ/√n) − 1.645). It reaches 80% at about n = 25. Each point is only 100 tests, so it scatters by up to ±5 percentage points.
Theory (Φ(−1.645 + 3√n/6)): 5: 29.9 · 10: 47.5 · 15: 61.5 · 20: 72.3 · 30: 86.3 · 40: 93.5 · 60: 98.7
Result from a test run: 5: 29.4 ± 2.62 · 10: 45.6 ± 1.99 · 15: 61.6 ± 1.72 · 20: 70.4 ± 3.17 · 30: 88.2 ± 1.46 · 40: 93.6 ± 1.29 · 60: 99.2 ± 0.374 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
