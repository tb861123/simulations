1 · Does the spread of the mean shrink like σ/√n?

Does the spread of the mean shrink like σ/√n?
Each dot is the SD of 2,000 sample means. On log–log axes σ/√n is a straight line of gradient −0.5: four times the sample size halves the spread. The points scatter a little because each run uses only 2,000 samples; the scatter is about 1/√(2 × 2000) ≈ 1.6% of the value.

2 · Does skewness fade away as n grows?

Does skewness fade away as n grows?
Bus waiting times are very skewed (γ = 2). The skewness of the mean falls like 2/√n — gradient −0.5 again — so the histogram gets more symmetrical, but slowly: even n = 30 leaves skewness 0.37. Skewness is hard to measure: with 5,000 samples the noise is about ±0.035, which is why the big-n points wobble.

3 · Is the Galton board really binomial? (χ² test)

Is the Galton board really binomial? (χ² test)
500 boards of 500 balls. For each, χ² measures how far the pile is from the binomial prediction. If the model is right, the χ² values follow the χ² distribution (yellow curve) with mean equal to the degrees of freedom, and about 5% of perfectly fair boards land above the critical value — that is what a 5% significance level means.

4 · How many rows before a tilted board looks normal?

How many rows before a tilted board looks normal?
p = 0.2 and 5,000 balls per board. The 'gap' is the % of balls in different bins from where the normal curve puts them. It falls quickly at first, wobbling because bins and the curve line up better for some numbers of rows than others, and then levels off at the random-scatter floor (about 3–4% for 5,000 balls on a big board). A fair board (p = 0.5) gets there with far fewer rows — try it. A common rule of thumb says the normal approximation is OK when Rp and R(1−p) are both above 5: here R ≥ 25.

5 · How many balls before the pile matches the curve?

How many balls before the pile matches the curve?
With few balls the pile is ragged — that is random scatter, not a failure of the theory. The gap shrinks roughly like 1/√balls (gradient −0.5) until it reaches the small true difference between the binomial and the normal curve for 12 rows (about 0.4%), where it levels off.

6 · Does averaging tame the Cauchy?

Does averaging tame the Cauchy?
For the lighthouse, averaging 256 values gives means just as spread out as single values: the IQR stays at 2 km (gradient 0). The CLT needs a finite variance, and the Cauchy has none. Re-run with the fair die selected (left panel) and the IQR falls with gradient −0.5.
