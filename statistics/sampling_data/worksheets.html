Sampling and Data · Teacher notessampling-data.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Census or sample?

What to look for: The sample mean is usually within about 1 °C of the census mean. A sample is quicker and cheaper; a census gives the exact population value but takes longer (and is impossible if testing destroys the item).

Investigation 2: Opportunity sampling

What to look for: Late July and August are the warmest part of the period, so the mean is far too high: the sample is biased because it does not represent the whole of May to October.

Investigation 3: Systematic sampling

What to look for: Yes: it covers every month evenly. The sampling frame is the numbered list of all 184 days. Systematic sampling is simple, but can be biased if there is a pattern with the same period as k.

Investigation 4: Stratified sampling

What to look for: 5 from each 30-day month, and 5 or 6 from each 31-day month: in proportion to the size of each stratum. Because every month is always represented, the mean varies less from sample to sample.

Investigation 5: Box plots and outliers

What to look for: Rainfall is strongly positively skewed: most days have little or no rain, a few have a lot. Several wet days lie above Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. They are real, not errors, so they should not be removed.

Investigation 6: Cleaning the data: 'tr' and 'n/a'

What to look for: Treating tr as 0 keeps those days as dry-ish days; leaving them out removes small values, so the mean and median rise. n/a values must always be left out. Always say how you cleaned the data.

Investigation 7: Comparing two places

What to look for: Beijing is warmer (higher mean and median) and much more variable (bigger SD and IQR): its continental climate swings from hot summer to cool autumn. Compare a measure of location AND a measure of spread, in context.

Investigation 8: Histograms with unequal classes A level

What to look for: Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width, so the AREA of each bar is proportional to the frequency. A wide class would otherwise look far too important.

Investigation 9: Standard deviation from Σx and Σx² A level

What to look for: σ² = Σx²/n − (Σx/n)² = Sxx/n. Edexcel uses the divisor n. The divisor n − 1 gives a slightly larger value (an unbiased estimate of the population variance).

Batch experiment 1: How close is a sample mean?

Centred on the population mean (random sampling is unbiased), SD about σ/√20 ≈ 0.9 °C.
Theory (population mean): 19.3
Result from a test run: mean 19.3, SD 0.58, SE 0.029 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 2: Bigger samples, less scatter

Falls like 1/√n, and faster near n = 184, when the sample is almost a census (the finite population correction).
Theory (σ/√n × √((N − n)/(N − 1))): 5: 1.24 · 10: 0.862 · 20: 0.591 · 40: 0.392 · 80: 0.235 · 120: 0.151 · 160: 0.08
Result from a test run: 5: 1.24 ± 0.0254 · 10: 0.879 ± 0.0143 · 20: 0.587 ± 0.0167 · 40: 0.402 ± 0.00661 · 80: 0.237 ± 0.00264 · 120: 0.157 ± 0.00436 · 160: 0.08 ± 0.00136 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Does a bigger opportunity sample help?

Biased (too warm) until the sample is almost the whole population: the summer holiday days are the warmest of the season. More data collected the wrong way does not remove bias.
Theory (no error): 10: 0 · 20: 0 · 40: 0 · 60: 0 · 90: 0 · 120: 0 · 150: 0 · 184: 0
Result from a test run: 10: 2.99 ± 0 · 20: 2.75 ± 0 · 40: 2.52 ± 0 · 60: 1.63 ± 0 · 90: 0.284 ± 0 · 120: -0.0972 ± 0 · 150: 0.0774 ± 0 · 184: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Stratified or simple random?

Below 1: stratifying by month removes the month-to-month (seasonal) differences from the sampling error, so the estimate is more precise.
Theory (no difference): 1
Result from a test run: mean 0.643, SD 0.049, SE 0.00775 from 40 runs

Batch experiment 5: How many outliers? (rain against temperature)

Skewed data produce several 'outliers' in almost every sample: they are part of the distribution, not mistakes. For roughly normal data (temperature) the rule flags very few.
Result from a test run: mean 1.84, SD 1.38, SE 0.0795 from 300 runs
