How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Arrivals: a Poisson process. In each tiny moment a customer turns up with the same small chance, independently of everyone else, so the gaps between arrivals are exponential with mean 1/λ. With the lunchtime pattern, λ rises smoothly to 2.5 times its base value at 12:30 and drops to 0.6 times it early and late (generated by "thinning").
  • Service time = mean service time × (trolley or basket factor) × a random "work" amount with mean 1. Exponential work has CV = 1; gamma and log-normal let you choose the CV; fixed has CV = 0. A trolley takes 4 times as long as a basket; the factors are set so the overall mean stays as chosen (mixing trolleys and baskets adds variability).
  • Choosing a queue: customers count the people ahead of them per till (waiting plus being served) and join the line with the fewest, breaking ties at random. They can't see how full the trolleys are.
  • Separate queues: once in a queue you stay, unless jockeying is on: then the last person in a queue moves if another queue is at least 2 people shorter. In a single snake, the person at the front goes to the next free till.
  • Express till: till 1 takes baskets only. Self-checkouts share one queue, take baskets only, and are faster or slower than a staffed till by the factor you set.
  • Spare tills: a member of staff opens one (instantly) when a queue is longer than the limit, and people at the back of the longest queues move over. It closes when nobody is waiting for a staffed till and it is idle.
  • Giving up (balking): a customer who sees at least the set number of people ahead per till walks out and is counted as lost. Real people also give up after joining ("reneging"); that is not modelled.
  • The day: the doors close at closing time, but everyone already inside is served. The shop opens empty, so the first part of the day is quieter than the long-run theory; batch runs can ignore customers who arrive in the first hour ("warm-up").
  • Waiting time = time from arriving to starting to be served. Unfairness = % of customers who saw someone who arrived after them get served first.
  • Theory: Erlang C (M/M/c), Pollaczek–Khinchine (M/G/1) and Allen–Cunneen (M/G/c ≈ Erlang C × (1 + CV²)/2) all assume a steady arrival rate, one shared queue, identical tills, nobody leaving and a shop that has been open for ever. "c separate M/G/1" assumes each customer picks a queue at random and never moves; choosing the shortest queue does much better than that, but not as well as a snake.
  • Not modelled: walking time, packing time, staff breaks, groups shopping together, people abandoning a queue after joining, and tills of different speeds.
