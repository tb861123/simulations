Teacher notes

The results below come from one test run with the default seed, so a class should get the same numbers. A different seed gives slightly different values within the scatter.

1 · Glass block: n = sin i / sin r

Glass block: n = sin i / sin r Each student measures i and r with a protractor (±1° each). The answers scatter around the true 1.50 with an SD of about 0.06 (4 %). A 1° error matters more for r (25.4°) than for i, because sin changes fastest at small angles relative to its size.

2 · Which angle of incidence is best?

Which angle of incidence is best? The mean stays near 1.50 at every angle, but the spread is huge at small angles (SD about 0.2 at 10°) and small at large angles (0.03–0.05 above 50°): a ±1° error is a big fraction of a small angle. So use large angles of incidence (but not so large that the refracted ray is hard to mark), or better, plot sin i against sin r and take the gradient.

3 · Critical angle method

Critical angle method Shine light into a semicircular block and find the angle where the refracted ray just disappears (θc = 41.8° for n = 1.50), to ±0.5°. Then n = 1/sin θc. The SD is about 0.015, much smaller than with one i and r reading, because only one angle is measured and it is large.

4 · Prism: n for each colour

Prism: n for each colour On a spectrometer the minimum deviation of each spectral line is measured to ±0.1°, and n = sin((A + D_min)/2)/sin(A/2). The results follow Cauchy's curve: n falls from about 1.533 (400 nm) to 1.516 (700 nm). The SD at each colour is only about 0.002, small enough to see dispersion clearly — that is why spectrometers measure to a fraction of a degree.

5 · Lens: find f from u and v

Lens: find f from u and v Students set u = 30 cm (±2 mm) and move the screen until the image looks sharpest (±5 mm, because the image looks sharp over a range). f = uv/(u + v) comes out at 15.0 cm on average with an SD of about 0.13 cm (under 1 %). Errors in u and v are partly cancelled because f depends on them both in the same way.

6 · Lens: how does v depend on u?

Lens: how does v depend on u? The measured image distances follow v = fu/(u − f): 60 cm at u = 20 cm, 30 cm at u = 2f = 30 cm (the image is the same size as the object) and 20 cm at u = 60 cm. Plot 1/v against 1/u from the CSV and you get a straight line of gradient −1 with both intercepts at 1/f.

7 · Fibre: does a smaller Δn cut pulse spreading?

Fibre: does a smaller Δn cut pulse spreading? The steepest guided ray travels L/cos θ instead of L, so a pulse spreads by Δt = (L n_core/c)(n_core/n_clad − 1): about 357 ns per km for n_clad = 1.40 but only 17 ns for 1.495. Each run sends 200 rays at random angles, so the measured spread is a little less than the theory maximum (the steepest possible ray is rarely sampled exactly). Telecom fibres use tiny index differences (and single-mode cores) to keep pulses sharp.
