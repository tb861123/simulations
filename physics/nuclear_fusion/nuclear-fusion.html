How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Two nuclei, head on. One nucleus is fixed; the other comes straight at it. The diagram shows potential energy U against separation r.
  • Coulomb barrier. Outside the touching distance R the only force is electric repulsion: U = 1.44 MeV·fm × Z₁Z₂ / r. Electrons are ignored (in a plasma they are stripped off; their slight "screening" of the charge, which helps fusion a little, is left out).
  • Touching distance R = 1.2 fm × (A₁⅓ + A₂⅓), using the A-level rule r = r₀A⅓ with r₀ = 1.2 fm.
  • The well is schematic. Inside R the strong force forms a well about 30–50 MeV deep. It is drawn much shallower than that (with a broken axis) so the barrier stays visible.
  • Energies of collisions. The chance of a collision having energy E is taken as the Boltzmann factor e−E/kT. This is exactly the weighting that sets fusion rates: faster nuclei collide more often but present a smaller quantum "target", and the two effects cancel.
  • Tunnelling. The chance of getting through the barrier is the Gamow factor P = exp(−√(EG/E)), EG = 2μc²(παZ₁Z₂)², and 1 above the barrier. This is the standard approximation; it is a little rough close to the top of the barrier.
  • Reaction rates use ⟨σv⟩ = √(8/πμ) (kT)−3/2 S ∫ e−E/kT P(E) dE with a constant "S-factor" for each reaction, taken from nuclear data at low energy. Real S-factors change with energy (D–T has a resonance at about 64 keV, so real D–T rates are about twice the model's at 10–20 keV).
  • Pressure → density. Ideal gas: n = P/kT counts every particle, nuclei and electrons, so the number of nuclei is P / ((1 + Z) kT). A 50:50 mix of the two nuclei is assumed. Real star cores also contain helium and other elements, have radiation pressure, and (in dense stars) degenerate electrons.
  • Weak-force reactions. For p + p (and p + ³He) the nuclei touch far more often than they fuse: a proton must turn into a neutron during the brief contact, which the weak force rarely manages. The tiny S-factor accounts for this in the rates; the animation shows them falling apart.
  • No product. ¹H + ⁴He makes lithium-5 and ⁴He + ⁴He makes beryllium-8. Both fall apart almost at once (in stars a third helium nucleus must arrive within 10⁻¹⁶ s to make carbon: the triple-alpha process), so no rate is given.
  • Demonstration odds (the default animation) multiply every real chance of getting through by the same fixed boost, capped at 100%. Because the boost is the same for every temperature and pressure, the ratios between settings stay true; divide what you see by the boost to get the real rate. The readouts and batch runs always use the real odds.
  • The animation is slowed down enormously: a real close approach lasts about 10⁻²¹ s.
