How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • The pendulum: two point masses on light, rigid rods, joined by frictionless hinges, swinging in a flat vertical plane. Angles θ₁ and θ₂ are measured from straight down.
  • Equations of motion (from Lagrange's method, with Δ = θ₁ − θ₂ and D = 2m₁ + m₂ − m₂cos2Δ):
    θ₁'' = [−g(2m₁+m₂)sinθ₁ − m₂g sin(θ₁−2θ₂) − 2 sinΔ · m₂(ω₂²L₂ + ω₁²L₁cosΔ)] ÷ (L₁D)
    θ₂'' = [2 sinΔ (ω₁²L₁(m₁+m₂) + g(m₁+m₂)cosθ₁ + ω₂²L₂m₂cosΔ)] ÷ (L₂D)
    Single pendulum: θ'' = −(g/L)sinθ.
  • Solving them: 4th-order Runge–Kutta with a fixed 1 ms step. Its error per step is tiny (about (step)⁵), so the energy drifts by less than a millionth of a per cent per minute. That is far smaller than the 0.001° differences we study, so the chaos is real, not a computer error.
  • Energy: KE = ½(m₁+m₂)L₁²ω₁² + ½m₂L₂²ω₂² + m₂L₁L₂ω₁ω₂cosΔ; PE = −(m₁+m₂)gL₁cosθ₁ − m₂gL₂cosθ₂. The drift is shown as a percentage of (m₁+m₂)gL₁ + m₂gL₂.
  • Separation is the distance between two pendulums in "phase space": √(Δθ₁² + Δθ₂² + τ²(Δω₁² + Δω₂²)) in radians, where τ = √(L₁/g) turns angular speeds into angles. For chaotic swings it grows like δeλt until the pendulums are completely different (about 1 radian), then stops growing.
  • Lyapunov exponent λ is found two ways: the gradient of ln(separation) against time on the graph, and a long-run average (the Benettin method: a shadow pendulum 10⁻⁸ rad away is pulled back every 0.05 s and the stretching is averaged).
  • Prediction horizon: the time until either angle differs by more than your chosen amount. Theory: about ln(X/δ) ÷ λ.
  • Small swings are not chaotic. A single pendulum has period 2π√(L/g) for small angles; bigger swings take longer (exact: 4√(L/g)·K(sin(θ₀/2)), where K is an elliptic integral). A double pendulum with small swings is a mix of two "normal modes" with fixed periods.
  • Damping is a simple drag on each angle, θ'' gets an extra −bω. Real air resistance depends on speed squared, and real hinges have friction.
  • Left out: rods with mass (real pendulums have heavy arms, which changes the numbers but not the chaos), the bobs' size, bending and stretching, air currents and the pendulums hitting each other. A real double pendulum can be predicted for only a few seconds, partly because you can never set it up more accurately than about 0.1°.
