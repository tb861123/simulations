Ripple Tank · Teacher noteswaves.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Two sources

What to look for: Calm lines (nodal lines) are where a crest from one source always meets a trough from the other: path difference = ½λ, 1½λ… Moving the sources closer spreads the lines apart.

Investigation 2: Young's double slit

What to look for: Fringe spacing ≈ λD/d. Halving d doubles the spacing; doubling λ doubles it too. Compare the measured and theory tiles.

Investigation 3: Diffraction through a gap

What to look for: Spreading is greatest when gap ≈ wavelength. A wide gap gives a beam with sharp shadows: that is why light (tiny λ) seems to travel in straight lines but sound bends round doorways.

Investigation 4: Refraction

What to look for: Wavelength shrinks by the factor n, the wave bends towards the normal, and frequency is unchanged: the two probe traces have the same period.

Investigation 5: Find the focus

What to look for: The middle of each wavefront is delayed the most, so the wavefront curves inward and converges. Larger n means more delay and a shorter focal length.

Investigation 6: Break the sound barrier

What to look for: In front, crests bunch up (higher pitch); behind, they spread. Above the wave speed the source outruns its own waves, which pile into a V-shaped shock: a sonic boom, or the bow wave of a boat.

Investigation 7: Engineer's challenge

What to look for: Try a curved (parabolic) wall behind the probe, a hand-drawn lens in front of it, or a wall with gaps spaced so that the waves through each gap arrive in step (a zone plate).

Batch experiment 1: Fringe spacing vs slit separation

Stray ripples in the tank (random each run) shift the fringes slightly, and peaks can only be located to a fraction of a millimetre. Real ripple tanks are much noisier.
Theory (path difference = λ): 36: 58 · 40: 51.9 · 46: 44.8 · 52: 39.5 · 60: 34.2 · 70: 29.3
Result from a test run: 36: 63.9 ± 0.0725 · 40: 52.8 ± 0.0242 · 46: 49.2 ± 0.0248 · 52: 41.6 ± 0.0267 · 60: 36.2 ± 0.0644 · 70: 31.1 ± 0.0539 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Fringe spacing vs wavelength

Stray ripples shift the peaks slightly. At long wavelengths the slits (8 mm wide) are no longer small compared with λ and the fringes are broad, so the peak is harder to pin down.
Theory (path difference = λ): 6: 30.5 · 7: 35.7 · 8: 41 · 9: 46.4 · 10: 51.9 · 11: 57.5 · 12: 63.2
Result from a test run: 6: 35 ± 0.00584 · 7: 39.5 ± 0.00498 · 8: 43.3 ± 0.0462 · 9: 47.6 ± 0.074 · 10: 52.9 ± 0.112 · 11: 56.7 ± 0.0332 · 12: 69.8 ± 0.0148 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Wavelength inside glass

The grid is made of 1 mm cells, so short wavelengths are slightly distorted (the simulation's own 'numerical dispersion'), and the stray ripples add noise.
Theory (λ / n): 1: 16 · 1.25: 12.8 · 1.5: 10.7 · 1.75: 9.14 · 2: 8 · 2.5: 6.4
Result from a test run: 1: 15.8 ± 0.0359 · 1.25: 12.7 ± 0.0127 · 1.5: 10.5 ± 0.00909 · 1.75: 8.92 ± 0.00681 · 2: 7.78 ± 0.0109 · 2.5: 6.12 ± 0.00568 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Doppler: wavelength ahead of a moving source

Close to the source the wavefronts are strongly curved and the amplitude changes quickly, which shifts the zero-crossings used to measure the spacing.
Theory (λ(1 − v/c)): 0: 16 · 0.15: 13.6 · 0.3: 11.2 · 0.45: 8.8 · 0.6: 6.4 · 0.75: 4
Result from a test run: 0: 16 ± 0.00689 · 0.15: 13.5 ± 0.00818 · 0.3: 11 ± 0.00678 · 0.45: 9.9 ± 0.308 · 0.6: 8.97 ± 0.321 · 0.75: 14.5 ± 1.83 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)
