Ripple Tank · Teacher noteswaves.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Two sources

What to look for: Calm lines (nodal lines) are where a crest from one source always meets a trough from the other: path difference = ½λ, 1½λ… Moving the sources closer spreads the lines apart.

Investigation 2: Young's double slit

What to look for: Fringe spacing ≈ λD/d. Halving d doubles the spacing; doubling λ doubles it too. Compare the measured and theory tiles.

Investigation 3: Diffraction through a gap

What to look for: Spreading is greatest when gap = wavelength: the wave fans out in semicircles. A wider gap spreads it less. Under the GCSE rule a gap narrower than the wavelength does not let the wave through at all: it is reflected. Switch Gaps narrower than λ to Real behaviour to see what really happens: a weak wave leaks through and spreads out in all directions. A wide gap gives a beam with sharp shadows: that is why light (tiny λ) seems to travel in straight lines but sound bends round doorways.

Investigation 4: Refraction

What to look for: Inside the glass the wavelength shrinks by the factor n and the wavefronts bend so that the wave travels closer to the normal; coming out they bend back. The frequency is unchanged: the two probe traces have the same period. The bigger n, or the more the block is turned, the bigger the change of direction.

Investigation 5: Find the focus

What to look for: The middle of each wavefront is delayed the most, so the wavefront curves inward and converges. Larger n means more delay and a shorter focal length.

Investigation 6: Break the sound barrier

What to look for: In front, crests bunch up (higher pitch); behind, they spread. Above the wave speed the source outruns its own waves, which pile into a V-shaped shock: a sonic boom, or the bow wave of a boat.

Investigation 7: Engineer's challenge

What to look for: Try a curved (parabolic) wall behind the probe, a hand-drawn lens in front of it, or a wall with gaps spaced so that the waves through each gap arrive in step (a zone plate).

Investigation 8: Path difference and phase A level

What to look for: In phase: bright (constructive) where the path difference is nλ, calm (destructive) where it is (n + ½)λ. In antiphase the sources start half a cycle apart, so the rules swap: the centre line (path difference 0) becomes calm. Interference needs coherent sources: a constant phase difference.

Investigation 9: Stationary waves A level

What to look for: The incoming and reflected waves superpose to make a stationary wave. Nodes (no movement) are λ/2 apart, with antinodes halfway between. Unlike a progressive wave, a stationary wave carries no energy along: every point between two nodes moves in phase. The tiles show the measured node spacing and λ/2.

Investigation 10: The diffraction grating A level

What to look for: The bright orders are in the same places as for two slits with the same d (path difference nλ between neighbouring slits), but they are much sharper and brighter, with faint subsidiary maxima between. That is why a grating measures wavelengths so precisely. The tile gives the theory position of the first order on the screen.

Investigation 11: Total internal reflection A level

What to look for: Waves meeting the face at more than the critical angle (sin c = 1/n = 0.5, so c = 30°) are totally internally reflected; only a cone within 30° of the normal escapes. Raise n and the cone narrows. This is how optical fibres trap light.

Batch experiment 1: Fringe spacing vs slit separation

Stray ripples in the tank (random each run) shift the fringes slightly, and peaks can only be located to a fraction of a millimetre. Real ripple tanks are much noisier.

Batch experiment 2: Fringe spacing vs wavelength

Stray ripples shift the peaks slightly. The slits are 12 mm wide, so every wavelength tested (up to 12 mm) can pass through them (the gap must be at least λ). At long wavelengths the slits are about one wavelength wide, so each spreads widely and the fringes are broad, so the peak is harder to pin down.

Batch experiment 4: Doppler: wavelength ahead of a moving source

Crests are measured only between the source and the first crest it sent out. Close to the source the wavefronts are strongly curved, which shifts the zero-crossings slightly, so the measured values sit a little below the line. Faster than about 0.6 × the wave speed the squashed wavelength (under 6 mm) is too short for the 1 mm grid to show faithfully.

The incoming and reflected waves superpose; nodes are λ/2 apart whatever the wavelength. The stray ripples and the 1 mm grid give a little scatter.
