How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Density practicals: data-book densities at about 20 °C (aluminium 2700, copper 8960, brass 8500, steel 7850, oak 700, pine 500, HDPE 950, acrylic 1180, granite 2700, modelling clay 1700 kg/m³; water 998, sea water 1025, ethanol 789, olive oil 912, glycerol 1261, golden syrup about 1430, mercury 13 534). Errors: ruler ±0.5 mm reading scatter (edges, parallax, blocks not quite square), rounded to 1 mm; vernier ±0.05 mm, rounded to 0.1 mm; balance ±0.005 g, rounded to 0.01 g; measuring cylinder read to the nearest 0.5 cm³ (±0.25 cm³), bubbles add up to 0.3 cm³; the eureka can loses 1.0 ± 0.4 cm³ (a systematic error). Real woods vary by ±20% and real stones by a few per cent.
  • Pressure on snow: soft snow gives way above about 3 kPa; depth = 45 cm × (1 − e^(−(p − 3 kPa)/25 kPa)) (illustrative). Areas and masses are typical round numbers.
  • Liquids: p = hρg (g = 9.81 N/kg), atmospheric pressure 101 kPa on top; liquids are taken as incompressible and at one temperature. Jets leave a hole at depth h at √(2gh) (no friction); the sensor reads ±0.5% ± 0.1 kPa (tank) or ±1 kPa (dam, sea).
  • Floating: a 10 cm cube; upthrust = ρ_liquid × volume under the surface × g. The bobbing is damped by an illustrative drag and the liquid it drags with it (added mass). Surface tension and the liquid level rising are ignored.
  • Gas: particles in a 3-D box (drawn from the front) bounce off the walls without colliding with each other (an ideal gas). The pressure on the right-hand wall is the momentum given to it per second. Each particle stands for about 10²⁰ real molecules; the gauge is scaled so 150 particles at 293 K read 101 kPa. Air pressure with height uses an isothermal atmosphere (scale height 8.4 km): real air gets colder with height.
  • Falling sphere: weight, upthrust and drag; drag = 6πηrv at low speeds (Stokes' law), with a standard correction at higher Reynolds numbers, × (1 + 2.1r/R) for the tube walls (R = 2.5 cm). Viscosities at 20 °C: glycerol 1.41 Pa s (it falls fast as it warms: 0.95 Pa s at 25 °C), olive oil 0.084, water 0.001, golden syrup about 50 Pa s (varies). The stopwatch adds ±0.12 s at each mark; light gates ±1 ms.
  • Quality only changes the picture (gas particles, jets, bubbles). The model and the batch runs are the same at every setting.
