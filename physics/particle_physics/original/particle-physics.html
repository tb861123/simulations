    📐 Physics notes

    Spec reference. Advanced: WJEC Eduqas A level Physics, particles and nuclear structure (the old WJEC page's Component 3, Topic 7). Standard: quarks and leptons are not in Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1; it has the nucleus, isotopes and β⁻ decay (Section 7).

    Key equations:

    Q(proton) = ⅔ + ⅔ − ⅓ = +1
    Q(neutron) = ⅔ − ⅓ − ⅓ = 0
    B = +⅓ per quark, B = −⅓ per antiquark

    What this shows: quarks come in six flavours and are always found in bound states (hadrons) because of quark confinement: a single quark has never been seen on its own. Baryons contain three quarks (B = 1); mesons contain one quark and one antiquark (B = 0). The strong force between quarks is carried by gluons.

    Advanced context. The residual strong force between hadrons (the nuclear force) is a "leak" of the colour force, rather like van der Waals forces are a residual electromagnetic interaction. Pions act as the exchange particle for the residual strong force at low energies (Yukawa theory).

    Key equations:

    ΣQ before = ΣQ after
    ΣB before = ΣB after
    ΣL before = ΣL after
    ΔS = 0 (strong), ΔS = ±1 (weak allowed)

    What this shows: in every particle interaction, charge (Q), baryon number (B) and lepton number (L) are always conserved. Strangeness (S) is conserved in strong interactions but may change by ±1 in weak interactions. A reaction that breaks Q, B or L is absolutely forbidden.

    Advanced context. Lepton number is conserved separately for each lepton family (electron, muon, tau). So μ⁻ → e⁻ + γ is forbidden even though total lepton number and charge are conserved, because L_μ would change. CPT symmetry means that for every allowed process, the charge- and parity-mirrored process is also allowed. (Energy and momentum must be conserved too: a reaction that passes these checks can still be impossible if the products have more mass than the reactants can supply.)

    Key equations:

    n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ (β⁻)
    p → n + e⁺ + νₑ (β⁺)
    p + e⁻ → n + νₑ (electron capture)
    e⁺ + e⁻ → 2γ (annihilation)

    What this shows: Feynman diagrams give a picture of particle interactions. Each diagram stands for a contribution to the quantum amplitude. Vertices are interaction points, where the coupling constant sets the probability. Virtual particles (internal lines) do not need to satisfy E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²: they are "off shell".

    Advanced context. The amplitude for each Feynman diagram is proportional to the coupling constant at each vertex (e for electromagnetic, g_W for weak). Higher-order diagrams (loops) give smaller corrections. QED (quantum electrodynamics) predictions agree with experiment to about one part in 10¹²: the most precisely tested theory in physics.

    📝 Exam practice
    How the model works (and what it leaves out)
    • Quantum numbers (Q, B, L, L_e, L_μ, S) are standard values. Strangeness S = −1 for each strange quark and +1 for each anti-strange quark.
    • The conservation checker adds up the numbers on each side. Q, B and L must balance or the reaction is forbidden. If only S changes, by 1, the reaction can only happen through the weak interaction; a change of 2 or more in one step is forbidden. Advanced also checks L_e and L_μ separately.
    • Not checked: energy and momentum (e.g. a neutron cannot decay into heavier particles), spin, and the tau family. A reaction that passes the checks is allowed by these laws, not guaranteed to happen.
    • The track picture is a cartoon of a bubble-chamber photo: charged particles curve in a magnetic field (opposite charges curve opposite ways), neutral particles leave no track (shown dashed), and neutrinos are never seen. Track curvatures and lengths are not to scale.
    • The quark animation shows quarks coming together and staying bound by gluons. In reality quarks are never free; this is a picture, not a model of the strong force. Colour charge is not shown.
    • Batch runs: random reactions are built by picking particles at random from the 20 in the list, with equal chances; most random reactions are forbidden. The beta-decay spectrum uses the allowed shape N(T) ∝ pE(Q − T)² (no Coulomb correction).
