Energy Resources and Electricity · Teacher notesenergy-resources.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: How a power station works

What to look for: About half is lost as heat in the cooling water from the steam turbine. Efficiency = 0.93 × 0.58 × 0.98 ≈ 53%: multiply the stages.

Investigation 2: Compare the resources

What to look for: Wind is cheap and low-carbon but variable (26–39% capacity factor); nuclear is low-carbon and steady but expensive; gas is flexible but emits about 40 times more CO₂ than wind per kWh.

Investigation 3: Run Britain: a winter week

What to look for: Gas fills the gap when the wind drops and in the evening peak. About 150 g/kWh in a typical winter week, but it changes a lot with the weather.

Investigation 4: A calm, cold week

What to look for: With little wind, gas, imports and storage do the work. Switch off gas and blackouts appear: storage of a few hours cannot cover days of calm.

Investigation 5: Why high voltage?

What to look for: I = P/V: 40 000 A at 25 kV, 2500 A at 400 kV. Loss = I²R, so 16 times the voltage gives 1/256 of the loss.

Investigation 6: Classroom model power line

What to look for: Without transformers the lamp's current also flows through the wires, wasting I²R. Stepping up 1 : 20 cuts the line current 20 times and the loss 400 times.

Investigation 7: Wind turbine: P ∝ v³

What to look for: 2³ = 8 times. P = ½ρAv³: twice the speed brings twice the mass of air per second, each kg with four times the kinetic energy.

Investigation 8: Britain's electricity since 2000

What to look for: Gas first (the 1990s 'dash for gas'), then wind and biomass after 2012. Coal fell from about a third to zero: the last coal plant closed in September 2024.

Investigation 9: I²R losses with numbers A level

What to look for: I = 10⁹/(4 × 10⁵) = 2500 A; P = I²R = 2500² × 3 = 18.75 MW, about 1.9%.

Investigation 10: Model turbine: log P against log v A level

What to look for: A gradient of about 3 (± its standard error) supports P ∝ v³. Each reading scatters because a 3% error in v becomes about 9% in P.

Batch experiment 1: Why transmit at high voltage?

Gradient −2: the loss ∝ 1/V². Doubling the voltage quarters the loss.
Theory (∝ 1/V²): 1.82: 1.84 · 2.121: 1.24 · 2.439: 0.599 · 2.602: 0.273
Result from a test run: 1.82: 1.84 ± 0.0019 · 2.121: 1.23 ± 0.00162 · 2.439: 0.598 ± 0.0113 · 2.602: 0.287 ± 0.00547 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Longer lines lose more

A straight line through the origin: R ∝ length, so the loss ∝ length.
Theory (∝ length): 50: 0.938 · 100: 1.88 · 200: 3.75 · 300: 5.63 · 400: 7.5
Result from a test run: 50: 0.949 ± 0.0183 · 100: 1.88 ± 0.0525 · 200: 3.82 ± 0.0886 · 300: 5.74 ± 0.0637 · 400: 7.49 ± 0.0672 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Model turbine: is P ∝ v³?

A gradient close to 3: the power in the wind is ½ρAv³.
Theory (P ∝ v³): 0.301: -1.38 · 0.477: -0.853 · 0.602: -0.478 · 0.699: -0.187 · 0.778: 0.0496 · 0.845: 0.251 · 0.903: 0.425
Result from a test run: 0.301: -1.37 ± 0.0117 · 0.477: -0.869 ± 0.00499 · 0.602: -0.489 ± 0.00481 · 0.699: -0.147 ± 0.0117 · 0.778: 0.0503 ± 0.0533 · 0.845: 0.259 ± 0.0232 · 0.903: 0.436 ± 0.0155 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: How precise is one turbine reading?

SD about 11%: the 3% wobble in the wind speed is cubed (about 9%) and the output adds 6%.
Theory (true): 0.649
Result from a test run: mean 0.653, SD 0.0709, SE 0.00317 from 500 runs

Batch experiment 5: A real turbine's year

About 0.38, varying by a few % from year to year: windy and calm years.
Theory (average over the wind speeds): 0.388
Result from a test run: mean 0.393, SD 0.0202, SE 0.00368 from 30 runs

Batch experiment 6: Windier sites

Rises steeply (v³), then levels off as the turbine spends more time at its rated (maximum) power.
Theory (Weibull average): 4: 0.0836 · 5: 0.159 · 6: 0.25 · 7: 0.343 · 8: 0.43 · 9: 0.504 · 10: 0.564
Result from a test run: 4: 0.0831 ± 0.00492 · 5: 0.159 ± 0.00804 · 6: 0.264 ± 0.00654 · 7: 0.363 ± 0.0142 · 8: 0.434 ± 0.00615 · 9: 0.509 ± 0.0208 · 10: 0.569 ± 0.00793 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 7: A winter week: how much does the weather matter?

A wide spread (windy weeks are clean, calm weeks dirty), and the mean is above the steady-weather value: the system is non-linear (surplus wind in gales is wasted, but shortfalls in calms are filled with gas).
Theory (steady, average weather): 105
Result from a test run: mean 116, SD 36.4, SE 2.57 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 8: More wind: diminishing returns

Each extra GW saves less: in gales the extra power is curtailed, and calm spells still need gas.
Result from a test run: 0: 250 ± 0.627 · 15: 189 ± 4.16 · 30: 120 ± 13.7 · 45: 62.3 ± 8.76 · 60: 35.4 ± 3.48 · 80: 35.1 ± 8.48 · 100: 32.5 ± 4.49 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 9: No gas: how much storage? (beyond the spec)

It falls only slowly: batteries cover hours, but calm spells last days.
Result from a test run: 0: 483 ± 27.3 · 25: 436 ± 53.6 · 50: 571 ± 55 · 100: 505 ± 66 · 200: 456 ± 76.6 · 400: 314 ± 43.6 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
