How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Photons: E = hf = hc/λ with h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s and e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (so hc/e ≈ 1241 eV nm). One photon interacts with one electron and gives it all its energy.
  • Escape: an electron needs at least the work function φ to leave. The fastest have KEmax = hf − φ. Electrons from below the surface lose some energy on the way out, so the model gives each electron a kinetic energy chosen at random, evenly between 0 and KEmax.
  • Work functions (typical clean-surface values): caesium 2.10 eV, potassium 2.30 eV, sodium 2.36 eV, zinc 4.3 eV, copper 4.7 eV, platinum 5.65 eV. Real values change by about ±0.2 eV with the crystal face and how clean the surface is.
  • Intensity sets the number of photons per second. In the animation every photon above the threshold frees one electron. In the photocell and batch run, intensity 1 means 10¹² photons per second and 1 photon in 5 frees an electron (quantum efficiency 20%), giving a saturation current of about 32 nA.
  • Photocell current: with a retarding voltage V only electrons with KE ≥ e|V| reach the anode. With the even spread of energies the current falls in a straight line from the saturation current at V = 0 to zero at −Vs. For V > 0 every emitted electron is collected. (The old version drew a smoothed step; real curves are rounded near −Vs.)
  • Animation: enormously slowed down. Real photons travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s and a 1 eV electron at about 600 km/s. On screen electron speeds are in proportion to √KE, as for real electrons, and in the photocell they slow down steadily in the uniform retarding field.
  • Batch runs: electrons are counted in 10 ns windows with random (Poisson) numbers; stopping voltages are read on a voltmeter with a random reading error (normal distribution) and 0.01 V resolution; LED peak wavelengths have a random uncertainty of a few nm.
  • Left out: light reflected by the surface, heating, the contact potential between cathode and anode (it shifts every Vs by the same amount, so the gradient h/e is unchanged), electrons knocked back off the anode, space charge, and thermionic emission.
