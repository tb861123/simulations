7 · Does the area under the ε–t graph change with speed?

What this shows. The area under the EMF–time graph while the magnet approaches equals the change of flux linkage NΔΦ = 100 × 0.5 × 0.01 = 0.5 Wb-turns, whatever the speed. A faster magnet gives a taller but narrower pulse with the same area. This is Faraday's law in its integrated form: ∫ε dt = NΔΦ. The hand-speed variation cancels out here, which is why this is a better way to measure flux than the peak EMF.

11 · Is the average torque (2/π)BINA?

What this shows. Each run measures the torque on the coil at 20 random moments during its rotation and averages them. With a commutator the torque is τ_max|cos θ|, which averages to (2/π)τ_max = 0.637 × 2.5 = 1.59 N m (B = 0.5 T, I = 5 A, N = 100, A = 0.01 m²). Single runs scatter (the blue curve is the predicted spread) because 20 random angles do not sample the turn evenly; the mean of 200 runs is very close to theory.
