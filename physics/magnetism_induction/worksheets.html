Magnetism and Induction · Teacher notesmagnetism-induction.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: The motor effect

What to look for: The force is proportional to both (F = BIL). Field left to right, current into the page: the force is upwards. Reverse either one and the force reverses.

Investigation 2: A wire along the field

What to look for: Zero: only the part of the current across the field feels a force (F = BIL sin θ). The force is biggest at 90°.

Investigation 3: Push a magnet into a coil

What to look for: Only while it moves: an EMF is induced when the flux through the coil is changing. Pull it out and the reading goes the other way.

Investigation 4: Faster, bigger

What to look for: The peak EMF is bigger (proportional to speed) but lasts less time: the area under the EMF–time graph (NΔΦ) stays the same.

Investigation 5: Step up the voltage

What to look for: Vs = Vp × Ns/Np = 240 V. The secondary current is 20 times smaller than the primary (VpIp = VsIs). The National Grid steps up to reduce current and so I²R losses in the cables.

Investigation 6: The d.c. motor

What to look for: Every half turn the commutator reverses the current in the coil, so the forces keep turning it the same way. Without it, the coil rocks to the vertical and stops.

Investigation 7: Charged particles in a field A level

What to look for: r = mv/(qB) = 5.7 cm; doubling v doubles r. The period T = 2πm/(qB) does not depend on speed (the idea behind the cyclotron).

Investigation 8: Lenz's law and energy A level

What to look for: The induced current makes the coil a magnet that repels the incoming magnet (and attracts the leaving one): whoever moves the magnet does work against this, and that work becomes heat in the coil.

Batch experiment 8: Does the load set the primary current?

The primary current is set by what the secondary supplies: VpIp = VsIs. No load, (almost) no current.

Result from a test run: 20: 2.4 ± 0 · 40: 1.2 ± 0 · 60: 0.8 ± 0 · 100: 0.48 ± 0 · 200: 0.24 ± 0 · 400: 0.12 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 9: Average torque of a d.c. motor

About 76 mN m: (2/π) of the peak, because the commutator keeps the torque in one direction but its size varies as cos θ.
Theory ((2/π)BINA): 76.4
Result from a test run: mean 76.4, SD 2.63, SE 0.263 from 100 runs
