Capacitors · Teacher notescapacitors.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Charge it up

What to look for: At the start: the empty capacitor has no voltage, so all 6 V is across R (I = V/R). As charge builds up, the capacitor's voltage opposes the cell, so the current falls: an exponential curve.

Investigation 2: Q = It

What to look for: Area under I–t = total charge. Here Q = CV = 470 µF × 6 V = 2.82 mC.

Investigation 3: Bigger resistor, slower

What to look for: The starting current halves (I = V/R) and everything takes twice as long. The time constant τ = RC doubles.

Investigation 4: Where does the energy go?

What to look for: The cell gives QV₀; the capacitor stores ½QV₀. Exactly half is turned into heat in the resistor, whatever its resistance.

Investigation 5: The time constant A level

What to look for: τ = RC = 10 kΩ × 470 µF = 4.70 s. After τ, V = V₀/e = 2.21 V; after 2τ, 0.81 V; after 5τ less than 1%.

Investigation 6: ln V against t A level

What to look for: ln V = ln V₀ − t/RC: a straight line of gradient −1/RC. The specified practical uses this to find C (or R).

Investigation 7: Series and parallel A level

What to look for: Series: 1/C = 1/100 + 1/220 + 1/470, C = 59.9 µF (less than the smallest). Parallel: C = 790 µF (the sum).

Investigation 8: A leaky capacitor A level

What to look for: The capacitor also discharges through its own insulation: the two resistances act in parallel, so τ = (R R_L/(R + R_L)) C is shorter.

Batch experiment 1: Discharge curve

Exponential: equal times give equal fractions (each 4.7 s multiplies V by 0.37). The scatter comes mostly from the ±10% tolerance of real capacitors.
Theory (V₀e^(−t/RC)): 0: 6 · 2: 3.92 · 4: 2.56 · 6: 1.67 · 8: 1.09 · 10: 0.715 · 15: 0.247 · 20: 0.0851
Result from a test run: 0: 5.99 ± 0.0104 · 2: 3.9 ± 0.031 · 4: 2.67 ± 0.0867 · 6: 1.85 ± 0.0766 · 8: 1.02 ± 0.107 · 10: 0.678 ± 0.0651 · 15: 0.285 ± 0.0237 · 20: 0.0829 ± 0.0144 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Time constant and resistance

A straight line through the origin with gradient C = 0.47 s per kΩ: τ ∝ R.
Theory (τ = RC): 2: 0.94 · 4: 1.88 · 6: 2.82 · 8: 3.76 · 10: 4.7 · 15: 7.05 · 20: 9.4
Result from a test run: 2: 0.876 ± 0.103 · 4: 1.8 ± 0.0812 · 6: 2.85 ± 0.0887 · 8: 3.85 ± 0.0737 · 10: 4.83 ± 0.0779 · 15: 7.15 ± 0.0781 · 20: 9.45 ± 0.085 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Finding C from a ln V graph

Within a few per cent: the straight-line fit uses all the readings, and the small late voltages (where ±0.02 V matters most) carry the largest error.
Theory (true C): 470
Result from a test run: mean 470, SD 10.4, SE 0.737 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 4: Energy stored

U ∝ V²: doubling the voltage stores four times the energy. The area under Q–V is a triangle, so U = ½QV.
Theory (½CV²): 1: 0.235 · 2: 0.94 · 3: 2.12 · 4: 3.76 · 5: 5.88 · 6: 8.46 · 8: 15 · 10: 23.5 · 12: 33.8
Result from a test run: 1: 0.235 ± 0 · 2: 0.94 ± 0 · 3: 2.11 ± 0 · 4: 3.76 ± 0 · 5: 5.87 ± 0 · 6: 8.46 ± 0 · 8: 15 ± 0 · 10: 23.5 ± 0 · 12: 33.8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Half the energy is always lost

Exactly half, whatever the resistance: a big R wastes energy slowly, a small R quickly, but the total is the same.
Theory (½): 1: 0.5 · 2: 0.5 · 3: 0.5 · 4: 0.5 · 5: 0.5 · 6: 0.5
Result from a test run: 1: 0.5 ± 0 · 2: 0.5 ± 0 · 3: 0.5 ± 0 · 4: 0.5 ± 0 · 5: 0.5 ± 0 · 6: 0.5 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Series and parallel (A level)

Dots (series) fall as 100/n; the line (parallel) rises as 100n. Series is like making the gap wider; parallel like making the plates bigger.
Theory (parallel: nC): 1: 100 · 2: 200 · 3: 300 · 4: 400 · 5: 500 · 6: 600
