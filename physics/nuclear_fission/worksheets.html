Nuclear Fission and Chain Reactions · Teacher notesnuclear-fission.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: One fission

What to look for: ²³⁵U + ¹n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3 ¹n: two smaller radioactive nuclei, 2 or 3 neutrons and energy (kinetic energy of the fragments, which heats the fuel). Mass numbers balance: 235 + 1 = 141 + 92 + 3.

Investigation 2: A chain reaction

What to look for: It multiplies by about 1.5 each generation: 1, 1.5, 2.3, 3.4, … an exponential runaway, as in a bomb. Each generation takes only about 10 nanoseconds.

Investigation 3: Control rods

What to look for: Control rods (boron or cadmium) absorb neutrons, lowering k. Operators move them in or out to keep exactly one neutron from each fission causing another fission: k = 1, a steady output.

Investigation 4: Critical mass

What to look for: A small sphere loses too many neutrons through its surface (large surface area for its volume). At about 52 kg (a ball 17 cm across) k = 1: the critical mass. A bomb brings sub-critical pieces together quickly.

Investigation 5: Plutonium

What to look for: Pu-239 releases more neutrons per fission (about 2.9 against 2.4) and is a little denser, so about 10 kg is enough.

Investigation 6: Energy from the mass defect A level

What to look for: Δm = 0.186 u; E = 0.186 × 931.5 MeV = 173 MeV = 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ J per fission. One kilogram of U-235 could release about 7 × 10¹³ J: millions of times more than burning a kilogram of coal.

Investigation 7: Binding energy per nucleon A level

What to look for: Uranium has about 7.6 MeV per nucleon; the fragments (Ba, Kr) about 8.4. The products are more tightly bound, so energy is released: about 0.85 MeV × 236 nucleons ≈ 200 MeV.

Batch experiment 1: What is the critical mass of U-235?

k crosses 1 at about 52 kg: below it the chain dies out, above it runs away.
Theory (ν(1 − P_escape)): 10: 0.671 · 20: 0.8 · 30: 0.882 · 40: 0.943 · 52: 1 · 65: 1.05 · 80: 1.1 · 100: 1.15
Result from a test run: 10: 0.685 ± 0.0036 · 20: 0.791 ± 0.0146 · 30: 0.886 ± 0.00985 · 40: 0.952 ± 0.0216 · 52: 1.02 ± 0.019 · 65: 1.04 ± 0.00643 · 80: 1.11 ± 0.00652 · 100: 1.15 ± 0.0177 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Does plutonium need less?

About 10 kg: more neutrons per fission, so less fuel is needed.

Result from a test run: 2: 0.659 ± 0.0127 · 4: 0.816 ± 0.0122 · 6: 0.876 ± 0.0229 · 8: 0.944 ± 0.0119 · 10: 1.01 ± 0.0127 · 14: 1.11 ± 0.019 · 20: 1.17 ± 0.0208 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Do control rods work?

k falls in a straight line as the rods go in: about 10% absorbed brings 70 kg back to critical.
Theory (k₀(1 − r)): 0: 1.07 · 0.1: 0.96 · 0.2: 0.853 · 0.3: 0.746 · 0.4: 0.64 · 0.5: 0.533 · 0.6: 0.427
Result from a test run: 0: 1.04 ± 0.00842 · 0.1: 0.958 ± 0.017 · 0.2: 0.846 ± 0.00491 · 0.3: 0.738 ± 0.0254 · 0.4: 0.652 ± 0.0106 · 0.5: 0.531 ± 0.0183 · 0.6: 0.431 ± 0.0053 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: How long is a chain when k < 1?

As k approaches 1 the chains get much longer and very variable (a few huge chains carry the average, so 200 chains often fall short of the theory near k = 1): a reactor near k = 1 needs careful control.
Theory ((k/ν) ÷ (1 − k)): 0.3: 0.176 · 0.5: 0.412 · 0.7: 0.96 · 0.8: 1.65 · 0.9: 3.7
Result from a test run: 0.3: 0.275 ± 0.0465 · 0.5: 0.36 ± 0.0684 · 0.7: 0.795 ± 0.171 · 0.8: 1.71 ± 0.345 · 0.9: 3.26 ± 0.768 (mean ± SE, 200 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Will one neutron start a runaway?

Even when k > 1, many chains die out by chance in the first few generations: one reason a weapon needs a neutron source and a fast assembly.
Theory (1 − extinction probability): 1.05: 6.31 · 1.2: 23 · 1.5: 49.1 · 1.8: 69.1 · 2.2: 90
Result from a test run: 1.05: 5.89 ± 0.556 · 1.2: 22.1 ± 0.801 · 1.5: 48.3 ± 1.53 · 1.8: 69.7 ± 0.385 · 2.2: 89.1 ± 1.83 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: How much energy does one fission release?

About 160–180 MeV each (plus about 20 MeV later from the radioactive decay of the fragments): roughly 200 MeV per fission in total.
Theory (¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n): 173
Result from a test run: mean 165, SD 4.89, SE 0.219 from 500 runs
