How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Conduction: each rod (30 cm × 6 mm) is cut into 30 slices. Heat flows between neighbouring slices at a rate kAΔT/Δx (k = thermal conductivity: copper 401, aluminium 237, brass 109, steel 50, glass 1.0, pine 0.12 W/m K) and is lost from the sides (15 W/m²K). Pins drop when their wax reaches 55 ± 1.5 °C. Real rods also differ in thickness and in how well they touch the hot block.
  • Convection: a single circulating loop whose strength depends on how much warmer the heated side is (buoyancy); temperature is carried with the flow and spreads a little by conduction. Real convection is three-dimensional and often turbulent; the speeds and temperatures here are illustrative.
  • Radiation: a 300 W radiant heater treated as a point source (intensity ∝ 1/d²). A plate absorbs a fraction ε (absorptivity = emissivity) and loses heat by convection from both faces (8 W/m²K) and by radiation. Emissivities in the infrared: matt black 0.95, shiny black 0.85, matt white 0.80, shiny silver 0.07.
  • The house: a semi-detached house 8 m × 6 m × 5 m with one shared wall. Heat loss rate = Σ U × A × (T inside − T outside) + 0.33 × air changes an hour × volume × ΔT. Areas: roof 48 m², walls 90 m², windows 14 m², floor 48 m², doors 4 m². U-values: loft 2.3 W/m²K with no insulation (mineral wool k = 0.04 W/m K on top), empty cavity 1.5, filled cavity 0.55, single glazing 4.8, old double 2.8, modern double 1.4, floor 0.7, doors 3.0; draughts 0.8 air changes an hour (0.5 when draught-proofed). Two heat stores: air and furniture (3 MJ/K) and walls and floors (25 MJ/K). The floor is treated as if it lost heat to the outside air (really the ground is warmer), and people, appliances and winter sunshine are taken as a steady 0.8 kW (really they vary through the day and the year).
  • A year and payback: the house is run for a year of UK weather (monthly means, approximately the Met Office 1991–2020 UK averages, ±3 °C each day) with a perfect thermostat and the heating switched off from May to September. Savings = (cost before − cost after) each year; payback = installed cost ÷ saving. Installed costs are typical round figures (loft £900, cavity £2,700, double glazing £8,000, draught-proofing £250, boiler £3,000). Prices rise and fall, grants change the costs, and houses differ a lot.
  • Cooling cans: each can loses heat at (U_eff × area) × (T − 20 °C), with h = 5 + 6ε W/m²K outside, insulator layers adding thermal resistance (m²K/W: newspaper 0.015, bubble wrap 0.07, felt 0.08, cotton wool 0.12) and evaporation from an open top. Errors: starting temperature ±1.5 °C, wrapping ±15%, thermometer ±0.3 °C (read to 0.5 °C) or ±0.3 °C calibration offset (digital, 0.1 °C).
  • Leslie cube: signal ∝ ε (T⁴ − T_room⁴) / (1 + (d / 6 cm)²); ±3% and ±0.02 mV noise. Without topping up, the cube cools with a time constant of about 45 minutes.
  • Quality only changes the picture (particles, rays, electrons). The model and the batch runs are the same at every setting.
