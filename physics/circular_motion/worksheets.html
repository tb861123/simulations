Circular Motion · Teacher notescircular-motion.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Speed in a circle

What to look for: Distance in one turn = circumference = 2πr = 6.28 m. Speed = distance ÷ time = 2πr/T. The speed stays the same, but the direction keeps changing.

Investigation 2: Let go!

What to look for: Straight on along the tangent, the direction it was moving at that instant. With no force on it, it carries on in a straight line at constant speed (Newton's first law). The string was the only thing pulling it towards the centre.

Investigation 3: Faster needs more force

What to look for: F = mv²/r: double v and the force goes up 2² = 4 times. That is why a car going twice as fast round a bend needs four times as much grip.

Investigation 4: Strong enough string?

What to look for: It snaps when mω²r reaches 20 N: ω = √(20 ÷ (0.5 × 1)) = 6.3 rad/s. Then the ball flies off along the tangent.

Investigation 5: Round the bend

What to look for: Above v_max the friction needed to keep the car on its circle is more than the tyres can give, so it skids outwards (it moves in a bigger circle, as if trying to go straight on). Banking helps: part of the normal reaction points towards the centre.

Investigation 6: Loop the loop

What to look for: Just making it: the string goes slack at the top when the speed there is √(gr), which needs √(5gr) = 9.9 m/s at the bottom. Slower than that, the string goes slack part way up and the ball falls inwards as a projectile.

Investigation 7: Conical pendulum

What to look for: T = 2π√(L cos θ/g): a bigger angle means a shorter period (0.55 s faster at 60°). The mass cancels out, just as for a simple pendulum: tension and weight both scale with m.

Investigation 8: Radians and ω A level

What to look for: T = 2π/ω = 1 s. v = ωr = 9.42 m/s; a = ω²r = 59.2 m/s² (6 g), towards the centre.

Investigation 9: Tension in a vertical circle A level

What to look for: Bottom: T − mg = mv²/r, T = 0.5 × 144/2 + 4.9 = 40.9 N. Top: v² = 144 − 4gr = 65.5, T + mg = mv²/r, T = 16.4 − 4.9 = 11.5 N. At the top weight helps provide the centripetal force; at the bottom the string has to overcome it.

Investigation 10: The rotating frame A level

What to look for: In a frame that turns with the ball, the ball is still, so it looks as if an outward force balances the tension. That 'force' is not real: it is the effect of the frame accelerating. In the real (inertial) frame there is only the inward tension. (Beyond the spec.)

Batch experiment 1: Whirling bung: is F = mω²r?

The results scatter evenly around Mg: the centripetal force is supplied by the tension, which is the weight of the hanging mass. Timing 10 turns instead of 1 makes the reaction-time error 10 times less important.
Theory (Mg): 0.981
Result from a test run: mean 0.98, SD 0.0466, SE 0.00466 from 100 runs

Batch experiment 2: Bung period against radius

Doubling r multiplies T by √2: the same force must give the bung a bigger circle, so it goes round more slowly.

Result from a test run: 0.2: 0.457 ± 0.0135 · 0.4: 0.651 ± 0.0101 · 0.6: 0.78 ± 0.0103 · 0.8: 0.885 ± 0.00725 · 1: 1.01 ± 0.0129 · 1.2: 1.1 ± 0.0102 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Conical pendulum: does mass matter?
