Investment Appraisal and Decision Trees · Teacher notesinvestment-appraisal.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: How long to pay back?

What to look for: After 2 years £55,000 is back; the remaining £25,000 comes from year 3's £30,000: 25/30 of a year = 10 months. Payback = 2 years 10 months.

Investigation 2: Average rate of return

What to look for: Total inflows £135,000 (with £5,000 resale) − £80,000 cost = £55,000 over 5 years = £11,000 a year. ARR = 11,000 ÷ 80,000 = 13.75%.

Investigation 3: Quick payback or more profit?

What to look for: The van pays back in 2 years 5 months, the panels in 5 years. But the panels go on earning for 15 years: ARR 13.3% against 11.7%, and (A level) NPV £28,500 against £7,000. Payback favours safety and cash; ARR and NPV favour total return.

Investigation 4: Every year counts the same?

What to look for: The second shop takes exactly 4 years to pay back (the van under 2½) but then earns £60,000 a year: ARR 13.3% against 11.7%, NPV £47,000 against £7,000. Payback alone would prefer the van; ARR and NPV reward the later years.

Investigation 5: Decision tree: launch or upgrade?

What to look for: EMV(launch) = 0.6 × 200k + 0.4 × 30k − 50k = £82,000; EMV(upgrade) = 0.7 × 90k + 0.3 × 30k − 20k = £52,000. Each single launch either earns £150k or loses £20k; only the average approaches £82k.

Investigation 6: Net present value A level

What to look for: NPV ≈ +£28,000 at 8%. Higher rates shrink distant cash flows more, so NPV falls; it reaches 0 at about 20.6% (the internal rate of return, beyond the spec).

Investigation 7: Risk: the future isn't certain A level

What to look for: The forecast NPV is positive, but a noticeable share of futures lose money. A spread of outcomes (sensitivity analysis) is more useful than one number.

Investigation 8: How sensitive is it? A level

What to look for: This is sensitivity analysis: NPV = 0 when cash flows are about 73% of the forecast, so a 27% shortfall wipes out the gain.

Investigation 9: A cautious owner A level

What to look for: With strong risk aversion, the chance of losing £20,000 on a launch weighs heavily: the certainty equivalent of launching can fall below upgrading, even though its EMV is higher. EMV assumes the owner is risk neutral.

Batch experiment 1: Possible futures for the machine

The mean matches the forecast NPV, but individual futures range widely. The fraction below zero is the chance the investment loses value.
Theory (forecast NPV): 28100
Result from a test run: mean 28500, SD 9800, SE 490 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 2: NPV and the discount rate

NPV falls as r rises, curving because discounting compounds. Where it crosses zero is the internal rate of return: the project is worthwhile if the business's cost of capital is below that.
Theory (NPV = Σ CF/(1+r)ᵗ − cost): 0: 55000 · 2: 47400 · 4: 40400 · 6: 34000 · 8: 28100 · 10: 22700 · 15: 10900 · 20: 1130 · 25: -7010
Result from a test run: 0: 50500 ± 2210 · 2: 49100 ± 1470 · 4: 41800 ± 822 · 6: 34400 ± 1750 · 8: 28500 ± 1350 · 10: 21500 ± 1320 · 15: 12400 ± 1220 · 20: -316 ± 814 · 25: -8040 ± 751 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Payback when sales disappoint

Payback stretches quickly as cash flows fall: at 60% the machine only just pays back within its life. Runs that never pay back are left out.
Theory (payback at that level): 60: 4.82 · 70: 4.16 · 80: 3.6 · 90: 3.16 · 100: 2.83 · 110: 2.59 · 120: 2.39
Result from a test run: 60: 4.81 ± 0.036 · 70: 4.04 ± 0.0695 · 80: 3.6 ± 0.0778 · 90: 3.12 ± 0.052 · 100: 2.9 ± 0.0656 · 110: 2.66 ± 0.0387 · 120: 2.38 ± 0.0234 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Decision tree: one launch at a time

Only two results ever happen (+£150k or −£20k): no single launch earns the EMV of £82k. The mean of many launches does.
Theory (EMV): 82000
Result from a test run: mean 82300, SD 83300, SE 3730 from 500 runs

Batch experiment 5: How many decisions before the average is near the EMV? (A level)

The error shrinks like 1/√n. A big firm making many similar decisions can rely on EMV; a small firm making one huge decision cannot.
Theory (σ√(2/πn) (large n)): 5: 29700 · 10: 21000 · 20: 14900 · 50: 9400 · 100: 6640 · 200: 4700 · 500: 2970
Result from a test run: 5: 30600 ± 5960 · 10: 21000 ± 3020 · 20: 13000 ± 2300 · 50: 8840 ± 1530 · 100: 5440 ± 993 · 200: 5210 ± 640 · 500: 3040 ± 388 (mean ± SE, 30 runs each)
