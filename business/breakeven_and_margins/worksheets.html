Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Find the break-even point

What to look for: Where the total revenue line crosses the total cost line: FC ÷ (P − VC) = £3,000 ÷ £2.30 = 1,305 cups. Below it the gap between the lines is a loss; above it, a profit.

Investigation 2: Every unit makes a contribution

What to look for: Each cup adds £3.20 − £0.90 = £2.30 contribution. The first 1,305 cups' contributions pay the rent and wages (fixed costs); only after that is the contribution profit.

Investigation 3: Margin of safety

What to look for: Margin of safety = expected sales − break-even = 1,800 − 1,305 = 495 cups, 27.5% of sales. A bigger margin of safety means a bad month is less likely to bring a loss.

Investigation 4: Cut the fixed costs

What to look for: Break-even falls to 2,400 ÷ 2.30 = 1,043 cups; the margin of safety rises to 757 cups (42%). The TC line moves down, parallel to before.

Investigation 5: Put the price up

What to look for: Contribution rises to £2.70 so break-even falls to 1,111 cups. But with PED 1.4, sales fall about 15%, to 1,526, so profit actually falls slightly (£1,140 to £1,120). Check the profit-against-price graph: the best price is where its curve peaks.

Investigation 6: A risky month

What to look for: With a margin of safety of 27% and sales varying by 40%, roughly a quarter of months fall below break-even: Φ(−27.5/40) ≈ 25%.

Investigation 7: Elastic or inelastic? A level

What to look for: Inelastic demand (PED < 1): a price rise loses relatively few sales, so revenue AND profit rise. Elastic demand (PED > 1): revenue falls. The profit-maximising price with constant PED > 1 is P* = VC × PED ÷ (PED − 1).

Investigation 8: Running out of capacity A level

What to look for: The second cart is a stepped fixed cost: total cost jumps by £1,500 at 2,600 cups. Just above the step the business is back in loss until enough extra cups are sold.

Investigation 9: Target profit A level

What to look for: Output for a target profit = (FC + target) ÷ contribution = (8,000 + 2,000) ÷ 10.50 = 953 cases.

Batch experiment 1: A year of uncertain months

The mean is close to (P − VC) × expected sales − FC = £1,140. Each extra cup adds £2.30, so profit swings £2.30 × 270 = £621 for a one-SD change in sales: profit is far more volatile (in %) than sales because fixed costs don't move.
Theory (expected profit): 1140
Result from a test run: mean 1130, SD 638, SE 31.9 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 2: Margin of safety and the risk of loss

With no margin of safety, half of all months lose money. Each extra 20% of margin (one SD of sales) cuts the risk sharply: 50% → 16% → 2%.
Theory (100 Φ(−MoS ÷ 20%)): 0: 50 · 5: 40.1 · 10: 30.9 · 15: 22.7 · 20: 15.9 · 30: 6.68 · 40: 2.28
Result from a test run: 0: 53.9 ± 3.43 · 5: 38.6 ± 2.75 · 10: 29.4 ± 2.39 · 15: 23.3 ± 2.51 · 20: 14.7 ± 1.82 · 30: 5.28 ± 1.29 · 40: 3.33 ± 1.03 (mean ± SE, 30 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Break-even and price

A curve, not a straight line: break-even falls steeply as price climbs above the variable cost, then flattens out. Near P = VC a tiny change in costs makes a huge change in break-even.
Theory (FC ÷ (P − VC)): 1.5: 5000 · 2: 2730 · 2.5: 1880 · 3: 1430 · 3.5: 1150 · 4: 968 · 5: 732 · 6: 588
Result from a test run: 1.5: 5080 ± 78.8 · 2: 2700 ± 20.5 · 2.5: 1900 ± 20.2 · 3: 1450 ± 14.5 · 3.5: 1150 ± 9.79 · 4: 962 ± 5.94 · 5: 734 ± 2.32 · 6: 587 ± 2.69 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Which price makes the most profit?

Profit peaks at P* = VC × PED/(PED − 1) = 0.90 × 1.4/0.4 = £3.15, close to the current price. Too cheap and each cup contributes little; too dear and nobody buys. Break-even alone can't find this.
Theory ((P − VC)Q(P) − FC): 2: -140 · 2.5: 1070 · 3: 1140 · 3.2: 1140 · 3.5: 1130 · 4: 1080 · 5: 951 · 6: 808
Result from a test run: 2: -153 ± 8.96 · 2.5: 891 ± 47.9 · 3: 1140 ± 92.6 · 3.2: 1090 ± 99.2 · 3.5: 1260 ± 103 · 4: 980 ± 103 · 5: 959 ± 108 · 6: 678 ± 90.5 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Price rise of 10%: what happens to revenue? (A level)

Inelastic (PED < 1): revenue rises. Unit elastic (PED = 1): unchanged. Elastic (PED > 1): revenue falls. Note the noise: real sales data never show the effect this cleanly.
Theory (1.1^(1−PED) − 1): 0.2: 7.92 · 0.5: 4.88 · 0.8: 1.92 · 1: 0 · 1.2: -1.89 · 1.5: -4.65 · 2: -9.09 · 3: -17.4
Result from a test run: 0.2: 6.65 ± 1.34 · 0.5: 4.57 ± 1.44 · 0.8: 0.812 ± 2.26 · 1: -2.47 ± 1.9 · 1.2: -0.925 ± 1.98 · 1.5: -4.12 ± 1.54 · 2: -8.79 ± 1.13 · 3: -16.9 ± 1.78 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)
