How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Cells: the road is 400 cells of 7.5 m (3 km). Each car fills one cell; a lorry fills two (15 m). Time steps are 1 s. Speeds are whole numbers of cells per second, so the possible speeds are 0, 27, 54, 81, 108 and 135 km/h. The speed limit is rounded to the nearest of these (70 mph → 108 km/h).
  • Nagel–Schreckenberg rules (every driver at once): speed up by 1; slow to the gap in front; with probability p slow by 1 more (dawdling); move. Dawdling is the only randomness, and it is what starts phantom jams.
  • Reaction time: in the cell model the reaction time is one step (1 s) for moving cars. A car that has stopped pulls away late: it waits an extra (reaction time − 0.5 s) on average ("slow-to-start"). Slow starters make jams last longer and lower the flow out of a jam. In the smooth model each driver responds to what they saw one reaction time ago.
  • Theory (fundamental diagram): with no dawdling and only cars, the flow per lane is exactly q = min(ρ vmax, 1 − ρ) (ρ = fraction of cells filled, q in cars per cell per second). The peak is at the critical density ρ = 1/(vmax+1). The mean-field estimate imagines cars scattered at random: q = ρ Σg ρ(1−ρ)g [min(vmax, g) − p]. It ignores the way real queues bunch, so it underestimates the flow.
  • Jam waves: a car leaves the front of a jam on average every 1/(1 − p) seconds, and each car frees 7.5 m, so the jam front moves backwards at about 27(1 − p) km/h in this model. Real motorway jams move back at about 15–20 km/h.
  • Lane changing: a driver moves out to overtake if the gap ahead is too small and the other lane has a bigger gap and enough room behind; drivers move back left when there is space (UK "keep left"). Lane changes happen before the speed update. Drivers in a closed lane must merge within 300 m of the cones.
  • Smooth model (IDM): desired speed = limit, safe time gap T = 1.5 s, minimum gap 2 m, acceleration 1 m/s² (lorries 0.5), comfortable braking 2 m/s², time step 0.1 s. Dawdling causes short random lifts off the accelerator.
  • Variable speed limits: every 30 s the overhead signs check the average speed in each 250 m stretch. Upstream of slow traffic they show 60 mph, and 50 mph right next to it, to slow traffic smoothly before it reaches the queue.
  • Straight road: vehicles arrive at random (Poisson) at the chosen rate; if there is no room they queue off-screen, and their waiting time counts in the journey time. The slip road joins the left lane at 1 km.
  • Not modelled: junctions leaving the road, weather, crashes, drivers changing lanes for exits, hard shoulders, different driver personalities (apart from cars and lorries) and drivers looking further ahead than the car in front.
