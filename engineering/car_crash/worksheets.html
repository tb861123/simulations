1 · Crush distance ∝ v²?

Does the crumple distance follow d = ½mv²/F? With a steady crush force, the work done F × d equals the kinetic energy ½mv², so doubling the speed should quadruple the crush (log–log gradient 2; the short force build-up at the start pulls it slightly below 2). A long, 1 m crumple zone is used so it never runs out in this range. The tiny differences come from the force build-up and the occupant's own momentum arriving later through the seatbelt.

2 · Speed and deceleration

Does the car's average deceleration follow v²/2d — and what happens when the crumple zone runs out? While the crumple zone lasts, the crush distance d grows as v², so v²/2d stays the same: a well-designed crumple zone gives the same steady deceleration at any speed (about 20 g here). Above about 37 mph this 65 cm zone is used up (the theory line stops), d cannot grow any more, and the deceleration climbs steeply — which is why 70 mph crashes are so much worse than 30 mph ones. Switch 'Record' to peak chest deceleration or HIC to see the effect on the occupant.

8 · Is momentum conserved?

Air track: 200 random collisions, speeds measured by light gates (±2%) Every run is a different collision: random masses (0.2–5 kg), speeds and bounciness of 0.5–1. The four speeds are measured by light gates with a 2% random error. The ratio pafter/pbefore clusters around 1 whatever the set-up: momentum is conserved in every collision. The spread is entirely measurement error; the model itself conserves momentum exactly, because the two objects push on each other with equal and opposite forces for the same time.

9 · KE lost against restitution

Equal gliders, 4 m/s into one at rest: how much KE is lost for e = 0 to 1? The theory ½μu²(1 − e²) says half the KE is lost when the gliders stick (e = 0), none when the collision is elastic (e = 1), and the curve in between is a parabola in e. The measured points follow it; their scatter comes from the 1% light-gate error, which matters most when little energy is lost.

11 · Glancing collisions: always 90°?

200 elastic collisions of equal balls at random impact parameters For equal masses in an elastic collision the two balls always leave at right angles, whatever the impact parameter: momentum conservation makes the velocity vectors a triangle, and KE conservation (v² = v₁² + v₂²) makes it a right-angled one (Pythagoras). The scatter comes only from measuring each angle with a protractor (±1°). Switch to sweeping b with unequal masses to see the angle change.

12 · Newton's cradle: k in, k out?

5-ball cradle: pull back 1, 2, 3 or 4 balls Each run releases k balls at 2 m/s into the row and counts how many swing out on the other side. It is always exactly k, because that is the only outcome that conserves both momentum and kinetic energy. There is no scatter: the model is deterministic, and real cradles differ only because their collisions are not quite elastic (try the restitution sweep).
