Ethical Theories and Dilemmas · Teacher notesethical-dilemmas.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Pull the lever?

What to look for: Utilitarianism and situation ethics: pull (more lives). Natural law: permitted by double effect (the death is a side-effect). Kant: disputed. Three of four can support pulling.

Investigation 2: Push the man?

What to look for: The man's death is the means of saving the others: double effect no longer applies, and he is used merely as a means. Only act utilitarianism cares purely about the numbers.

Investigation 3: Kant and the murderer

What to look for: A maxim of lying whenever useful could not be a universal law (lies would stop being believed). Kant also says a duty to truth isn't overridden by consequences, a famous objection to his theory.

Investigation 4: Euthanasia

What to look for: Natural law and Kant (traditional reading) are absolutist: no amount of suffering permits killing. Utilitarianism and situation ethics are consequentialist/relativist: their answers depend on the situation.

Investigation 5: Where all agree

What to look for: All four support blowing the whistle, for different reasons (precepts, duty, consequences, love). Convergence strengthens a conclusion, though it still depends on facts (is the danger real?).

Investigation 6: The hedonic calculus

What to look for: Certainty is one of Bentham's seven criteria: an uncertain good counts for less. Here the extent is the expected number saved (5 × chance) minus the one who certainly dies: below a 20% chance, pulling the lever does more harm than good.

Investigation 7: Act or rule? A level

What to look for: A rule 'never kill innocent bystanders' generally maximises happiness (people can trust that they won't be sacrificed). Rule utilitarianism can agree with Kant in practice while still judging by consequences.

Investigation 8: A society of act utilitarians A level

What to look for: Each broken promise does a little good right now, but trust erodes, deals stop happening, and everyone ends up poorer. Rule utilitarians (and Kantians) keep the trust that makes cooperation possible: a strong argument for rule over act utilitarianism.

Investigation 9: Situation ethics in society A level

What to look for: Rarely broken promises keep trust high: situation ethics behaves much like a rule-follower most of the time, with exceptions for serious need, as Fletcher intended.

Batch experiment 1: Act utilitarians and welfare

Each act-utilitarian promise-breaker does slightly more good in the moment, yet welfare falls as their share grows: lost trust destroys the cooperation that produced most of the good. A classic argument (Hume, Mill) for rule utilitarianism.
Result from a test run: 0: 1.19 ± 0.00136 · 10: 1.17 ± 0.00253 · 25: 1.12 ± 0.006 · 50: 1.05 ± 0.00385 · 75: 0.973 ± 0.0089 · 100: 0.895 ± 0.00518 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Trust in the long run

Trust settles at the share of promises kept: each act utilitarian breaks a promise whenever its cost exceeds its benefit. Kant's point: a maxim of breaking promises when convenient undermines the very practice of promising.
Theory (1 − fraction × P(break)): 0: 1 · 10: 0.966 · 25: 0.915 · 50: 0.831 · 75: 0.746 · 100: 0.662
Result from a test run: 0: 1 ± 4.97e-17 · 10: 0.963 ± 0.00181 · 25: 0.917 ± 0.00539 · 50: 0.837 ± 0.00421 · 75: 0.753 ± 0.00799 · 100: 0.666 ± 0.00509 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Where Kant and utilitarianism part company

They agree on 'side-effect' cases and disagree on 'means' cases: the means–end distinction, not the numbers, separates deontology from consequentialism.
Theory (100(1 − q)): 0: 100 · 0.2: 80 · 0.4: 60 · 0.6: 40 · 0.8: 20 · 1: 0
Result from a test run: 0: 100 ± 0 · 0.2: 79.2 ± 1.19 · 0.4: 62.3 ± 2.36 · 0.6: 40.5 ± 2.67 · 0.8: 20 ± 1.77 · 1: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Hedonic calculus and certainty

The score rises steadily with certainty and is negative below 20% (5 × chance < 1 life). The scatter shows a known weakness of the calculus: different people give different scores, so it is less exact than it looks.
