The Circular Flow and the Multiplier · Teacher notescircular-flow.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Follow the money

What to look for: Firms pay incomes to households; households spend most of it back on firms (C). Saving, taxes and imports leak out; investment, government spending and exports flow back in to firms.

Investigation 2: Two sectors

What to look for: With no leakages and no injections, every pound earned is spent: income goes round and round unchanged. Real economies have all five sectors.

Investigation 3: Injections = withdrawals

What to look for: It rises until withdrawals (S + T + M) equal injections (I + G + X): at Y = J ÷ MPW. If injections exceed withdrawals, income grows; if withdrawals exceed injections, it shrinks.

Investigation 4: The multiplier

What to look for: First £10bn, then MPC × 10, then MPC² × 10, … The total approaches £10bn ÷ MPW = £10bn × 2.5 = £25bn (MPW 0.4). Most of the effect comes in the first few rounds.

Investigation 5: Leaky economies

What to look for: More leaks out in imports each round, so the multiplier falls: 1 ÷ (0.1 + 0.15 + 0.4) = 1.5. Small open economies have small multipliers.

Investigation 6: Who gets the money? A level

What to look for: No: the economy's MPC is the income-weighted average, and the richest have the most income but the lowest MPC, so the multiplier falls below 1 ÷ (average MPW). Spending aimed at poorer households has a bigger multiplier.

Investigation 7: The accelerator A level

What to look for: Rising income makes firms invest more (I = I₀ + v ΔY), which raises income further: the rise overshoots and then oscillates (the multiplier–accelerator model of the business cycle).

Investigation 8: Demand-pull inflation A level

What to look for: Short run: output and the price level rise (positive output gap). Then wages catch up, SRAS shifts left, and output returns to potential with a permanently higher price level.

Investigation 9: Spare capacity A level

What to look for: Mostly output: on the flat part of the Keynesian AS curve there is spare capacity, so demand raises real output with little inflation. Near full capacity the same boost mainly raises prices.

Batch experiment 1: Does the multiplier equal 1 ÷ MPW?

Close to 2.5 on average; each economy's value differs a little because its MPC is the income-weighted average of its households' MPCs.
Theory (1 ÷ MPW): 2.5
Result from a test run: mean 2.5, SD 0.0274, SE 0.00158 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 2: Bigger leakages, smaller multiplier

Every leakage shrinks the multiplier: open economies with high imports get less extra income from the same injection.
Theory (1 ÷ (MPS + MPT + MPM)): 0.05: 3.33 · 0.1: 2.86 · 0.2: 2.22 · 0.3: 1.82 · 0.4: 1.54 · 0.5: 1.33
Result from a test run: 0.05: 3.34 ± 0.0168 · 0.1: 2.87 ± 0.027 · 0.2: 2.21 ± 0.0127 · 0.3: 1.82 ± 0.00782 · 0.4: 1.54 ± 0.00374 · 0.5: 1.34 ± 0.00363 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Rounds of spending

A geometric series: 1, 1.8, 2.44, … approaching 5. Half the long-run effect arrives within about three rounds.
Theory ((1 − MPCⁿ) ÷ (1 − MPC)): 1: 1 · 2: 1.8 · 3: 2.44 · 4: 2.95 · 5: 3.36 · 6: 3.69 · 8: 4.16 · 10: 4.46 · 15: 4.82
Result from a test run: 1: 1 ± 0 · 2: 1.8 ± 0 · 3: 2.44 ± 0 · 4: 2.95 ± 0 · 5: 3.36 ± 0 · 6: 3.69 ± 0 · 8: 4.16 ± 0 · 10: 4.46 ± 0 · 15: 4.82 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Who gets the money?

The multiplier falls below 1 ÷ (average MPW): what matters is the MPC of those who receive the income.
Theory (1 ÷ (1 − weighted MPC)): 0: 2.48 · 0.25: 2.37 · 0.5: 2.27 · 0.75: 2.18 · 1: 2.09
Result from a test run: 0: 2.47 ± 0.00852 · 0.25: 2.41 ± 0.00978 · 0.5: 2.31 ± 0.0193 · 0.75: 2.21 ± 0.00762 · 1: 2.11 ± 0.0139 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: The accelerator

With a strong accelerator, income overshoots its new level and oscillates: one explanation of booms and busts.
Theory (multiplier–accelerator): 0: 2.5 · 0.2: 2.51 · 0.4: 2.8 · 0.6: 3.33 · 0.8: 4.06 · 1: 5.13
Result from a test run: 0: 2.49 ± 0.0142 · 0.2: 2.53 ± 0.0318 · 0.4: 2.77 ± 0.00583 · 0.6: 3.31 ± 0.0233 · 0.8: 4.07 ± 0.0186 · 1: 5.16 ± 0.0455 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Does income settle where J = W?

Income climbs from £400bn and settles around £1,000bn, where withdrawals (0.4 × Y) equal injections (£400bn).
Theory (J ÷ MPW): 1000
Result from a test run: mean 999, SD 19.4, SE 1.12 from 300 runs
