Inequality and Redistribution · Teacher notesinequality.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Income or wealth?

What to look for: Wealth: its Lorenz curve bows much further from the line of equality (Gini about 0.6 against about 0.3 for disposable income in the UK). Wealth builds up over a lifetime, earns returns and is inherited.

Investigation 2: Taxes and benefits

What to look for: The UK's progressive income tax and benefits cut the Gini a lot (ONS: about 0.5 for original income to about 0.33 disposable). VAT is regressive: post-tax income is a little more unequal than disposable.

Investigation 3: Poverty

What to look for: Relative poverty (below 60% of this year's median) rises with unemployment. Growth lifts everyone, so absolute poverty (a fixed line) falls even if relative poverty does not.

Investigation 4: Luck alone

What to look for: No: it spreads into an exponential distribution with a Gini of about 0.5, even though nobody is more skilled. Keeping some money back (saving) or a wealth tax narrows the spread.

Investigation 5: The minimum wage

What to look for: Low earners who keep their jobs get more, which narrows inequality, but some lose their jobs, which widens it. The overall effect depends on how many jobs are lost: a real policy trade-off.

Investigation 6: Inheritance

What to look for: Wealth concentrates in families: estates pass on untaxed and well-paid parents tend to have well-paid children. Inheritance tax and low skill persistence (social mobility) slow this down.

Investigation 7: Kuznets curve A level

What to look for: An inverted U: inequality rises as some people move to higher, more varied city pay, then falls once most have moved. The idea is Kuznets' (1955); real data are mixed. Beyond the spec.

Investigation 8: Laffer curve A level

What to look for: t* = 1 ÷ (1 + e) = 67%. Above it, higher rates raise less. Higher rates cut the Gini but also earnings: the equity–efficiency trade-off. HMRC (2012) estimated e ≈ 0.48 for top earners.

Batch experiment 1: Does luck alone create inequality?

About 0.5 every time: random exchange alone gives an exponential distribution. Inequality does not prove that some people are more skilled.
Theory (exponential: ½(1 − 1/n)): 0.497
Result from a test run: mean 0.5, SD 0.0191, SE 0.00191 from 100 runs

Batch experiment 2: Saving in the trading game

Saving narrows the spread: the money follows a gamma distribution whose Gini falls as λ rises (theory: Chakraborti and Chakrabarti, 2000).
Theory (gamma distribution): 0: 0.5 · 0.2: 0.397 · 0.4: 0.313 · 0.6: 0.235 · 0.8: 0.155
Result from a test run: 0: 0.498 ± 0.0045 · 0.2: 0.393 ± 0.00252 · 0.4: 0.315 ± 0.0034 · 0.6: 0.236 ± 0.00235 · 0.8: 0.153 ± 0.00174 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: How much does redistribution lower the Gini?
