Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Why do banks fail?

What to look for: Banks keep only a fraction of deposits as cash. A small rumour is covered by reserves. A larger one makes others fear they will be last in the queue, so they withdraw too: the run feeds itself, the bank sells loans cheaply and can fail even though its loans were good.

Investigation 2: The lender of last resort

What to look for: The central bank lends cash against the bank's loans, so there is no fire sale. Depositors know they will be paid, so they do not panic: the run never starts. This is what the Bank of England did in 2007–08.

Investigation 3: Deposit insurance

What to look for: Insured depositors have no reason to rush. The run is much smaller and the bank survives. Northern Rock (2007) was partly insured, and depositors still queued.

Investigation 4: Bond prices and interest rates A level

What to look for: Nobody pays £100 for £5 a year when new bonds pay £6: the price falls until the yield matches. Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions. Quantitative easing works by buying bonds, raising their price and lowering long-term interest rates.

Investigation 5: Long bonds are riskier A level

What to look for: The 30-year bond's payments are far in the future, so a higher discount rate cuts their value much more. This is what hurt UK pension funds in the 2022 gilt crisis.

Investigation 6: Moral hazard A level

What to look for: With no bailout, depositors charge risky banks more, so banks stay safe. With a guaranteed bailout, risk costs the bank nothing extra: shareholders keep the profits in good years and taxpayers pay in crashes. Regulation (capital ratios) and credible no-bailout rules try to stop this.

Investigation 7: Bubbles A level

What to look for: Chartists buy because prices have risen, which pushes prices up further: a bubble, until fundamentalists' selling wins and it bursts. More speculation means bigger, longer departures from value: a market failure.

Batch experiment 1: Bank runs and the size of the rumour

Below a threshold nothing happens; above it nearly every bank fails. Small differences in confidence decide which side a bank ends up on: runs are self-fulfilling.
Result from a test run: 0.04: 0 ± 0 · 0.08: 24 ± 6.1 · 0.12: 98 ± 2 · 0.16: 100 ± 0 · 0.2: 100 ± 0 · 0.25: 100 ± 0 · 0.3: 100 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 50 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Deposit insurance and runs

Insured depositors have no reason to join a run, so it fizzles out once enough are covered.
Result from a test run: 0: 100 ± 0 · 0.2: 100 ± 0 · 0.4: 94 ± 3.39 · 0.6: 4 ± 2.8 · 0.8: 0 ± 0 · 1: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 50 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Bond price against interest rate

An inverse relationship: at 5% (the coupon rate) the bond is worth exactly £100.
Theory (Σ C/(1+r)ᵗ + 100/(1+r)ⁿ): 1: 138 · 2: 127 · 3: 117 · 4: 108 · 5: 100 · 6: 92.6 · 8: 79.9 · 10: 69.3
Result from a test run: 1: 138 ± 0.0999 · 2: 127 ± 0.0734 · 3: 117 ± 0.153 · 4: 108 ± 0.13 · 5: 99.9 ± 0.178 · 6: 92.6 ± 0.125 · 8: 80 ± 0.0815 · 10: 69.2 ± 0.131 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Which bonds suffer when rates rise?

Long bonds lose far more: their payments are further away, so discounting them more heavily matters more (their 'duration' is longer).
Theory (exact): 1: 0.962 · 2: 1.87 · 5: 4.31 · 10: 7.65 · 20: 12.5 · 30: 15.7
Result from a test run: 1: 0.864 ± 0.13 · 2: 1.74 ± 0.105 · 5: 4.28 ± 0.0893 · 10: 7.78 ± 0.0935 · 20: 12.5 ± 0.16 · 30: 15.6 ± 0.139 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Moral hazard: bailouts and risk

The more certain the rescue, the more risk banks take, because depositors no longer charge for it.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.0309 ± 0.0048 · 0.2: 0.345 ± 0.00612 · 0.4: 0.895 ± 0.0106 · 0.6: 0.999 ± 0.00125 · 0.8: 1 ± 0 · 1: 1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Speculators and mispricing

With few speculators prices track value closely. Past a point, trend-chasing creates bubbles and crashes: prices swing far from value.
Result from a test run: 0: 2.38 ± 0.134 · 0.2: 2.97 ± 0.0854 · 0.4: 3.99 ± 0.2 · 0.5: 4.8 ± 0.347 · 0.6: 5.53 ± 0.16 · 0.7: 9.83 ± 0.875 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)
