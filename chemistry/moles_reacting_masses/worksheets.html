Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Weigh out a mole

What to look for: One mole always has 6.02 × 10²³ particles, but each particle has a different mass: the mass of a mole in grams = Mr (18 g, 58.5 g, 180 g).

Investigation 2: Reacting masses

What to look for: moles Mg = 4.8 ÷ 24 = 0.20; 2Mg → 2MgO, so 0.20 mol MgO × 40 = 8.0 g.

Investigation 3: The limiting reactant

What to look for: Fe: 0.10 mol, S: 0.125 mol; 1 : 1, so iron is limiting: 0.10 mol FeS = 8.8 g, with 0.8 g of sulfur left over.

Investigation 4: Formula of magnesium oxide

What to look for: Usually a little below 1 (about 0.9): some magnesium doesn't burn, some white MgO smoke escapes when the lid is lifted, and a little magnesium nitride forms. These are systematic errors: repeating doesn't remove them.

Investigation 5: Water of crystallisation

What to look for: Blue crystals turn white as the water leaves. With heating to constant mass x ≈ 5. Too few heatings give x < 5 (water still there).

Investigation 6: Molar gas volume

What to look for: 0.060 ÷ 24 = 0.0025 mol H₂ × 24 000 cm³ = 60 cm³. One mole of any gas takes up 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure.

Investigation 7: Percentage yield

What to look for: 3.8 ÷ 5.0 × 100 = 76%. Product is lost in filtering and transferring, the reaction may be reversible or incomplete, and side reactions happen.

Investigation 8: CP1: molar volume A level

What to look for: RT/p = 8.314 × 293 ÷ 101 300 = 0.0240 m³ = 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹. An oxide coat on the ribbon makes your value low; collecting over water makes it a little high.

Investigation 9: Atom economy A level

What to look for: Hydration: 46 ÷ 46 = 100% (one product). Fermentation: 92 ÷ 180 = 51% (CO₂ is a waste product). Atom economy = Mr of the desired product ÷ total Mr of the reactants × 100.

Batch experiment 1: Formula of magnesium oxide: 200 students

The class centres around 0.9, not 1.0: smoke loss, unburnt magnesium and magnesium nitride are systematic errors that all push the same way.
Theory (MgO): 1
Result from a test run: mean 0.896, SD 0.0405, SE 0.00287 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 2: How often to lift the lid?

An inverted U: too rarely and the magnesium can't get enough oxygen; too often and smoke escapes. Best: briefly, about once a minute.
Theory (MgO): 1: 1 · 2: 1 · 3: 1
Result from a test run: 1: 0.686 ± 0.0194 · 2: 0.897 ± 0.00715 · 3: 0.746 ± 0.00862 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Bigger sample, better result?

A bigger sample shrinks the random error (the balance's 0.01 g matters less), but the mean stays below 1: a systematic error doesn't go away with a bigger sample.
Theory (MgO): 0.05: 1 · 0.1: 1 · 0.2: 1 · 0.3: 1 · 0.5: 1
Result from a test run: 0.05: 0.85 ± 0.0191 · 0.1: 0.875 ± 0.0139 · 0.2: 0.893 ± 0.00763 · 0.3: 0.898 ± 0.00539 · 0.5: 0.909 ± 0.00438 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Water of crystallisation: heat for longer

x creeps up towards 5 as more water is driven off: heat to constant mass.
Theory (true x): 1: 5 · 2: 5 · 3: 5 · 4: 5 · 6: 5 · 8: 5
Result from a test run: 1: 1.67 ± 0.0339 · 2: 2.8 ± 0.0391 · 3: 3.64 ± 0.0364 · 4: 4.18 ± 0.0218 · 6: 4.71 ± 0.0169 · 8: 4.9 ± 0.012 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Too strong a flame?

x overshoots 5: the extra mass lost (spitting, decomposition) is wrongly counted as water.
Theory (true x): 1: 5 · 2: 5 · 3: 5 · 4: 5 · 6: 5
Result from a test run: 1: 4.9 ± 0.0256 · 2: 5.53 ± 0.0366 · 3: 5.86 ± 0.0426 · 4: 6.26 ± 0.0495 · 6: 6.96 ± 0.0746 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Limiting reactant: magnesium and acid

The volume rises with the mass of magnesium until the acid runs out (0.0025 mol H₂ = 60 cm³); after that extra magnesium is left over.
Theory (limiting reactant): 0.01: 9.9 · 0.02: 19.8 · 0.04: 39.6 · 0.06: 59.4 · 0.08: 60.1 · 0.1: 60.1
Result from a test run: 0.01: 9.69 ± 0.563 · 0.02: 19.3 ± 0.366 · 0.04: 39.5 ± 0.381 · 0.06: 59.4 ± 0.16 · 0.08: 59.9 ± 0.688 · 0.1: 60.3 ± 0.318 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 7: CP1: molar volume of hydrogen

Close to 24 dm³: the oxide coat (less magnesium than you weighed) and water vapour (extra gas volume) push in opposite directions.
Theory (RT/p): 24.1
Result from a test run: mean 23.7, SD 0.286, SE 0.0202 from 200 runs

Batch experiment 8: pV = nRT: molar volume and temperature

A straight line: Vm ∝ T (in kelvin), from 22.4 dm³ at 0 °C to 25.7 dm³ at 40 °C.
Theory (RT/p): 0: 22.4 · 10: 23.2 · 20: 24.1 · 30: 24.9 · 40: 25.7
Result from a test run: 0: 22.4 ± 0.0803 · 10: 23.2 ± 0.0672 · 20: 24.1 ± 0.0723 · 30: 24.9 ± 0.0776 · 40: 25.6 ± 0.0908 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)
