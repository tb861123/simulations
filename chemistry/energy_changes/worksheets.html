Energy Changes · Teacher notesenergy-changes.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Breaking and making

What to look for: Breaking bonds always takes energy in (uphill); making bonds always gives energy out (downhill). Burning methane breaks 4 C–H and 2 O=O (2648 kJ) and makes 2 C=O and 4 O–H (3466 kJ). More is given out than taken in: ΔH = 2648 − 3466 = −818 kJ/mol, exothermic. The data-book value is −802: bond energies are averages from many molecules.

Investigation 2: Endothermic: lightning

What to look for: The N≡N triple bond is very strong (945 kJ/mol), so breaking the reactants' bonds takes more energy than making NO gives back: ΔH = +181 kJ/mol, endothermic. The activation energy is also huge, so it only happens at thousands of degrees.

Investigation 3: A catalyst

What to look for: The catalyst provides a different route with a lower activation energy, so more collisions have enough energy to react and the reaction is faster. The energies of the reactants and products are unchanged, so ΔH is the same. (The Haber process uses an iron catalyst.)

Investigation 4: Neutralisation in a cup

What to look for: With 1.0 mol/dm³ the temperature rises by about 6.6 °C. Q = 50 g × 4.18 J/g/°C × 6.6 °C = 1380 J. Moles of water = 0.025. ΔH = −1.38 kJ ÷ 0.025 = −55 kJ/mol, a little less negative than the data book (−57.1) because some heat escapes and warms the cup.

Investigation 5: A cold pack

What to look for: The temperature falls: dissolving ammonium nitrate is endothermic (ΔH positive). The energy needed to break up the solid comes from the water, which cools down. Instant cold packs for sports injuries use this.

Investigation 6: Glass beaker, no stirring

What to look for: Glass conducts heat away much faster than polystyrene and has a bigger heat capacity itself, and without stirring the thermometer reads late, by which time heat has been lost. ΔT is too small, so ΔH comes out much too small. Polystyrene is a good insulator with a tiny heat capacity: that is why the practical uses it.

Investigation 7: Extrapolate!

What to look for: While the reaction is still going, heat is already leaking away, so the highest reading is lower than the true rise. Extending the straight cooling line back to the time of mixing estimates the rise there would have been with no heat loss. The extrapolated ΔH is closer to the data-book value (−217 kJ/mol).

Investigation 8: Burning alcohols

What to look for: Each extra CH₂ adds about 650 kJ/mol to ΔHc in the data book, a steady pattern. The measured values are less than half: heat warms the air, the can and the clamp, combustion is incomplete (soot), and some alcohol evaporates. A draught screen and a short gap between flame and can help.

Investigation 9: Build a Born–Haber cycle A level

What to look for: Atomising Na (+107), ionising Na (+496) and atomising ½Cl₂ (+122) take energy in; the electron affinity of Cl (−349) gives some out. Lattice energy = ΔfH − (sum of the others) = −411 − 376 = −787 kJ/mol.

Investigation 10: Why is MgO so strong? A level

What to look for: Ions with double charges attract four times as strongly, and Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ are smaller, so the lattice energy (−3845) more than pays for IE₂ (+1451) and EA₂ (+798). That is why MgO melts at 2852 °C.

Investigation 11: Covalent character A level

What to look for: NaCl agrees within 3%: almost purely ionic. AgCl (10%) and MgCl₂ (8%) are more exothermic than the ionic model predicts: the small or highly charged cation polarises the anion's electron cloud, adding covalent character.

Investigation 12: Hess's law from combustion A level

What to look for: ΔfH = 2(−394) + 3(−286) − (−1367) = −279 kJ/mol (data book −277). Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen do not react cleanly to give only ethanol, so we go round the cycle via CO₂ and H₂O.

Investigation 13: When is it feasible? A level

What to look for: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. For CaCO₃, ΔH = +178 kJ/mol and ΔS = +161 J/K/mol: ΔG < 0 when T > 178 000 ÷ 161 = 1106 K (833 °C). For burning magnesium ΔH is so large and negative that ΔG stays negative until very high temperatures, even though ΔS is negative. Feasible does not mean fast: kinetics still needs the activation energy.
