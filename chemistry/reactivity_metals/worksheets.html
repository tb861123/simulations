Reactivity and Extracting Metals · Teacher notesreactivity-metals.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Metals and acid

What to look for: Magnesium fizzes fast, zinc steadily, iron slowly and copper not at all: Mg > Zn > Fe > Cu. The gas is hydrogen (it pops with a lighted splint): metal + acid → salt + hydrogen. Metals below hydrogen in the series do not react with dilute acids.

Investigation 2: Metals and water

What to look for: Potassium reacts violently (the hydrogen catches fire with a lilac flame), sodium fizzes and melts into a ball, calcium fizzes steadily, magnesium barely reacts with cold water but burns in steam. metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen.

Investigation 3: Aluminium's head start

What to look for: Aluminium is covered in a thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide that protects it. The acid takes a while to remove it; then the aluminium reacts quickly. The same layer is why aluminium is used for window frames and drinks cans: it does not corrode.

Investigation 4: How much hydrogen?

What to look for: Twice the magnesium makes twice the hydrogen (with excess acid): Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂, one mole of gas (24 dm³) per mole of Mg (24.3 g), so 0.1 g gives about 99 cm³. Powder has more surface area, so it reacts faster, but makes the same total amount.

Investigation 5: Displacement

What to look for: Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it displaces it: zinc + copper sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper. The blue colour fades, brown copper appears, and the solution warms up. Copper cannot displace zinc: no reaction. The more reactive metal ends up as the ion.

Investigation 6: Further apart, hotter

What to look for: The further apart two metals are in the series, the more energy is released when one displaces the other: magnesium + copper sulfate gets hottest. That is the basis of cells and batteries: the voltage depends on how far apart the two metals are.

Investigation 7: Extracting iron

What to look for: Carbon is more reactive than iron, so it can take the oxygen from iron oxide (reduction): Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce aluminium oxide: it must be extracted by electrolysis, which uses a lot of electricity.

Investigation 8: Oxidised or reduced? A level

What to look for: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (zinc is oxidised: its oxidation number rises from 0 to +2). Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (copper ions are reduced: +2 to 0). Cu²⁺ is the oxidising agent; zinc is the reducing agent. Sulfate ions are spectators.

Batch experiment 1: Reactivity and rate

The order of rates follows the reactivity series, except aluminium, which has not started yet: its oxide layer must be removed first. Copper gives none: it is below hydrogen.
Result from a test run: 1: 82.3 ± 1.9 · 2: 1.46 ± 1.46 · 3: 12.6 ± 1.69 · 4: 6.14 ± 0.809 · 5: 0.67 ± 0.401 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Mass of magnesium and volume of hydrogen

A straight line through the origin: the volume of gas is proportional to the moles of metal (Mg : H₂ = 1 : 1).
