Key Equations

Le Chatelier: a disturbed equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
↑[Reactant] → shifts RIGHT
↑T (exothermic) → shifts LEFT
↑T (endothermic) → shifts RIGHT
↑Pressure → fewer gas moles
Catalyst: faster rate only — position unchanged.
Exam Practice

[4 marks] In the Haber process, N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) ΔH = −92 kJ/mol. Explain why a temperature of about 450°C is used rather than a much higher or much lower temperature.

At low temperatures, the rate of reaction is too slow [1]; the forward reaction is exothermic, so higher temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left [1]; this decreases the yield of ammonia [1]; 450°C is a compromise between an acceptable rate and yield [1].

[3 marks] The equilibrium N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) is established in a sealed container. State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure.

The equilibrium shifts to the left / towards N₂O₄ [1]; because increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium towards the side with fewer moles of gas [1]; there is 1 mole of gas on the left and 2 moles on the right [1].

[3 marks] A solution contains CoCl₄²⁻ (blue) and Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ (pink): CoCl₄²⁻ + 6H₂O ⇌ Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ + 4Cl⁻, ΔH = +50 kJ/mol. Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added. State the colour change and explain it.

The solution turns blue [1]; adding HCl increases the concentration of Cl⁻ ions [1]; the equilibrium shifts to the left to reduce the Cl⁻ concentration, forming more CoCl₄²⁻ [1].
