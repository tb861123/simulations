Key Facts

Flame: Li red · Na yellow · K lilac · Ca orange-red · Cu blue-green
NaOH ppt: Cu²⁺ blue · Fe²⁺ green · Fe³⁺ brown
Halides + AgNO₃: Cl⁻ white · Br⁻ cream · I⁻ yellow
Gases: H₂ squeaky pop · O₂ relights splint · CO₂ milky limewater
Exam Practice

[2 marks] Describe how you would carry out a flame test and state the flame colour for a potassium compound.

Dip a clean (nichrome/platinum) wire in the sample and hold it in a blue Bunsen flame [1]; potassium gives a lilac (purple) flame [1].

[3 marks] A solution gives a green precipitate when sodium hydroxide is added, which turns brown on standing. Identify the ion and explain.

The ion is iron(II), Fe²⁺ [1]; it forms a green precipitate of iron(II) hydroxide with NaOH [1]; it slowly oxidises in air to brown iron(III) hydroxide [1].

[3 marks] Describe the tests and positive results for hydrogen, carbon dioxide and chlorine gases.

Hydrogen: a lit splint gives a squeaky pop [1]; carbon dioxide: turns limewater milky/cloudy [1]; chlorine: bleaches damp blue litmus paper (turns it white) [1].
