Key Facts

Crude oil = mixture of hydrocarbons
Separated by fractional distillation
Up the column: cooler, shorter chains
Shorter chain → lower b.p., more volatile,
more flammable, less viscous
Column is hot at the bottom, cool at the top
Exam Practice

[3 marks] Explain how fractional distillation separates crude oil into fractions.

The crude oil is heated and vaporised, entering the column which is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top [1]; vapours rise and cool, and each fraction condenses when it reaches a level below its boiling point [1]; fractions with higher boiling points (longer chains) condense lower down; those with lower boiling points condense higher up [1].

[3 marks] Describe how the properties of the fractions change as chain length increases down the column.

Boiling point increases [1]; viscosity increases (they get thicker / flow less easily) [1]; flammability/volatility decreases (they ignite less easily) [1].

[2 marks] Explain why shorter-chain hydrocarbons have lower boiling points.

Shorter chains have weaker intermolecular forces between molecules [1]; so less energy is needed to separate the molecules and boil them [1].
