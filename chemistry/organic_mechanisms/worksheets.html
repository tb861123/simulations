Teacher notes

Investigation 1: From sugar to vinegar

What to look for: Fermentation (yeast, 30 °C, no air) gives ethanol; oxidation (acidified potassium dichromate, or microbes in air) gives ethanoic acid. Overall yield = 0.45 × 0.80 ≈ 36%: every step loses some.

Investigation 2: Testing for C=C

What to look for: Only ethene: bromine adds across the C=C double bond (an addition reaction), so the orange colour goes. Ethane and poly(ethene) are saturated: no change.

Investigation 3: Making an ester

What to look for: Esterification is an equilibrium (Kc ≈ 4), so with 1 mol of each only 2/3 mol of ester forms. Concentrated sulfuric acid is a catalyst: it speeds up reaching equilibrium but does not move it.

Investigation 4: SN2, step by step A level

What to look for: Arrow 1 starts at a lone pair on OH⁻ and ends at the δ+ carbon. Arrow 2 starts at the middle of the C–Br bond and ends on Br, which leaves as Br⁻. Curly arrows always move a pair of electrons.

Investigation 5: Substitution or elimination? A level

What to look for: Elimination is favoured by ethanol as solvent (OH⁻ acts as a base), by higher temperature (it has the higher activation energy) and by branching. Aqueous, warm conditions with a primary bromoalkane give mostly the alcohol.

Investigation 6: Which halogenoalkane is fastest? A level

What to look for: C–I first (yellow AgI), then C–Br (cream), C–Cl last (white). The rate depends on breaking the C–X bond: C–I is the weakest (228 kJ/mol), even though C–Cl is the most polar. Bond enthalpy wins over polarity.

Investigation 7: Aldehyde or acid? A level

What to look for: Ethanal boils at 21 °C: distilling it off at once stops it being oxidised further. Under reflux it condenses back and is oxidised to ethanoic acid. Test the product with Tollens' or Fehling's to see an aldehyde.

Investigation 8: Adding a carbon A level

What to look for: Ethene → bromoethane (HBr) → propanenitrile (KCN in ethanol: this adds the carbon) → propanoic acid (hydrolysis). Three steps: about 0.9 × 0.7 × 0.8 ≈ 50%.

Investigation 9: Identify by tests A level

What to look for: Both give an orange 2,4-DNPH precipitate (C=O). Only ethanal (an aldehyde) gives a silver mirror. Both give iodoform (both have CH₃CO–).

Investigation 10: Benzene: substitution, not addition A level

What to look for: Adding would destroy the delocalised ring, which is very stable (about 150 kJ/mol). By losing H⁺ the ring is restored. The electrophile NO₂⁺ is made from concentrated HNO₃ and H₂SO₄.

Batch experiment 1: Hydrolysis race: C–Cl, C–Br, C–I

Each step down the group is about ten times faster (a log scale): the weaker C–X bond breaks more easily.
Theory (model): 1: 3.86 · 2: 2.96 · 3: 1.96
Result from a test run: 1: 3.85 ± 0.0195 · 2: 2.97 ± 0.023 · 3: 1.98 ± 0.0191 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Primary, secondary, tertiary

Tertiary is fastest: it reacts by SN1 through a stable tertiary carbocation.
Theory (model): 1: 906 · 2: 226 · 3: 15.1
Result from a test run: 1: 896 ± 40.6 · 2: 234 ± 11.8 · 3: 15.8 ± 0.7 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Elimination and temperature

Higher temperature favours elimination, which has the higher activation energy. In water (not shown) it would stay mostly substitution.
Theory (ethanolic): 20: 63.5 · 35: 72.2 · 50: 78.9 · 65: 83.9 · 78: 87.1
Result from a test run: 20: 63.6 ± 0.811 · 35: 72.1 ± 1 · 50: 79.8 ± 0.757 · 65: 84.6 ± 0.979 · 78: 87 ± 0.485 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Esterification: more alcohol, more ester?

Using an excess of the cheaper reagent pushes the equilibrium to the right (Le Chatelier): 2/3 mol with equal amounts, 0.93 mol with six times the alcohol.
Theory (Kc = 4): 0.5: 0.423 · 1: 0.667 · 2: 0.845 · 3: 0.903 · 4: 0.93 · 6: 0.955
Result from a test run: 0.5: 0.431 ± 0.00736 · 1: 0.672 ± 0.0068 · 2: 0.85 ± 0.0059 · 3: 0.894 ± 0.00559 · 4: 0.933 ± 0.0105 · 6: 0.968 ± 0.00994 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Yield of a three-step route

Yields multiply: about 50%. Longer routes lose much more, which is why chemists look for the shortest route.
Theory (0.9 × 0.7 × 0.8): 50.4
Result from a test run: mean 49.9, SD 10.1, SE 0.586 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 6: Distil quickly to keep the aldehyde

The faster the aldehyde escapes, the less is oxidised further. Under reflux (k_d = 0) none is collected.
Theory (model): 0.005: 25 · 0.01: 40 · 0.02: 57.1 · 0.05: 76.9 · 0.1: 87 · 0.2: 93
Result from a test run: 0.005: 26.2 ± 1.1 · 0.01: 40.8 ± 1.02 · 0.02: 57.9 ± 0.885 · 0.05: 75.6 ± 0.838 · 0.1: 87.5 ± 1.58 · 0.2: 95.1 ± 1.49 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)
