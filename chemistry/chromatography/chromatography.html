Key ideas

• Rf = distance moved by the substance ÷ distance moved by the solvent, both from the baseline.
• Rf is always between 0 and 1.
• More soluble substances (in this solvent) travel further: higher Rf.
• A pure substance gives ONE spot; a mixture gives two or more.
• Same Rf (same solvent, same paper) and same colour → probably the same substance.
• Baseline in pencil (graphite is insoluble), above the solvent level; lid on, so the tank is full of solvent vapour.

How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Rf values are fixed for each substance in each solvent. The food-dye, leaf-pigment and amino-acid values are typical of school chromatograms on chromatography paper; real values vary with the paper, temperature and how saturated the tank is with vapour, so always run known reference substances on the same paper.
  • The solvent front rises by capillary flow (Lucas–Washburn): height² = k × time, measured from the solvent surface, so it slows down as it rises. k (cm²/min): water 3.0, salt water 2.8, ethanol 1.6, propanone mixture 5.0, butanol/ethanoic acid/water 0.7. The baseline is 1 cm above the solvent.
  • Each spot moves at its Rf × the front, and spreads as a blurred patch: its size squared = (starting spot)² + spreading along the paper (0.002 cm per cm travelled) + diffusion (2 × 0.0002 cm²/min × time). How dark it looks depends on the amount of dye; overlapping dyes mix like paints (yellow + blue looks green).
  • Lid off: solvent evaporates from the paper, so the front slows and stops near 9 cm, and Rf values come out about 8% higher and less repeatable (the model adds ±0.03).
  • Mistakes: a felt-tip baseline's dyes dissolve and run up the paper; if the solvent is above the baseline the spots dissolve into the solvent in the tank and the chromatogram is lost.
  • Measuring (batch): both distances are read with a mm ruler (to the nearest mm), with a small reading error, and the centre of a spot is judged with an error of about 0.3 × its blur size. That is why large, blurred spots give less precise Rf values.
  • Left out: tailing and streaking of overloaded spots, dyes that change colour with pH, two-way chromatography, and the chemistry of how a locating agent reacts (ninhydrin turns amino acids purple; proline yellow).
