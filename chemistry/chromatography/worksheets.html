Chromatography · Teacher noteschromatography.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Separate black ink

What to look for: Three spots: yellow (tartrazine), red (allura red) and blue (brilliant blue), at the same heights as the references in lanes 2–4.

Investigation 2: Measure Rf

What to look for: Measure from the baseline to the centre of the spot, and from the baseline to the front. Allura red: Rf ≈ 0.38 in water.

Investigation 3: Identify an unknown

What to look for: Four spots: sunset yellow, green S, carmoisine and indigo carmine. A match needs the same Rf (and colour) in the same solvent.

Investigation 4: Pure or a mixture?

What to look for: A pure substance gives one spot (in any solvent). Red food colouring gives one spot here; the inks and green colouring give several.

Investigation 5: Choosing the solvent

What to look for: Permanent marker dye is insoluble in water, so it stays on the baseline (Rf = 0); it dissolves in ethanol and moves. The substances must dissolve in the solvent.

Investigation 6: Spot too big

What to look for: Big spots spread into big blurs that overlap, so close dyes cannot be told apart. Use small, concentrated spots (dry between dabs).

Investigation 7: Solvent too deep

What to look for: The dyes dissolve into the solvent in the tank instead of moving up the paper. The baseline must be above the solvent.

Investigation 8: A felt-tip baseline

What to look for: The ink of the line dissolves and runs, adding streaks of dye. Pencil (graphite) is insoluble, so it stays put.

Investigation 9: Colourless amino acids

What to look for: Amino acids are colourless. A locating agent (ninhydrin) reacts with them to make purple spots so their Rf values can be measured.

Investigation 10: TLC: is my aspirin pure? A level

What to look for: Salicylic acid: aspirin slowly hydrolyses in damp air. Two spots means the old sample is impure.

Investigation 11: TLC: a more polar eluent A level

What to look for: A more polar eluent competes better with the polar silica for the compounds, so they all move further (higher Rf), but may crowd together near the front.

Investigation 12: Gas chromatography A level

What to look for: Higher boiling point → longer retention time. A hotter column shortens every retention time (peaks may merge). % of each = its peak area ÷ total area × 100.

Batch experiment 1: How precise is one Rf measurement?

Most measurements are within ±0.02 of the true value: good enough to match references on the same paper.
Theory (true Rf): 0.38
Result from a test run: mean 0.38, SD 0.00925, SE 4.14e-4 from 500 runs

Batch experiment 2: Rf error and ruler error

Careful reading matters, but rounding to the nearest mm and judging the spot's centre set a floor.
Theory (propagated error): 0: 0.00544 · 0.5: 0.00762 · 1: 0.012 · 1.5: 0.0169 · 2: 0.022 · 2.5: 0.0272
Result from a test run: 0: 0.00655 ± 2.00e-4 · 0.5: 0.00787 ± 5.01e-4 · 1: 0.0104 ± 8.14e-4 · 1.5: 0.0173 ± 0.00133 · 2: 0.0251 ± 0.00174 · 2.5: 0.027 ± 0.00217 (mean ± SE, 100 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Bigger spots: worse separation?

Small spots give sharp, separate spots; big spots merge.
Theory (gap ÷ 2(σ₁ + σ₂)): 1: 1.02 · 2: 0.972 · 4: 0.833 · 6: 0.694 · 8: 0.58 · 10: 0.493
Result from a test run: 1: 1.02 ± 0.00716 · 2: 0.968 ± 0.00734 · 4: 0.828 ± 0.0163 · 6: 0.732 ± 0.0264 · 8: 0.594 ± 0.0499 · 10: 0.47 ± 0.0375 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Does Rf depend on how far the solvent runs?

The mean is the same at every distance (Rf is a ratio), but short runs give less precise values: let the solvent run well up the paper.
Theory (true Rf): 3: 0.38 · 5: 0.38 · 8: 0.38 · 10: 0.38 · 12: 0.38
Result from a test run: 3: 0.376 ± 0.0031 · 5: 0.377 ± 0.0022 · 8: 0.382 ± 0.00113 · 10: 0.382 ± 0.0012 · 12: 0.38 ± 9.27e-4 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: How fast does the solvent rise?

Fast at first, then slower and slower: h ∝ √t (capillary flow). Quadrupling the time only doubles the height.
Theory (h = √(kt)): 1: 1.73 · 4: 3.46 · 9: 5.2 · 16: 6.93 · 25: 8.66 · 36: 10.4 · 49: 12.1 · 64: 13.9
Result from a test run: 1: 1.73 ± 0.00682 · 4: 3.44 ± 0.0254 · 9: 5.25 ± 0.0974 · 16: 7.09 ± 0.0862 · 25: 8.75 ± 0.142 · 36: 10.4 ± 0.194 · 49: 12.2 ± 0.173 · 64: 14 ± 0.298 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Which solvent?

The same dye has a different Rf in each solvent: always compare with references run on the same paper in the same solvent.
Theory (true Rf): 1: 0.9 · 2: 0.93 · 3: 0.35 · 4: 0.02 · 5: 0.45
Result from a test run: 1: 0.899 ± 0.00126 · 2: 0.929 ± 0.00317 · 3: 0.352 ± 0.00272 · 4: 0.0213 ± 0.00192 · 5: 0.444 ± 0.00289 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)
