How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • E° values are the standard table values (298 K, 100 kPa, 1.00 mol dm⁻³), to 2 d.p. Concentration and temperature change E by the Nernst equation, E = E° + (RT/zF) ln([oxidised]/[reduced]); activities are taken as concentrations (real solutions at 1 mol dm⁻³ are far from ideal, so real EMFs differ by a few hundredths of a volt).
  • Displacement: happens when E° of the ion in solution is more positive than E° of the metal. Excess metal in 25 cm³ of 0.50 mol dm⁻³ solution; rate ∝ the ions left (fast for Mg and Zn powder, slow for iron, ×0.08 for a strip). ΔH from standard enthalpies of formation of the aqueous ions; heat capacity 110 J K⁻¹; 0.5% of the temperature excess lost each second. Magnesium also reacts slowly with the water (fizzing): not modelled.
  • Simple cells: each metal takes its approximate potential in seawater (the galvanic series), not E°: the electrolyte is not a solution of the metal's own ions, and surfaces corrode and get coated. Internal resistance 400 Ω per cell; an LED needs 1.8 V before any current flows; a torch bulb (10 Ω) needs far more current than a lemon can give.
  • Half-cells (CP10): voltmeter ±0.005 V, read to 0.01 V; liquid junction ±0.006 V; concentrations ±5%; oxide films on uncleaned metals (a few hundredths of a volt); magnesium (+0.7 V) and aluminium (+1.0 V) never reach E° in water. A 1 kΩ meter loads a cell of about 2 kΩ internal resistance. No salt bridge: no circuit, no reading.
  • Fuel cell: V = 1.229 − 0.05 ln((j + 0.003)/0.0001) − 0.15 j + 0.05 ln(1 − j/1.4) (j in A cm⁻²), a typical shape, not a particular cell; 10 cm² of electrode. Water is taken as liquid; the cell at 25 °C.
  • Lithium-ion: an illustrative open-circuit curve from 3.0 V (empty) to 4.2 V (full), 3.0 A h, 0.05 Ω. Real cells age, warm up and must not be charged above 4.2 V or run below about 2.5 V.
  • Titrations: pipette ±0.06 cm³; burette read to 0.05 cm³; drops of 0.05 cm³; overshooting by up to 0.4 cm³ if you go fast near the end. The electrode-potential curve uses the Nernst equation for the flask with [H⁺] = 1 (beyond the spec).
  • Live readings use new random numbers each time; batch runs use fixed seeds, so they repeat exactly. No quality setting: each picture is a few dozen shapes and draws in about a millisecond.
