Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Displacement

What to look for: The blue fades, a brown coating forms and the temperature rises. Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu. Zinc loses electrons (oxidised); copper ions gain them (reduced).

Investigation 2: Order of reactivity

What to look for: A metal displaces any metal below it: Mg > Zn > Fe > Cu > Ag. The more reactions a metal gives, the more reactive it is; the bigger the temperature rise, the bigger the difference.

Investigation 3: A simple cell

What to look for: Electrons flow from the more reactive metal (zinc, the negative terminal, which dissolves) through the wire to copper. About 0.7 V in salt water.

Investigation 4: Bigger difference, bigger voltage

What to look for: Voltage rises the further apart the metals are, but aluminium gives less than expected: its oxide layer protects it.

Investigation 5: Light an LED

What to look for: About three (3 × 0.7 V). Cells in series add their voltages: that is what a battery is.

Investigation 6: Hydrogen fuel cell

What to look for: The voltage falls from about 1 V (energy lost driving the reactions and pushing current through the cell), so the efficiency falls too. The only product is water.

Investigation 7: CP10: E°cell A level

What to look for: E°cell = E°(+) − E°(−) = 0.34 − (−0.76) = 1.10 V. Readings scatter by about 0.01 V (concentrations, junction potential, cleaning).

Investigation 8: Feasibility and its limits A level

What to look for: E°cell is only 0.03 V: feasible but marginal, and a change of conditions can reverse it. Diluting Cu²⁺ lowers its E by 0.089 V (0.0296 V per tenfold dilution).

Investigation 9: Why a high-resistance voltmeter? A level

What to look for: The cell has about 2 kΩ of internal resistance; drawing current wastes most of the voltage inside it. A high-resistance voltmeter draws almost no current, so it reads the EMF.

Investigation 10: Redox titration A level

What to look for: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O. The end point is the first permanent pink. [Fe²⁺] = 5 × moles of MnO₄⁻ ÷ 0.0250 dm³.

Batch experiment 1: Simple cells: reactivity and voltage

Voltage rises with the difference, but always below E° in salt water (non-standard conditions, oxide films), and aluminium (x = 2.00) falls well below the trend: its oxide layer.
Theory (E° difference): 0.47: 0.47 · 0.78: 0.78 · 1.1: 1.1 · 2: 2 · 2.71: 2.71
Result from a test run: 0.47: 0.159 ± 0.00984 · 0.78: 0.265 ± 0.0113 · 1.1: 0.702 ± 0.00887 · 2: 0.466 ± 0.00539 · 2.71: 1.26 ± 0.00397 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: CP10: EMF of the zinc–copper cell

Theory (E°cell): 1.1

Batch experiment 7: Redox titration: 200 students

Within about ±0.3%: careful volumetric work is very precise. A rough (fast) titre would be about 0.2 cm³ high.

Batch experiment 8: Displacement: how hot does it get?

Mg > Zn > Fe, all below the 'no heat lost' rise (heat escapes while the reaction runs; iron reacts so slowly that much of its heat is lost).
Theory (no heat lost): 0: 60.4 · 1: 24.9 · 2: 17.5
Result from a test run: 0: 38.4 ± 0.473 · 1: 16 ± 0.206 · 2: 6.52 ± 0.152 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
