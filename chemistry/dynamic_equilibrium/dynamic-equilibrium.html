How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Particles are hard discs in a flat (2D) box 40 nm × 25 nm. In the physics every particle has radius 0.3 nm and mass 30 g/mol; they are drawn as different molecules. Speeds follow the Maxwell–Boltzmann spread for the temperature.
  • Two-particle steps (2NO₂ → N₂O₄, H₂ + I₂ → 2HI, 2HI → H₂ + I₂, N₂ + H₂…, 2NH₃ → …) happen on a collision whose energy along the line of centres (½μv²) is at least the activation energy. For 2HI → H₂ + I₂ only 1 in 5 such collisions is the right way round.
  • One-particle steps (A → B, B → A, N₂O₄ → 2NO₂) happen at random: each particle has chance k = ν e−Ea/RT per picosecond. The new NO₂ appears right next to the old one, flying apart.
  • Haber: N₂ has to meet three H₂ at once, which is too rare to simulate directly. When N₂ hits an H₂ with enough energy, the chance that two more H₂ are close enough is taken as ([H₂] ÷ 1 per nm²)². The two nearest H₂ are used up. Real ammonia is made on an iron catalyst in several steps.
  • Cobalt chloride: Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ (pink) + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ CoCl₄²⁻ (blue) + 6H₂O, in water. The water is not drawn (there is so much of it that its concentration hardly changes, so it is left out of Kc). When Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ hits a Cl⁻ with enough energy, the chance that three more Cl⁻ are close enough is ([Cl⁻] ÷ 0.3 per nm²)³. The volume control here means adding water (dilution), not pressure. Ea forward 9 kJ/mol, back 0: ΔH = +9 kJ/mol (real reaction about +50), so heating turns it blue.
  • Energies (kJ/mol): A ⇌ B: Ea 12 forward, 18 back (ΔH = −6). NO₂: 2 and 27 (ΔH = −25; real −57). HI: 14 and 23.4 (ΔH = −9.4, close to the real value). Haber: 5 and 28 (ΔH = −23; real −92). Energies are scaled down where needed so that both directions happen often enough to watch.
  • Catalyst: lowers both activation energies by the same amount, so forward and reverse speed up by the same factor. K and the yield do not change.
  • Theory: at equilibrium forward rate = reverse rate, so Kc = kforward ÷ kreverse. For collisions k = 2d × v̄rel × g × P × e−Ea/RT (halved for two identical particles), with v̄rel = √(πRT/2μ) and g a small crowding correction. ln K against 1/T is a straight line with gradient −ΔH/R (van 't Hoff).
  • Measured Kc uses the average amounts over the last 300 ps. It is only Kc once the mixture is at equilibrium; before that it is the reaction quotient Q.
  • Water bath: an unreactive solvent (not drawn) bumps each particle about once every 3 ps, keeping the temperature steady and the speed spread right. Heat from the reaction is taken away at once.
  • Pressure for the Haber process is shown in scaled atmospheres: about 200 atm for the preset. With only a few hundred particles, amounts and Kc jiggle about: real vessels have about 10²³ particles, so their equilibria look perfectly steady.
  • Not modelled: 3D motion, attractions between particles, heat from the reaction changing the temperature, and different sizes and masses of molecules.

Key equations

Le Chatelier's principle: if a system at equilibrium is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
more reactant (or less product) → shifts RIGHT
raise T → shifts in the endothermic direction
raise pressure → shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules
A catalyst speeds up both directions equally: equilibrium is reached sooner, but the position does not change.
