1 · Is Kc the same every time?

Is Kc the same every time?

Each run starts from a mixture close to equilibrium and Kc = [HI]² ÷ ([H₂][I₂]) is measured from the average amounts. With only about 120 molecules the amounts jiggle, so each run gives a slightly different Kc, scattered around the theory value.

The small amounts of H₂ and I₂ (about 13 each) on the bottom of the fraction make Kc scatter a lot. Their ups and downs also push the average a few percent above theory: a small-number effect that disappears in a real flask.

2 · Does more particles mean less scatter?

Does more particles mean less scatter?

For each number of particles, 30 runs measure Kc, and we plot how much they disagree (the SD). Random fluctuations shrink as 1/√N, so the log–log graph has gradient −½.

A real flask holds about 10²³ molecules: the scatter is about 10⁻¹¹ of the value, far too small to see. That is why real equilibria look perfectly steady.

3 · van 't Hoff: how does K change with T?

van 't Hoff: how does K change with T?

The forward reaction is exothermic (ΔH = −6 kJ/mol), so K falls as the temperature rises. ln K against 1/T is a straight line with gradient −ΔH/R: measuring K at different temperatures tells you ΔH.

This is Le Chatelier in numbers: heating favours the endothermic direction.

4 · Haber: how does pressure change the yield?

Haber: how does pressure change the yield?

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ turns 4 gas molecules into 2, so higher pressure pushes the equilibrium to the right: more ammonia. Higher pressure also makes the reaction faster (more collisions).

Real plants stop at about 200 atm because thicker pipes, stronger pumps and the energy to compress the gas cost too much.

5 · Haber: the temperature compromise

Haber: the temperature compromise

The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature gives a higher equilibrium yield. Now change Record to Time to reach equilibrium or Forward rate: at low temperatures the reaction is much slower.

A fast reaction with a lower yield beats a slow one with a high yield, because unreacted gas is recycled. So plants run at about 450 °C (723 K).

6 · Does a catalyst change the yield?

Does a catalyst change the yield?

With the catalyst, equilibrium is reached much sooner. Now change Record to Kc measured or Yield: they are the same with and without the catalyst, because it speeds up both directions equally.

The theory time is ln 10 ÷ (kf + kr), the time to get 90% of the way.
