Teacher notes

Investigation 1: The molecular ion

What to look for: The molecular ion M⁺ is the peak at the highest m/z (46), ignoring the tiny M+1 peak. The base peak at 31 is CH₂OH⁺: the C–C bond breaks easily next to the O.

Investigation 2: Fragments tell you pieces

What to look for: Propanone gives a huge peak at 43 (CH₃CO⁺); propanal gives 29 (CHO⁺ and C₂H₅⁺). A ketone breaks either side of the C=O.

Investigation 3: Chlorine and bromine

What to look for: Chlorine is 75.8% ³⁵Cl and 24.2% ³⁷Cl, so M : M+2 ≈ 3 : 1. Bromine is about half ⁷⁹Br and half ⁸¹Br, so M : M+2 ≈ 1 : 1. Every fragment that still has the halogen shows the same pair.

Investigation 4: Alcohol, acid or neither?

What to look for: Ethanol: a broad O–H at 3200–3550 and no C=O. Ethanoic acid: a very broad O–H from 2500 to 3300 and a C=O at 1710. Propanone: a strong C=O at 1715 and no O–H.

Investigation 5: Is the oxidation finished?

What to look for: The alcohol's O–H (3200–3550, sharp-ish) is replaced by the much broader acid O–H (2500–3300) and a strong C=O near 1710 appears.

Investigation 6: Counting environments A level

What to look for: Propan-1-ol has three different carbons (three peaks). Propan-2-ol has two: the two CH₃ groups are identical by symmetry. The CH–O and CH₂–O carbons are near 64 ppm because O pulls electrons away from them.

Investigation 7: The n + 1 rule A level

What to look for: CH₃ has 2 neighbouring H (on CH₂) → 3 lines; CH₂ has 3 neighbours → 4 lines. O–H exchanges quickly so it is not split and does not split: a singlet. Areas are in the ratio of the numbers of H: 3 : 2 : 1.

Investigation 8: Shake with D₂O A level

What to look for: O–H (and N–H) protons swap with deuterium, which does not show in ¹H NMR. The peak that vanishes is the O–H. Useful for spotting alcohols, acids and amines.

Investigation 9: Higher field A level

What to look for: The splitting J stays 7 Hz, but the gap between signals in Hz is δ × MHz, so at higher field the multiplets spread apart and stop overlapping. In ppm, the lines of a multiplet crowd together at high field.

Investigation 10: Isomeric esters A level

What to look for: Ethyl ethanoate: a singlet at 2.0 (CH₃ next to C=O) and the quartet at 4.1 (CH₂ next to O). Methyl propanoate: a singlet at 3.7 (CH₃ on O) and the quartet at 2.3 (CH₂ next to C=O). The shift tells you what each group is joined to.

Investigation 11: Mystery molecules A level

What to look for: Start with M⁺ (Mr, and Cl/Br pairs), then IR (which functional groups), then ¹³C (how many carbon environments), then ¹H (groups and neighbours).

Batch experiment 1: M+1 and the number of carbons

A straight line through the origin, gradient 1.1: the number of carbons = (M+1 height ÷ M height) × 100 ÷ 1.1.
Theory (1.1 × n): 2: 2.2 · 3: 3.3 · 4: 4.4 · 5: 5.5 · 6: 6.6 · 8: 8.8 · 10: 11
Result from a test run: 2: 2.24 ± 0.0847 · 3: 3.24 ± 0.1 · 4: 4.33 ± 0.107 · 5: 5.63 ± 0.114 · 6: 6.7 ± 0.144 · 8: 8.82 ± 0.172 · 10: 11.1 ± 0.173 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Chlorine: how close to 3 : 1?

Centred on 3.1 with a spread from counting. Bromine would give about 1.0.
Theory (³⁵Cl ÷ ³⁷Cl): 3.13
Result from a test run: mean 3.14, SD 0.311, SE 0.0156 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 3: Scans and signal-to-noise

Four times the scans doubles S/N: noise averages away as √n. 256 scans take 256 times as long for only 16 times the S/N.
Theory (∝ √scans): 1: 50.4 · 4: 101 · 16: 201 · 64: 403 · 256: 806
Result from a test run: 1: 50 ± 0.574 · 4: 101 ± 1.59 · 16: 205 ± 2.36 · 64: 416 ± 5.7 · 256: 801 ± 7.03 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: How accurate is the 3 : 2 integration?

With few scans the ratio scatters; with more, it settles on 1.5 = 3 H ÷ 2 H. Always round integrations to the nearest simple whole-number ratio.
Theory (3 ÷ 2): 1: 1.5 · 4: 1.5 · 16: 1.5 · 64: 1.5
Result from a test run: 1: 1.55 ± 0.122 · 4: 1.47 ± 0.0412 · 16: 1.5 ± 0.0217 · 64: 1.5 ± 0.0119 (mean ± SE, 12 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Higher field, better resolved?

J stays 7 Hz but the gap between the signals (0.3 ppm) is 18 Hz at 60 MHz and 180 Hz at 600 MHz: higher fields pull signals apart, so the quartet stops running into the singlet.
Theory (noise-free): 60: 0.296 · 100: 0.814 · 200: 0.961 · 300: 0.983 · 400: 0.991 · 600: 0.996
Result from a test run: 60: 0.299 ± 0.00321 · 100: 0.837 ± 0.00236 · 200: 0.986 ± 0.00161 · 300: 1 ± 0 · 400: 1 ± 0 · 600: 1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: How much evidence do you need?

Mr alone cannot tell isomers apart (propanal and propanone, the two esters, the propanols). Each technique removes more candidates; with ¹H NMR almost every structure is fixed.
Result from a test run: 1: 75 ± 5.64 · 2: 86.7 ± 4.43 · 3: 95 ± 2.84 · 4: 96.7 ± 2.34 (mean ± SE, 60 runs each)
