Key Facts

Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ — saturated (single bonds)
Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ — unsaturated (C=C)
Bromine water: alkene decolourises orange→colourless; alkane stays orange
Cracking: long alkane → shorter alkane + alkene (heat + catalyst)
Exam Practice

[2 marks] Describe the bromine water test and its results for an alkane and an alkene.

Add bromine water and shake: with an alkene the orange bromine water turns colourless (decolourises) [1]; with an alkane it stays orange (no change) [1].

[2 marks] Explain why alkenes are described as unsaturated.

Alkenes contain a carbon–carbon double bond (C=C) [1]; so they do not contain the maximum number of hydrogen atoms / can undergo addition reactions [1].

[3 marks] Explain what cracking is and why it is carried out.

Cracking is breaking long-chain hydrocarbons into shorter ones using heat and a catalyst [1]; it produces more useful short-chain alkanes (e.g. petrol) which are in high demand [1]; it also produces alkenes, used to make polymers [1].
