Fractal Explorer · Teacher notesfractal-explorer.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Seahorse valley

What to look for: Spirals of 'seahorse tails' made of smaller spirals, and eventually miniature Mandelbrot sets, each surrounded by its own decorations. The set is not exactly self-similar (the copies are slightly distorted and their surroundings differ), but it is full of near-copies at every scale.

Investigation 2: Inside or outside?

What to look for: For c inside the Mandelbrot set the Julia set is one connected piece. As soon as c leaves it, the Julia set shatters into 'dust' (infinitely many separate points). That is in fact the definition: the Mandelbrot set is the map of which Julia sets are connected.

Investigation 3: Follow the orbit

What to look for: In the big cardioid the orbit spirals into one point. In the big circle to its left it ends up hopping between 2 points; in the bulbs on top, 3; and so on. The number of spokes on a bulb's antenna equals its period. Outside the set the orbit flies away.

Investigation 4: π in the cusp

What to look for: The orbit squeezes through a narrow gap near z = 0.5 and takes about π/√ε steps to escape, so steps × √ε → π. Dave Boll found this in 1991. Through the 'neck' at c = −0.75 + iε it is π/ε. Nobody expected π here. The batch run checks both.

Investigation 5: Newton's three colours

What to look for: Never: wherever two colours meet, the third is there too, in ever smaller blobs. Every boundary point touches all three regions. Try z³ − 2z + 2: the black regions are starting points that never settle, stuck in a cycle.

Investigation 6: The edge of precision

What to look for: Computers store numbers to a fixed number of digits. A graphics card's float has about 7 significant figures; this page glues two together ('double-single') for about 14. Below about 10⁻¹² the neighbouring pixels' c values round to the same number, so they get the same colour. Deeper zooms need cleverer maths ('perturbation') or many more digits.

Investigation 7: How long is the coastline?

What to look for: c = 0 gives a perfect circle: dimension 1, an ordinary curve. The basilica's edge has dimension about 1.27: more than a line, less than an area. Wigglier Julia sets (near the edge of the Mandelbrot set) have dimensions closer to 2.

Batch experiment 1: π in the cusp

The steps are almost exactly proportional to 1/√ε, with gradient π: the best-fit line's gradient comes out at 3.14. Each run's ε is nudged by up to 1%, so the points scatter a little. Boll (1991); proved by Eckmann and others.
Theory (π / √ε): 10: 31.4 · 20: 62.8 · 40: 126 · 80: 251 · 160: 503 · 320: 1010
Result from a test run: 10: 29.8 ± 0.25 · 20: 61 ± 0 · 40: 124 ± 0.289 · 80: 249 ± 0.479 · 160: 500 ± 0.946 · 320: 1010 ± 1.25 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: π through the neck

Here the steps grow like 1/ε (not 1/√ε), again with π as the constant. Why π? Close to these points the orbit behaves like a slowly turning rotation, and π comes from the angle it must turn through.
Theory (π / ε): 10: 31.4 · 30: 94.2 · 100: 314 · 300: 942 · 1000: 3140
Result from a test run: 10: 32.8 ± 0.25 · 30: 95.3 ± 0.25 · 100: 316 ± 0.479 · 300: 941 ± 2.39 · 1000: 3120 ± 3.28 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: The area of the Mandelbrot set

A Monte Carlo estimate: each run's answer scatters because the points are random (SD about 0.06 for 2000 points; four times the points halves it). The mean comes out a little high because some points just outside the set have not escaped by step 500. The true area is about 1.5066, worked out with enormous computer runs; nobody knows a formula for it.
Theory (best known value): 1.51
Result from a test run: mean 1.53, SD 0.0618, SE 0.00565 from 120 runs

Batch experiment 4: Rough edges: box-counting dimension

c = 0 gives a circle, dimension 1, and the estimate agrees (1.00). The edges get rougher further along the axis, reaching about 1.3 at c = −1.3. For the basilica (c = −1) this coarse grid gives about 1.2, a little below the true 1.2683: box-counting on a 512 × 512 grid misses the finest wiggles. Each run shifts the grid slightly, so runs differ a little.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.997 ± 6.17e-4 · -0.25: 0.989 ± 0.00138 · -0.5: 1.03 ± 9.08e-4 · -0.75: 1.23 ± 0.0068 · -1: 1.18 ± 0.00731 · -1.3: 1.3 ± 0.00509 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)
