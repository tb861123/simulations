How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Long profile: height z = z0(e−x/λ − e−L/λ) ÷ (1 − e−L/λ) with λ = L ÷ 4, a smooth concave curve, plus a 20 m step where a band of hard rock makes a waterfall. Real long profiles have more steps (knickpoints).
  • Downstream changes: drainage area grows as (distance)1.67 (Hack's law), so discharge grows downstream. The channel is shaped by the bankfull flow (4 × the mean): width = 3.2√Qbankfull m (Leopold and Maddock); at lower flows the water is narrower (∝ Q0.26). Depth and velocity come from Manning's equation, v = R2/3S1/2 ÷ n, with roughness n from the bed particle size (Strickler) plus extra roughness from boulders, steps and pools upstream.
  • Particle size falls downstream by attrition: D = D0e−0.07x (Sternberg's law), from 250 mm boulders to sand at the mouth. Roundness (Powers scale 1–6) rises downstream. At a site, pebbles vary (log-normal spread).
  • Hjulström curve (cm/s, mm): erosion velocity = 28√D + 7 ÷ √D; settling velocity = 0.7 × 28√D × (D ÷ (D + 0.06))1.5. A fit to the shape of Hjulström's (1935) curves: in reality they are bands, not lines. A moving particle travels in suspension if v > 4 × its settling velocity, by saltation if v > 1.6 ×, otherwise by traction. Particle sizes are drawn on a log scale.
  • Erosion and transport view: each particle feels the velocity with ±15% turbulence. Abrasion lowers the bed in proportion to the bedload moving × velocity; hydraulic action breaks new angular pieces from the bed above 1.5 m/s; attrition rounds and shrinks moving stones (shown much faster than real); solution only happens on limestone (illustrative rates).
  • Meanders: the centre line migrates sideways at a rate E × (−C + 2.5 × upstream-weighted C) (Howard and Knutson 1984), where C is the curvature and the weighting fades over about 1.5 channel widths. Bends grow, move downstream and cut off when a neck is narrower than 1.1 widths. Sizes scale with the channel width, so the wavelength comes out near 11 widths, as Leopold and Wolman (1960) measured. Point bars and river cliffs are drawn from the curvature.
  • Levees: each overbank flood drops a layer that thins away from the channel: 60% of it within a few channel widths (coarse, building levees), 40% spread over about 12 widths (fine silt and clay). Flood sizes vary at random.
  • Waterfalls: the softer rock is undercut at 0.03 × k × √(Q ÷ 10) m per year (k = 1 shale, 0.55 limestone, 0.3 sandstone; Q varies from year to year); the hard cap collapses when the overhang reaches 0.6–1.4 × its thickness × strength (joints at random). Long-run retreat rate = the average undercutting rate. Illustrative constants, set so that High Force and Niagara come out at about the right speeds.
  • Fieldwork: width ±2%, depths at 5 points ±6%, a float timed 3 times over 10 m (±8% speed, ±0.3 s reaction time), surface speed × 0.85 for the mean velocity; discharge = width × mean depth × velocity.
  • Left out: weathering and mass movement on valley sides, rejuvenation, braiding, tides and estuaries in detail, vegetation, human changes (straightening, dredging), and how floods vary over time (see the flooding simulation).
