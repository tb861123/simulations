How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Stores (1750): air 595 GtC (280 ppm; 1 ppm = 2.124 GtC), plants 550, soils 1,700, surface ocean ~870 (top 100 m), middle ocean ~8,800 (100–1,000 m), deep ocean ~29,500, fossil fuels 5,000 (coal, oil and gas that could be dug up — a rough figure), rocks ~60 million GtC.
  • Book-keeping: each year, change in a store = flows in − flows out. The model adds this up every 0.1 year.
  • Photosynthesis: GPP = 120 GtC/yr × forest area × (1 + β ln(CO₂/280)). β (CO₂ fertilisation, about 0.3) means more CO₂ helps plants grow faster — but with diminishing returns (a logarithm).
  • Plants: half of GPP is breathed straight back out (plant respiration). Plants drop 60 GtC/yr × (plants ÷ 550) of leaves and wood into the soil (they last about 9 years).
  • Soils: microbes breathe out 60 GtC/yr × (soils ÷ 1,700), which rises by 1.3× for every 10 °C of warming (the "Q₁₀" feedback). Soil carbon lasts about 28 years.
  • Deforestation moves carbon from plants straight to the air. 10% of the cleared area never grows back (farms, towns), so the land can absorb a little less. Tree planting increases the forest area; the trees then soak up carbon as they grow.
  • Air ↔ sea: about 80 GtC/yr goes each way. Flow in = 0.286 × pCO₂(air); flow out = 0.286 × pCO₂(sea). The sea's pCO₂ is worked out with real seawater chemistry (alkalinity 2,300 µmol/kg, 18 °C, salinity 35, the carbonic acid and borate constants). Most dissolved carbon becomes bicarbonate, which is why the sea holds so much — but each extra bit pushes the sea's pCO₂ up about 10 times faster than its share (the Revelle factor, 9.5 in 1750, rising as the ocean takes up more). So the ocean's uptake slows down as it fills up.
  • Ocean mixing: the extra dissolved carbon is mixed down: surface ↔ middle layer in about 6 years (of surface water), middle ↔ deep in about 300 years. The natural "biological pump" and the old gradient between the layers stay constant, so only the extra carbon is tracked.
  • Ocean pH comes from the same chemistry. A drop of 0.1 means about 26% more hydrogen ions.
  • Rocks: volcanoes give off 0.1 GtC/yr (× the activity slider); rock weathering takes about 0.1 GtC/yr from the air into rivers and the sea; shells and particles sinking from the surface ocean bury 0.1 GtC/yr in sea-floor sediments. These take hundreds of thousands of years to matter.
  • Warming: forcing F = 5.35 ln(CO₂/280) W/m². Surface (heat capacity 8 W yr m⁻² K⁻¹) and deep ocean (100) layers; heat lost to space = (3.71 ÷ sensitivity) × warming. Only CO₂ is counted: methane, other gases and air pollution (aerosols) are left out (today they roughly cancel out). Warming is measured from 1750.
  • Emissions 1750–2024 follow the Global Carbon Project's history approximately (fossil fuels + cement, plus land-use change). Future paths are simplified versions of the IPCC SSP scenarios; after 2100 they are smoothly extended. Negative emissions (capturing CO₂) put carbon back underground.
  • Left out: methane, permafrost thaw, wildfires, changes in ocean circulation, ocean warming and sea-ice effects on uptake (apart from a simple temperature effect), nitrogen limits on plant growth, the slow dissolving of sea-floor chalk (which matters after thousands of years), and regional differences. Real land uptake jumps about from year to year (El Niño); the model is smooth.
  • Where the model and reality disagree: the model starts at 280 ppm, slightly above the ice-core value (277 ppm). It tracks Mauna Loa within a few ppm. Because only CO₂ is counted, its warming for each scenario differs from IPCC figures.
