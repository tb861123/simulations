How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • One column, global average. The whole planet is treated as one surface and one column of air, with day and night, equator and poles averaged. Sunlight arriving per m² of globe is S/4 because a sphere has 4 times the area of the disc that catches the sunlight.
  • Sunlight absorbed = (S/4)(1 − a). The albedo a is set by you. In this model it does not change when ice melts or forms (see the Ice–albedo simulation for that).
  • Stefan–Boltzmann: the surface radiates σT⁴ of infrared, where T is in kelvin. With no atmosphere the balance gives T = (S(1−a)/4σ)¼ = 255 K (−18 °C) for Earth.
  • Grey layers: the air is split into N thin layers. Each absorbs a fraction ε of the infrared reaching it and re-emits the same energy, half up and half down. Energy balance of the stack gives σTs⁴ = OLR × (1 + Nε/(2 − ε)); for many thin layers this is σTs⁴ = OLR × (1 + τ/2), where τ is the total infrared optical thickness. The drawing shows 4 layers; their temperatures come from the same equations.
  • CO₂: τCO₂ grows with the logarithm of the amount, calibrated so each doubling traps 5.35 ln 2 = 3.7 W/m² near today's climate (IPCC simplified formula ΔF = 5.35 ln(C/C₀)). Very thick, high-pressure air (Venus) absorbs far more because crowded molecules absorb over a wider range of wavelengths ("pressure broadening"); the model adds a term for this, tuned to give Venus its 737 K. Thin Martian air gets less.
  • Methane: forcing 0.036(√M − √M₀) W/m² (M in ppb), as in the IPCC formula, ignoring its overlap with nitrous oxide.
  • Water vapour feedback: the water vapour part of τ rises smoothly with temperature, set so it adds the chosen feedback strength f (W/m² per °C) near today's climate; it fades away in very cold climates. It stands in for all the fast feedbacks (water vapour, lapse rate, clouds). Switched off, water vapour stays at its pre-industrial amount.
  • Climate sensitivity (warming for doubled CO₂): simple theory gives ΔT = 3.7 / (λ₀ − f), where λ₀ = 4 × OLR / T ≈ 3.3 W/m² per °C is the extra infrared a warmer planet gives off (the "Planck response"). With no feedback that is about 1.1 °C. The model's own answer is found by solving for the balance temperature.
  • Heat capacity: C dT/dt = absorbed sunlight − OLR + random variability, where C = 4.18 MJ/m³/K × h for a well-mixed ocean layer h metres deep. The response time is about C / (λ₀ − f): about 8 years for 70 m.
  • Calibration: pre-industrial Earth (280 ppm CO₂, 722 ppb methane, albedo 0.30, 1361 W/m²) settles at 13.9 °C; the rest of the greenhouse effect (clouds' infrared effect, ozone, N₂O) is a fixed part of τ.
  • Volcanoes: an eruption adds to the albedo (Pinatubo: +0.011, about −3.7 W/m²), and the haze decays with an e-folding time of 1 year.
  • Left out: the deep ocean (it would slow the last part of any warming by centuries), ice and vegetation changes, the carbon cycle (CO₂ only changes when you change it), weather and the difference between day and night or equator and pole, the real wavelength-by-wavelength spectrum of each gas, and convection, which in reality carries heat up from the surface and makes the lower air cool by about 6.5 °C per km. Because there is no deep ocean, warming in the model finishes sooner than it would in reality.
  • The packets in the picture are illustrative: their numbers are roughly in proportion to the energy flows, but the readouts come from the equations, not from counting packets.
