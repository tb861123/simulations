Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Random sampling

What to look for: The scatter graph rises to the east (longshore drift sorts the pebbles). Your mean is close to the truth but not exact: another trip would differ a little by chance.

Investigation 2: Systematic sampling

What to look for: It covers the whole beach evenly, so the pattern shows clearly and no stretch is missed. It could be biased if the sites line up with a regular pattern (like groynes).

Investigation 3: Stratified sampling

What to look for: Every zone is represented equally, so you can compare them fairly; the medians rise from west to east and the boxes barely overlap.

Investigation 4: Opportunistic sampling

What to look for: The easy places are all at the western, small-pebble end: the mean is far too low (biased) and the pattern is hidden. Taking more sites the same way does not fix it.

Investigation 5: Too few sites

What to look for: With n = 5, rs must be a perfect 1.000 to be significant at 0.05. A real but weak pattern is often missed with so few sites.

Investigation 6: River width downstream

What to look for: Width increases downstream (rs close to +1, significant): discharge grows as tributaries join.

Investigation 7: Is your data reliable?

What to look for: With only 3 rough measurements per site they differ a lot. Measure more items at each site, more carefully, and they agree better.

Investigation 8: How many sites do you need?

What to look for: Only about half find a significant result with 10 sites (the others make a Type II error); with 30 almost all do. That fraction is the test's power.

Investigation 9: Chi-squared: shape and zone A level

What to look for: Expected E = row total × column total ÷ grand total. With 4 degrees of freedom the critical value at 0.05 is 9.49: above it, reject the null hypothesis.

Investigation 10: Student's t-test: centre and suburbs A level

What to look for: t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) with df = n₁ + n₂ − 2; compare |t| with the critical value (2.228 for df = 10 at 0.05).

Investigation 11: False alarms A level

What to look for: About 5%: the 0.05 level means a 1 in 20 chance of a Type I error when the null hypothesis is true.

Batch experiment 1: How close is one fieldtrip's mean?

Centred on the true mean with SD close to (SD of one site) ÷ √10: the standard error. Random sampling is unbiased.
Theory (true mean): 31.1
Result from a test run: mean 31.1, SD 4.03, SE 0.18 from 500 runs

Batch experiment 2: Does a bigger sample fix a biased method?

The error stays about the same however many sites you take: a biased method stays biased. Only the scatter shrinks.
Theory (method bias): 5: -12.2 · 10: -12.2 · 15: -12.2 · 20: -12.2 · 30: -12.2 · 40: -12.2
Result from a test run: 5: -13.2 ± 0.212 · 10: -12.3 ± 0.175 · 15: -12.5 ± 0.121 · 20: -12 ± 0.11 · 30: -12 ± 0.106 · 40: -12.2 ± 0.0823 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: How many sites to find a real pattern?

The power rises with the number of sites: with 6 only a few trips find the pattern (Type II errors), with 30 or more almost all do. The formula is rough for very small samples.
Theory (power (Fisher z)): 6: 0.226 · 10: 0.452 · 15: 0.673 · 20: 0.818 · 30: 0.951 · 40: 0.988
Result from a test run: 6: 0.0333 ± 0.022 · 10: 0.408 ± 0.0417 · 15: 0.692 ± 0.0441 · 20: 0.833 ± 0.0464 · 30: 0.933 ± 0.03 · 40: 1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: False alarms when there is no pattern (A level)

About 5% at every sample size: the significance level is the chance of a Type I error.
Theory (0.05): 6: 0.05 · 10: 0.05 · 15: 0.05 · 20: 0.05 · 30: 0.05
Result from a test run: 6: 0.015 ± 0.015 · 10: 0.05 ± 0.0209 · 15: 0.06 ± 0.01 · 20: 0.08 ± 0.0184 · 30: 0.035 ± 0.0127 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Stratified or random: which is more precise?

Unbiased, and the spread falls as 1 ÷ √n; it is about half that of random sampling, because each zone is always represented.
Theory (unbiased): 6: 0 · 9: 0 · 12: 0 · 18: 0 · 24: 0 · 30: 0 · 45: 0
Result from a test run: 6: -0.0682 ± 0.417 · 9: -0.918 ± 0.376 · 12: -0.344 ± 0.315 · 18: -0.0595 ± 0.272 · 24: 0.00968 ± 0.238 · 30: -0.11 ± 0.24 · 45: -0.0331 ± 0.152 (mean ± SE, 30 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Chi-squared when there is no link (A level)

The mean is about the degrees of freedom (4) and about 5% are above the critical value 9.49.
Theory (df = 4): 4
Result from a test run: mean 3.88, SD 2.83, SE 0.141 from 400 runs

Batch experiment 7: Student's t-test when there is no difference (A level)

Close to 0 (the fixed local differences between the two zones shift it slightly) with an SD a little above 1 (about 1.2 with 6 sites in each group); dividing the SDs by n makes t slightly bigger than the textbook version, so about 7% pass ±2.228.
Theory (no difference): 0
Result from a test run: mean -0.189, SD 1.2, SE 0.0537 from 500 runs
