How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • The sites are made up. Each has a true trend along it (pebbles 12 → 50 mm west to east; channel width 1.6 → 8.6 m over 20 km; EQS −4 → +12 from the centre to the suburbs, 4 points lower on the main road) plus fixed local differences (the same every visit). The strength slider scales the trend; at 0 there is none.
  • Measuring: each item differs from the local mean at random (pebbles by about 35%, width readings by 10%, surveyors' scores by 3 points); then the ruler, tape or surveyor adds an error. Picking by eye takes bigger pebbles (+25%) or kinder scores. The site value is the mean of the items.
  • Sampling: random (uniform), systematic (every L ÷ n from a random start, along the middle), stratified (equal numbers in each third, random within it), opportunistic (close to the car parks, bridges or station), or your own clicks.
  • Statistics: quartiles at the (n + 1) ÷ 4 and 3(n + 1) ÷ 4 positions; mode = most common recorded value, else the modal class; SD dividing by n (Pearson's worked method; dividing by n − 1 gives a slightly larger SD); least-squares line of best fit; Spearman's rank with tied ranks averaged and two-tailed critical values (Zar 1972; above n = 30 from Student's t); chi-squared on zones × categories with standard critical values; Student's t-test (two samples, unpaired) on the first and last zones, Edexcel's formula: t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) with each SD dividing by n, df = n₁ + n₂ − 2 (check against an Edexcel mark scheme; the formula is given in the exam), two-tailed critical values from the t distribution (dividing by n makes t slightly bigger than the textbook pooled Student's t, so a little more than 5% of samples pass by chance when groups are small; it assumes each group is roughly normal with a similar spread); Mann–Whitney U (alternative test for non-normal data, listed by Pearson as relevant for the NEA; not a specified 9GE0 exam technique) on the same two zones with exact critical values.
  • The truth (true mean, whole-site rs, bias of opportunistic sampling) comes from a survey of 3000 places. Power is predicted with Fisher's z, using the variance 1.06 ÷ (n − 3) for Spearman's rs.
  • Left out: real maps and data, tides, weather and seasons, safety and ethics, secondary data, qualitative methods (interviews, photos), and the many other tests an investigation might use.
