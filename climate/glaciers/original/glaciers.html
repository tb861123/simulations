How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Mass balance: b = 0.007 × (height − ELA) m of ice per year, at most +2 m/yr. The ELA rises by 1 ÷ 0.0065 ≈ 154 m for each 1 °C of warming (the temperature falls 6.5 °C per km of height) and falls by about 40 m for 10% more snowfall. Each year's weather moves the ELA up or down at random. Real balance gradients and sensitivities vary from glacier to glacier.
  • Ice flow (shallow-ice approximation): ice flux = Γ H⁵ |slope|² + fs H³ |slope|² (deformation + sliding), Γ from Glen's flow law with n = 3 and A = 2.4 × 10⁻²⁴ Pa⁻³ s⁻¹ (ice at 0 °C); sliding only when warm-based (Oerlemans' fs). One flowline, 200 m steps, a constant-width valley; the bed falls 100 m per km (Alps) or 80 m per km (Lake District) with a rock step.
  • Moraines: a recessional moraine is marked where the snout stayed within 60 m for at least 25 years and then retreated; the furthest advance leaves the terminal moraine.
  • Response time: Jóhannesson et al. (1989): τ ≈ Hmax ÷ (−b at the snout). Steady length: the mean surface height settles at the ELA (as in Oerlemans' minimal glacier model, 2008), L = 2(b₀ − ELA + H̄) ÷ s with mean thickness H̄ ≈ 130 m measured from this model.
  • Corries: abrasion under rotational flow, fastest in the middle of the hollow (∝ sin² across it), plucking and freeze–thaw at the foot of the back wall; up to 1.5 mm a year when warm-based, almost none when cold-based. Corrie glacier balance = snowfall + snow blown onto lee slopes (∝ cos of the angle from downwind) − melt from the midday sun (greatest facing south). Illustrative sizes.
  • Glacial trough: Nye's model of ice as a viscous fluid in the valley cross-section (∇²u = −1, no slip on the bed, free surface), solved on a grid; erosion ∝ (drag on the bed)², up to 2.5 mm a year. The shape is measured by fitting depth ∝ |distance|b: b ≈ 1 for a V, ≈ 2 for a U.
  • Valley map: a made-up valley drawn from formulas (not a real map): the cross-profile changes from V (b = 1) to U (b = 2) as glacial erosion increases, spurs are truncated, the main valley is deepened more than its tributaries (hanging valleys), a rock basin becomes a ribbon lake, corries are carved round a peak (arêtes, a pyramidal peak), and moraines, drumlins, an esker, an outwash plain and erratics are added as the ice retreats.
  • Freeze–thaw: a cycle each time the rock surface temperature crosses 0 °C and thaws again; each day = yearly cycle + day–night cycle + random weather. Ground temperatures by heat conduction (diffusivity 0.08 m² per day), without the latent heat of freezing, so real active layers thaw more slowly. Blocks fall after 20 cycles; ice wedges grow only in permafrost with very cold winters.
  • Left out: real valley shapes and widths, glacier hydrology and surges, debris cover, calving, regelation creep, sub-glacial deposition in detail, the whole ice sheet, and human impacts beyond the threats cards.
