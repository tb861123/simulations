How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Chemical weathering: hydrolysis and oxidation rates ∝ rainfall × an Arrhenius factor (activation energy 55 kJ/mol, about ×2.1–2.4 per 10 °C). Carbonation ∝ water left after evaporation (runoff) × CO₂ solubility (higher in cold water) × a weak temperature factor. Rates are illustrative, of the order measured in the UK and the tropics.
  • Freeze–thaw: a day counts as a freeze–thaw cycle when its temperature range (mean ± day–night swing) straddles 0 °C; each day = yearly cycle + random weather that lasts a few days. Rate ∝ cycles × how often the rock is wet. Exfoliation and salt weathering ∝ (day–night swing)², largest in hot, dry climates. Biological ∝ plant growth (Lieth's Miami model).
  • Peltier-style diagram: the kind of weathering that wins in each climate, worked out from the same rates (after Peltier, 1950). Real rock type, structure and time matter as much as climate.
  • Slope stability: Mohr–Coulomb strength τ = c′ + (σ − u) tan φ′ with Bishop's simplified method of slices; about 120 trial circles joining points behind the crest to points near the foot; the lowest factor of safety is shown. The water table rises in proportion between the sea, river or hillside and the ground surface (fraction m); pore pressure = 9.81 kN/m³ × depth below it. Roots add cohesion only where they reach the slip surface. Strong bedrock lies below the base. Soil strengths are typical published values, chosen to show each case; they are not measurements of these sites.
  • Failures: a slump rotates the slipped block about the circle's centre; falls and flows spread the debris beyond the foot. Ventnor's movement speed rises as F falls towards 1 (illustrative: about 8 mm a year at F = 1.15).
  • Infinite slope: F = [c′ + c_r + (γ − mγ_w) z cos²β tan φ′] ÷ [γ z sin β cos β] for a shallow slip parallel to the surface (the tilt box, the hillside and the A level block).
  • Landslides over the years: daily rain (wet days and storm sizes random, wetter in winter); plants catch some; soil water drains with a 1-day time constant and evaporates (more in summer); water from upslope collects in hollows (×1.5–3). A strip fails when F < 1, loses its soil and slowly refills. One weather series for the whole hillside.
  • Soil creep: heave ε at right angles to the slope, drop vertically: ε tan β downslope per cycle (Kirkby's model).
  • Left out: 3D shape, tension cracks, layered water tables and artesian pressure, earthquakes, progressive failure, rapid drawdown, the real strength and rainfall of each site, and the time it takes for water to reach deep slip surfaces.
