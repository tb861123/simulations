Stellar Life Cycle · Teacher notesstellar-life-cycle.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: The life of the Sun

What to look for: The main sequence lasts about 10 billion years (the Sun is about halfway through). Then it swells into a red giant hundreds of times bigger, puffs off its outer layers as a planetary nebula, and leaves a white dwarf about the size of the Earth, which slowly cools.

Investigation 2: A massive star

What to look for: Only about 11 million years: it is 15 times more massive but over 10 000 times brighter, so it burns its fuel much faster (lifetime ∝ M^−2.5). It becomes a red supergiant, builds an iron core, which collapses: a supernova. What is left is a neutron star (above about 25 solar masses, a black hole).

Investigation 3: A red dwarf

What to look for: Red dwarfs are cool and dim and burn their hydrogen very slowly: a 0.2 solar mass star lives for about a trillion years, far longer than the 13.8 billion years since the Big Bang. None has died yet. They are the most common stars in the galaxy (Proxima Centauri is one).

Investigation 4: Colour and temperature

What to look for: Hotter objects give out more light at every wavelength, and their peak moves to shorter wavelengths (Wien's law: λmax = b ÷ T). A 3300 K star peaks in the infrared and looks red; the Sun peaks in green-blue but looks white; a 25 000 K star peaks in the ultraviolet and looks blue-white.

Investigation 5: The H–R diagram

What to look for: Most stars lie on the main sequence, a band from hot, bright stars (top left) to cool, dim ones (bottom right). Giants and supergiants are cool but bright, so they must be huge (top right). White dwarfs are hot but dim, so they must be tiny (bottom left). Temperature runs backwards: hot on the left.

Investigation 6: How big is Betelgeuse? A level

What to look for: R = √(L ÷ (4πσT⁴)). With L = 1.2 × 10⁵ × 3.83 × 10²⁶ W and T = 3600 K, R ≈ 9 × 10¹¹ m, about 900 times the Sun's radius. If it replaced the Sun it would reach beyond the orbit of Mars.

Investigation 7: Lifetime and mass A level

What to look for: Fuel available ∝ M, but the rate of burning (luminosity) ∝ M^3.5, so lifetime ∝ M ÷ M^3.5 = M^−2.5. A star twice the Sun's mass lives only about 1.8 billion years.
