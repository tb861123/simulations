Planet Formation · Teacher notesplanet-formation.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Build a solar system

What to look for: Usually 2–6 rocky planets inside the frost line, one or two gas giants just beyond it, and leftover icy bodies further out. The 120 embryos merged in thousands of collisions.

Investigation 2: The frost line

What to look for: Just beyond the frost line (2.7 AU for the Sun): water freezes into ice, so there is about four times more solid material, and cores reach the critical mass while gas is still there.

Investigation 3: No gas, no giants

What to look for: No gas giants at all: only rocky and icy planets. The biggest cores reach 10–20 Earth masses but there is no gas to pull in.

Investigation 4: A race against time

What to look for: Often not: the cores need a few million years to reach about 10 Earth masses. Real discs last 1–10 Myr, so giant planets must form quickly.

Investigation 5: A red dwarf

What to look for: The frost line is much closer (about 0.7 AU) because the star is dimmer (L ≈ M⁴). Small planets, rarely a giant: like the planets found around red dwarfs such as TRAPPIST-1.

Investigation 6: A massive disc

What to look for: Cores grow faster and earlier, so they take more gas: super-Jupiters of several hundred Earth masses, often two of them.

Investigation 7: Kepler's third law A level

What to look for: For the Sun, T²/a³ = 1 year² per AU³ for every planet: T² = 4π²a³/GM. For a 0.5 M☉ star it is 2.

Investigation 8: Throwing or catching? A level

What to look for: Jupiter: escape speed 60 km/s against an orbital speed of 13 km/s, so a close pass flings a body faster than the escape speed from the Sun at that distance. Earth: 11 km/s against 30 km/s, so bodies are bent but stay bound: it collides instead.

Batch experiment 1: How many planets form?

Usually 3–7, a little fewer than our 8. Our Solar System also has Uranus and Neptune at 19 and 30 AU, beyond this model's disc.
Theory (Solar System): 8
Result from a test run: mean 5.4, SD 0.96, SE 0.124 from 60 runs

Batch experiment 2: Bigger disc, bigger planets?

A sudden jump: with too little solid material no core reaches the critical mass in time; above that, cores run away into giants hundreds of times Earth's mass.
Result from a test run: 30: 5.79 ± 0.563 · 60: 19.6 ± 1.99 · 100: 250 ± 24 · 150: 477 ± 37.7 · 200: 729 ± 71.5 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: How much gas makes a gas giant?

No gas, no gas giants. A core can only take the gas within reach of its orbit, so even a very gassy disc usually makes just one or two.
Result from a test run: 0: 0 ± 0 · 2: 0.7 ± 0.213 · 5: 1 ± 0 · 10: 1.1 ± 0.1 · 20: 1 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Does the frost line matter?

More rocky planets around brighter stars: the rocky zone is wider, while around dim red dwarfs ice forms close in.
Result from a test run: 0.6: 1.6 ± 0.163 · 0.8: 2.8 ± 0.249 · 1: 3.5 ± 0.224 · 1.2: 5.3 ± 0.423 · 1.4: 7.3 ± 0.213 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: The critical core mass

If a core needs more than about 15 Earth masses, none gets there before the gas disappears: a tipping point.
Result from a test run: 5: 1 ± 0 · 10: 1 ± 0 · 15: 0.7 ± 0.153 · 20: 0 ± 0 · 30: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)
