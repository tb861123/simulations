How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Circular orbits: v² = G M(<r) / r for a sphere. The speeds from each part add as squares: v² = vbulge² + vdisc² + vgas² + vhole² + vhalo². G = 4.30 × 10⁻⁶ kpc (km/s)²/M☉ (887 AU (km/s)²/M☉ for the Solar System).
  • Bulge (Hernquist 1990): M(<r) = M r² / (r + a)².
  • Star and gas discs (Freeman 1970): a thin disc with surface density Σ0 e−R/Rd. v² = 4πGΣ0Rd y² [I0(y)K0(y) − I1(y)K1(y)] with y = R / 2Rd, using modified Bessel functions (polynomial approximations from Abramowitz & Stegun). A disc pulls a little harder than a sphere of the same mass, so its curve peaks at 2.2 Rd. Real gas discs are not exactly exponential.
  • Dark matter halo: NFW: ρ ∝ 1/[(r/rs)(1 + r/rs)²], M(<r) ∝ ln(1 + x) − x/(1 + x). Pseudo-isothermal: ρ = ρ0/(1 + (r/rc)²), M(<r) ∝ x − arctan x. The "halo mass" is the mass inside r₂₀₀, the radius where the halo's average density is 200 times the critical density of the universe (136 M☉/kpc³).
  • Kepler expectation: √(G Mvisible(<r) / r), treating the visible mass as a sphere. Far outside the stars it falls as 1/√r.
  • MOND: the Newtonian pull of the visible matter gN = vvisible²/r is boosted: g = gN [½ + √(¼ + a₀/gN)] (the "simple" interpolating function), a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
  • Fitting: χ² = Σ ((vobserved − vmodel) / error)². For a good fit χ² is about the number of points. The automatic fit changes only the halo (its mass, and the radius if ticked), keeping the visible matter fixed: a coarse search, then a golden-section search.
  • Mystery galaxy and batch data: made from the model on the left: speeds at evenly spaced radii, plus random errors (normal distribution, chosen SD).
  • The animation: (Quality sets how many stars are drawn, from about 900 to 7400; it changes only the picture) stars and gas clouds on circular orbits at the model speed, with 2-armed spirals drawn in at the start (they wind up, because the inner parts go round faster — real spiral arms are density waves that don't wind up). "Observed speeds, visible gravity only" gives each star its observed speed but only the visible matter's pull (treated as a sphere), stepped forward with the leapfrog method.
  • Edge-on view and slit: colours show the speed along our line of sight: blue coming towards us, red going away. The slit spectrum plots the Hα line (656.3 nm) shifted by Δλ = λ v/c for stars along the slit.
  • Not modelled: non-circular motions, warps, the thickness of discs, pressure support in gas, the bar of the Milky Way, how mass-to-light ratios are measured, adiabatic contraction of halos, and other evidence for dark matter (lensing, the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clusters). The observed curves are approximate, rounded re-creations for teaching.
