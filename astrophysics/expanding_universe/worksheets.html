Teacher notes

The results below come from one test run with the default seed, so a class should get the same numbers. A different seed gives slightly different values within the scatter.

1 · Do more galaxies shrink the random error? (SD ∝ 1/√N?)

What this shows. The spread of fitted H0 values falls as more galaxies are measured. On log–log axes the gradient is close to −0.5: random errors shrink as 1/√N, so 4 times as many galaxies halves the error. The points scatter a little around the theory line because each SD is itself estimated from only 40 runs.

2 · Can more galaxies fix a calibration error?

What this shows. Every distance is 20% too small, so the fit converges on 70 ÷ 0.8 = 87.5, not the true 70. The error bars shrink with more galaxies but they shrink around the WRONG value. Systematic errors do not average away — you have to find and fix the cause.

3 · How big is the bias from a calibration error?

What this shows. Fitted H0 follows H0 ÷ (1 + error) exactly: a distance scale that is too short makes the universe expand too fast. Hubble's distances were about 86% too small, which is why he got about 500 km/s/Mpc.

4 · How old is the universe? (age from 1/H0)

What this shows. Each survey of 30 galaxies gives a slightly different Hubble time, scattered around 977.8/70 = 14.0 billion years (the theory line is at 14.07: random errors in the distances pull a least-squares slope down by about half a percent, called regression dilution). The full ΛCDM age is a little less (13.5 billion years) because the expansion has not always run at the same rate.

5 · What age did Hubble's 1929 data give?

What this shows. With distances 7 times too small, the Hubble time comes out near 2 billion years — younger than the Earth (4.5 billion years) as measured from rocks. That conflict was only solved when the distance scale was recalibrated in the 1950s.

6 · Are the quoted error bars honest? (68% inside 1σ?)

What this shows. With only random errors, the true H0 should be within ±1 quoted uncertainty about 68% of the time. Try it again with a calibration error: the error bars stay small but almost never include the truth.

7 · Peculiar velocities: does looking further help?

What this shows. Nearby, galaxies' own motions (600 km/s here) swamp the expansion. Further away, H0 × d is much bigger than the peculiar speed, so the same velocity error matters less. That is why astronomers want standard candles that can be seen far away.
