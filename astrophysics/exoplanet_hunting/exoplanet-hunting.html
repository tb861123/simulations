Exoplanet Hunting

How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Orbits: circular, all planets in one plane at inclination i. Kepler's third law gives the orbit size: a³ = (Mstar + Mplanet) P² (AU, years, solar masses). Planets do not pull on each other.
  • Transits: the fraction of the star covered by the planet's disc (exact circle-overlap formula, so grazing transits work). The star is a uniform disc: real stars are darker near the edge (limb darkening), which rounds the bottom of real transits.
  • Transit depth = (Rp/Rs)². Duration T = (P/π) arcsin(√((1 + k)² − b²) / ((a/Rs) sin i)), k = Rp/Rs, impact parameter b = (a/Rs) cos i. A transit happens only if b < 1 + k; for random orientations the chance is (Rs + Rp)/a.
  • Noise: each brightness measurement has a random (normal) error: ground 2,500 ppm every 5 minutes, observing 8 hours a night and losing 35% of nights to cloud, plus a random offset each night; TESS-like 600 ppm every 30 minutes with a 1-day gap every 13.7 days; Kepler-like 80 ppm every 30 minutes. Real noise also depends on the star's brightness (the noise slider).
  • Star spots make a slow wave in brightness as the star spins (Sun-like 800 ppm, 25 days; red dwarf 4,000 ppm, 12 days). Flares are sudden brightenings that fade in about half an hour. A running median 2 days wide (one night from the ground) is subtracted to remove them, and bright flares are cut out. This also makes very long transits a little shallower.
  • Search (BLS, box least squares): for each trial period the light curve is folded and a box-shaped dip is slid along it; the signal-to-noise ratio is SNR = √(χ² improvement). Trial periods are spaced so a dip of the expected length can't be missed, from 0.5 days to two-thirds of the observing time (at least about two transits). A planet counts as found if SNR > 7.
  • Fitting: a trapezoid-shaped dip (flat bottom, sloping ingress and egress, never sharper than one exposure). The depth is the least-squares best fit: depth = −Σym/Σm², error = σ/√Σm². The period error is how far the period can move before χ² rises by 1.
  • Theory: SNR = depth/σ × √(points in transit). Radius Rp = Rs √depth, so its error is Rs × σdepth / (2√depth). The star's radius and mass are taken as known exactly; in reality their errors (often 5–10%) add to the planet's.
  • Radial velocity: the star's speed along our line of sight is −K sin(2π(t − t0)/P) for each planet, added together, measured on random nights with a random error (the precision slider; active, spotty stars add "jitter"). A sine wave is fitted at the trial period by least squares to get K, then the minimum mass M sin i = K/28.4 m/s × MJupiter × Mstar2/3 × (P/1 yr)1/3. If the planet transits, sin i ≈ 1, so this is the true mass.
  • Density and make-up: density = 5.51 g/cm³ × (M/M⊕)/(R/R⊕)³. Above about 4 g/cm³ (and smaller than 1.7 R⊕) it is probably rocky; below about 2 g/cm³ it must have a thick gas envelope.
  • Habitable zone: where a planet gets between 0.53 and 1.1 times the sunlight Earth gets: from √(L/1.1) to √(L/0.53) AU. Equilibrium temperature 278.6 K × L¼ / √a × 0.7¼ (30% of light reflected).
  • Not modelled: limb darkening, eccentric orbits, planets tugging on each other (transit timing variations), the planet's own light, moons, false positives such as eclipsing binary stars, and stellar granulation noise.
