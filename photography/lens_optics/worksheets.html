Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Wide, standard, telephoto

What to look for: The field of view shrinks as the focal length grows (FOV = 2 arctan(36 ÷ 2f) on full frame): 104° at 14 mm, 40° at 50 mm, 7° at 300 mm. About 40–50 mm on full frame is called 'standard' because it looks natural, roughly like the central view of your eye.

Investigation 2: Telephoto compression is really distance

What to look for: To keep the person the same size you walk back as you zoom in. Because you are now much further away, the person and the tower are at more similar distances from you, so the tower looks much bigger relative to the person: 'compression'. The lens did not do it: standing in the same place with a wide lens and cropping would give the same perspective. Perspective depends only on where the camera is.

Investigation 3: Wide-angle drama

What to look for: You must stand very close (about 1 m), so the background is many times further away than the person and looks tiny and distant. Close up, the nose is much nearer than the ears, so it looks enlarged: the 'wide-angle distortion' of faces is really a perspective effect of standing close.

Investigation 4: Crop factor

What to look for: A smaller sensor records only the middle of the image: the field of view narrows as if the focal length were longer by the crop factor. 50 mm on APS-C frames like 75–77 mm on full frame; on Micro Four Thirds like 100 mm. The perspective is the same, because you have not moved.

Investigation 5: Why phones have tiny lenses

What to look for: A phone's main camera is about 5–6 mm focal length: with a crop factor of about 4.8 that frames like 26 mm. Its depth of field is enormous (everything sharp) because the real focal length is so short, which is why phones fake background blur in software ('portrait mode').

Investigation 6: Focusing and macro A level

What to look for: From 1/f = 1/u + 1/v, the lens must move out by f² ÷ (u − f): only 0.25 mm for a subject at 40 m, but 50 mm at 0.3 m. Magnification m = f ÷ (u − f) reaches 1 when u = 2f (20 cm for a 100 mm lens). Macro lenses can move their glass that far; extension tubes do the same job.

Investigation 7: Depth of field at the same framing A level

What to look for: Surprisingly, the depth of field across the person is almost the same: when the framing (magnification) and aperture are the same, DoF ≈ 2Nc(1 + m) ÷ m², which does not depend on focal length. What changes is the background: the telephoto magnifies it, so its blur looks much bigger, which is why telephotos seem to blur backgrounds more.

Batch experiment 1: Field of view: how much wall fits?

Width ∝ 1/f: doubling the focal length halves the width (and quarters the area) in the frame. A plot of width against 1/f would be a straight line.
Theory (d × 36 ÷ f): 14: 51.4 · 24: 30 · 35: 20.6 · 50: 14.4 · 85: 8.47 · 135: 5.33 · 200: 3.6
Result from a test run: 14: 51.5 ± 0.422 · 24: 29.7 ± 0.166 · 35: 20.6 ± 0.13 · 50: 14.5 ± 0.0961 · 85: 8.52 ± 0.0667 · 135: 5.36 ± 0.0297 · 200: 3.61 ± 0.0237 (mean ± SE, 8 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Telephoto 'compression'

At 14 mm you stand about 1.4 m away, so the tower (41 m from you) looks less than half the person's height. At 300 mm you stand about 30 m away and the tower looks six times taller than the person. Only the distances matter: the focal length just decides where you have to stand.
Theory (from distances only): 14: 0.474 · 24: 0.794 · 35: 1.13 · 50: 1.56 · 85: 2.45 · 135: 3.53 · 200: 4.66 · 300: 5.99
Result from a test run: 14: 0.472 ± 0.00274 · 24: 0.781 ± 0.0042 · 35: 1.13 ± 0.0116 · 50: 1.57 ± 0.0191 · 85: 2.41 ± 0.0297 · 135: 3.47 ± 0.0336 · 200: 4.66 ± 0.027 · 300: 5.93 ± 0.0538 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Longer lens, more shake

The same shake is magnified by the focal length, so long lenses need faster shutter speeds (the '1/focal length' rule) or stabilisation. At 1/60 s, a 50 mm lens gives mostly sharp shots, a 300 mm lens almost none.
Theory (Rayleigh theory): 14: 100 · 24: 99.9 · 35: 96.3 · 50: 80.2 · 85: 42.9 · 135: 19.9 · 200: 9.63 · 300: 4.4
Result from a test run: 14: 100 ± 0 · 24: 99.9 ± 0.0833 · 35: 97 ± 0.471 · 50: 80.1 ± 1.29 · 85: 41 ± 2.46 · 135: 19 ± 1.52 · 200: 10.6 ± 0.699 · 300: 3.67 ± 0.471 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Depth of field at the same framing

The depth of field is almost the same (about 1.7–2 m here) at every focal length; only the wide lens at close range gains a little, because it is nearer its hyperfocal distance. A telephoto background looks more blurred because it is magnified, not because the zone of sharpness is thinner.
Theory (2Nc(1 + m) ÷ m²): 24: 170 · 35: 170 · 50: 170 · 85: 170 · 135: 170 · 200: 170
Result from a test run: 24: 202 ± 5.04 · 35: 185 ± 3.98 · 50: 179 ± 3.22 · 85: 169 ± 2.89 · 135: 174 ± 5.45 · 200: 179 ± 5.21 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Extension tubes and magnification

A straight line through the origin with gradient 1/f: 50 mm of extension on a 50 mm lens gives life size (m = 1). This is the thin-lens equation rearranged: v = f + e, and m = v/u = e/f.
Theory (m = e ÷ f): 0: 0 · 10: 0.2 · 20: 0.4 · 30: 0.6 · 40: 0.8 · 50: 1 · 60: 1.2
Result from a test run: 0: -0.00636 ± 0.0103 · 10: 0.192 ± 0.00812 · 20: 0.403 ± 0.00887 · 30: 0.609 ± 0.00737 · 40: 0.813 ± 0.00779 · 50: 1.01 ± 0.00781 · 60: 1.21 ± 0.0085 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
