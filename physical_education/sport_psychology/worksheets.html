Motivation, Confidence and Leadership · Teacher notessport-psychology.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Goals that work

What to look for: Specific, measurable, achievable goals focus attention and effort and give clear evidence of progress, so confidence and performance rise. 'Do your best' is not a goal.

Investigation 2: Sources of confidence

What to look for: Performance accomplishments matter most, but watching a similar person succeed, encouragement from the coach and controlling nerves all add. Together they can stop a downward spiral.

Investigation 3: Learned helplessness A level

What to look for: Blaming losses on a lack of ability (internal, stable) and wins on luck makes failure feel permanent: effort and self-efficacy fall, results get worse, and the athlete may drop out. The self-serving style protects confidence.

Investigation 4: Attribution retraining A level

What to look for: Effort can be changed, so a loss becomes something to work on, not proof of failure. Coaches retrain attributions from 'I'm no good' to 'I didn't prepare well enough'.

Investigation 5: Who wants a close game? A level

What to look for: The high achiever seeks a 50:50 challenge, where success means most. The failure-avoider picks a sure win or a hopeless task, where failure carries no shame. Coaches can build NAch by setting achievable challenges and rewarding effort.

Investigation 6: The right leader A level

What to look for: Fiedler: task-orientated leaders do best when the situation is very favourable or very unfavourable; person-orientated leaders in between. Chelladurai: performance needs actual behaviour to match what is required; satisfaction needs it to match what players prefer.

Batch experiment 1: Goal setting and wins

Better goals raise effort and confidence: more wins on average, but every season has luck in it too.
Result from a test run: 0: 18.5 ± 1 · 0.25: 17.7 ± 0.943 · 0.5: 18.4 ± 0.98 · 0.75: 19.1 ± 1.18 · 1: 19.8 ± 0.68 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Attribution style and dropping out

Learned helplessness makes dropping out far more likely: losses feel permanent and personal.
Result from a test run: 1: 0 ± 0 · 2: 92.5 ± 4.22 · 3: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Sources of self-efficacy

Each source helps a little; performance accomplishments (winning and owning it) remain the most powerful.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.568 ± 0.0568 · 1: 0.761 ± 0.0433 · 2: 0.859 ± 0.0325 · 3: 0.915 ± 0.0281 (mean ± SE, 15 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Atkinson: tendency against chance of success

Greatest at Pₛ = 0.5: a real challenge. For a failure-avoider the curve turns upside down.
Theory ((NAch − NAF) Pₛ(1 − Pₛ)): 0.1: 0.054 · 0.3: 0.126 · 0.5: 0.15 · 0.7: 0.126 · 0.9: 0.054
Result from a test run: 0.1: 0.0538 ± 0.00246 · 0.3: 0.125 ± 0.00283 · 0.5: 0.153 ± 0.00222 · 0.7: 0.13 ± 0.00135 · 0.9: 0.0546 ± 9.93e-4 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
