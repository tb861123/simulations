# Arousal, Anxiety and Performance

An athlete attempts a skill every two seconds while their arousal changes;
each attempt succeeds or fails with the chance a theory gives. Four views:
arousal theories, mental preparation in a competition, the audience, and groups.

## How to run

Open `arousal-performance.html` in Chrome or Edge. Nothing to install; works offline.

## What it shows

- **Arousal**: golf putt, free throw, penalty, rugby tackle or weightlifting;
  expert or beginner; inverted-U and drive theory (catastrophe theory and the
  zone of optimal functioning at A level). Success against arousal with your
  attempts; arousal over time.
- **Mental preparation**: a cup final, league match, friendly or trial;
  imagery, selective attention, positive self-talk, deep breathing, psyching up
  (progressive muscle relaxation and centring at A level).
- **Audience**: 0 to 60 000 spectators, evaluating or not, home or away:
  social facilitation and inhibition.
- **Groups**: tug of war, Ringelmann's data, social loafing, Steiner's model.
- Nine guided investigations and a Revise tab.

## Batch run

The inverted-U; fine against gross skills (optimum about 25 for a putt, 70 for a
tackle); drive theory for beginners; which mental preparation works best (all
the calming techniques beat doing nothing; psyching up makes a final worse);
the audience; the Ringelmann effect (matches his 1913 data).

## Limits

The theories are drawn as clean curves; real athletes vary, and the theories
are debated. See "The model" tab.
