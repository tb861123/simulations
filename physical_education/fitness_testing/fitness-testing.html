How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Performers: each has a sex, age, height and mass, and a true level for each component of fitness (cardiovascular endurance, flexibility, strength, speed, agility, power, muscular endurance, reaction time), as z-scores. The components are linked through a general fitness factor, and speed, agility and power share a second factor, so fit people tend to be fit at several things, but not all. The squad's fitness level shifts everyone up or down.
  • True test scores come from the z-scores with typical means and spreads for 15–19 year-olds (illustrative). Each test also has a part that is NOT the component it is meant to measure, so no test is perfectly valid: running economy and pacing for the bleep test and Cooper run, limb lengths for the sit and reach, hand size for grip, technique for the vertical jump. VO₂ max: boys 48, girls 40 ml/kg/min on average, SD 6.
  • Measurement: score = true score + day-to-day variation + trial-to-trial variation + tester error, plus protocol effects: no warm-up (worse), a first attempt with no practice (worse, the learning effect), motivation (maximal tests change most; submaximal step tests hardly at all), and varied conditions (extra variation). The sizes are typical published test–retest values where known (for example a typical error of about 0.5 levels for the bleep test, 0.08 s for a hand-timed 30 m sprint, 2 cm for the vertical jump) and otherwise illustrative.
  • Scores are rounded as a tester records them: bleep test to the last full shuttle, Cooper to 10 m, sit and reach and the ruler to 0.5 cm, jumps to 1 cm, pulse counts to whole beats (the Queens College test multiplies a 15 s count by 4, so heart rate goes in steps of 4 bpm). Multi-trial tests take the best trial (or the mean, if chosen; the ruler drop always uses the mean).
  • Equations: bleep VO₂ max = 3.46 × (L + S ÷ (0.4325 L + 7.0048)) + 12.2 (Flouris et al., 2005, reproducing the Ramsbottom tables); Cooper VO₂ max = (d − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 (Cooper, 1968); Harvard index = 100 × time (s) ÷ (2 × sum of three 30 s pulse counts) (Brouha, 1943); Queens College VO₂ max = 111.33 − 0.42 HR (male), 65.81 − 0.1847 HR (female) (McArdle et al., 1972); Lewis power = √4.9 × mass × √(jump height in m) × 9.81 W; ruler drop time t = √(2d ÷ g).
  • Training raises the true z-scores of the components it trains (specificity), by up to about 0.6 SD after many weeks, more for responsive people (responsiveness varies by ±40%), levelling off with a time constant of 6 weeks.
  • Typical error (TE) = SD of the test–retest differences ÷ √2; coefficient of variation CV = TE ÷ mean × 100%; smallest worthwhile change SWC = 0.2 × the between-performer SD (Hopkins, 2000). A test is sensitive enough to see the SWC when its TE is smaller than the SWC.
  • Left out: injury, fatigue from earlier tests on the same day, the weather in detail, growth and maturation, menstrual cycle, and the many published versions of each norm table. The rating tables are the widely reproduced teaching versions; check them against the tables your course uses.
