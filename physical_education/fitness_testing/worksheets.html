Fitness Testing · Teacher notesfitness-testing.html

Teacher notes

Investigation 1: 30 m sprint

What to look for: Most cluster around the middle (average); a few are excellent or poor. With mixed squads, compare boys' and girls' ratings against their own norm tables, not each other's times.

Investigation 2: Multi-stage fitness test

What to look for: Aerobic endurance (cardiovascular fitness). It is maximal: performers run until they can't keep up, so motivation matters. The level predicts VO₂ max (indirectly).

Investigation 3: Ruler drop

What to look for: A 20 cm catch is t = √(2 × 0.20 ÷ 9.81) = 0.20 s. Single drops vary a lot, so average several trials.

Investigation 4: Reliability: test and retest

What to look for: Close but not identical: points scatter around the line of perfect agreement. The scatter comes from day-to-day and trial-to-trial variation and the tester: that is reliability.

Investigation 5: Why protocols matter

What to look for: Times get slower (no warm-up, no practice) and more variable (tester error): a standardised protocol makes tests fair and reliable.

Investigation 6: Fitness profile

What to look for: A profile shows which components are good (health- or skill-related) and which need work, so training can be specific to the weaknesses that matter for their sport.

Investigation 7: Before and after training

What to look for: Most improve, by different amounts (individual response). Some apparent changes are measurement noise: a small rise may not be real.

Investigation 8: Validity: sit and reach

What to look for: Partly: arm and leg length and back flexibility also change the score, so people with the same hamstring flexibility score differently. A goniometer is a more valid measure.

Investigation 9: Submaximal: Queens College A level

What to look for: It predicts VO₂ max from the heart rate after 3 minutes of stepping, so no one runs to exhaustion. But it assumes heart rate rises with workload in the same way for everyone, so predictions can be several ml/kg/min out.

Investigation 10: Smallest worthwhile change A level

What to look for: Changes smaller than about the typical error could be noise. SWC = 0.2 × the squad's SD; a change is likely real only if it beats both the SWC and the noise (beyond the H555 specification).

Batch experiment 1: Test and retest: how big is the typical error?

Centred near 0 with an SD of about 3.6 cm, which is √2 × the typical error (about 2.6 cm). Untick the practice attempt in a real test and the second score is higher: the learning effect.
Theory (no change): 0
Result from a test run: mean -0.06, SD 3.65, SE 0.163 from 500 runs

Batch experiment 2: How many drops for a reliable ruler-drop score?

More drops help at first, then level off: averaging removes trial-to-trial error but not day-to-day variation.
Theory (√(2/π) × √(day² + trial² ÷ n)): 1: 2.9 · 2: 2.37 · 3: 2.16 · 5: 1.98 · 8: 1.87 · 10: 1.84
Result from a test run: 1: 2.96 ± 0.354 · 2: 2.54 ± 0.319 · 3: 2.77 ± 0.26 · 5: 1.91 ± 0.242 · 8: 1.8 ± 0.226 · 10: 2.14 ± 0.256 (mean ± SE, 40 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Can a retest show a real improvement?

With no real change, about a quarter still 'improve' by chance; it takes a true gain of about 1 level before nearly everyone's retest shows it.
Theory (Φ((Δ − SWC) ÷ (√2 TE))): 0: 25.4 · 0.25: 39.5 · 0.5: 55 · 0.75: 69.8 · 1: 82 · 1.5: 95.8
Result from a test run: 0: 24.2 ± 3.52 · 0.25: 40 ± 3.87 · 0.5: 54.2 ± 3.75 · 0.75: 74.2 ± 2.71 · 1: 79.2 ± 2.71 · 1.5: 99.2 ± 0.833 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Ruler drop: distance and reaction time

Distance grows with the square of the reaction time: double the time, four times the distance, which is why the ruler drop separates slow reactions well.
Theory (d = ½gt²): 0.12: 7.06 · 0.15: 11 · 0.18: 15.9 · 0.21: 21.6 · 0.24: 28.3 · 0.27: 35.8
Result from a test run: 0.12: 7.35 ± 0.358 · 0.15: 11.2 ± 0.374 · 0.18: 16.2 ± 0.355 · 0.21: 21.1 ± 0.814 · 0.24: 28.3 ± 0.824 · 0.27: 37.3 ± 0.897 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Does the bleep test measure VO₂ max?

On average the prediction follows the true value (valid for groups), but individuals scatter by a few ml/kg/min: pacing, running technique and motivation also change the level reached.
Theory (perfect agreement): 38: 38 · 42: 42 · 46: 46 · 50: 50 · 54: 54 · 58: 58
Result from a test run: 38: 40.2 ± 0.671 · 42: 43.4 ± 0.802 · 46: 45.4 ± 0.608 · 50: 50.1 ± 1.26 · 54: 53.5 ± 0.501 · 58: 54.7 ± 1.01 (mean ± SE, 10 runs each)
