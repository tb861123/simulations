How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Teams have a true strength s drawn from a bell curve (spread = its SD), strongest first; the best team can be given an extra edge. Names are made up and shuffled, so a name tells you nothing about strength.
  • Matches: each side's goals are a Poisson random number with mean λ = g × e^(±(s_home − s_away) ÷ 2 ± h ÷ 2), where g is the average goals per team and h the home advantage. With g = 1.35 (2.7 goals a match) and h = 0.3 about 47% of matches are home wins, close to the Premier League before 2020. The exact chances of a win, draw or loss come from adding up the chance of every scoreline.
  • League: every team plays every other twice, home and away, in a random fixture order; 3 points for a win, 1 for a draw; ties on points split by goal difference, then goals scored. Cup: the 16 strongest teams, a random draw, one match per tie (a coin toss decides who is at home; the final is at a neutral ground), penalties are a 50 : 50 lottery.
  • Theory: the cup-winning chance is exact (worked out through the bracket from the chance of winning each tie). The league-winning chance uses a normal approximation: each team's points total is treated as normal with the mean and variance from its fixtures, independently of the others. The spread of points from luck alone and the expected points per game are exact.
  • Manager of the month: the season is split into months of M matches; the team with most points per game (then goal difference) wins. "Next month" is its following M matches; its expected points come from its true strength against those opponents.
  • Sample size: the correlation between points per game and true quality after n matches; theory r = √(σq² ÷ (σq² + σe² ÷ n)), where σq² is the spread of true quality and σe² the luck in one match's points (classical test theory: observed = true + error).
  • Drug testing: each athlete dopes with the chance set by the base rate. A test is positive for a doper with chance = sensitivity, and for a clean athlete with chance = false-positive rate. The re-test is assumed independent of the first test (in reality the B sample is the same urine, so its errors are not independent). PPV = sens × prev ÷ (sens × prev + fpr × (1 − prev)). Sensitivity, false-positive rate and the share who dope are illustrative: none is known exactly.
  • Left out: form that really changes during a season (injuries, transfers, a new manager), red cards, tactics, fixture congestion, matches that are not independent, extra time in the cup, and real penalty skill. Real teams' strengths change; here they are fixed for a season, so any "form" you see is luck.
