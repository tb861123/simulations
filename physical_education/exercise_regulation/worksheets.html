Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Before the start

What to look for: The anticipatory rise: adrenaline is released as the athlete prepares, acting on the SA node. Raise the anxiety slider: the rise is bigger.

Investigation 2: Q = SV × HR

What to look for: Q rises from about 5 to 15–20 L/min. HR roughly doubles or more; SV rises too, mainly early, as venous return increases (Starling's law), then levels off.

Investigation 3: Where does the blood go?

What to look for: The vascular shunt: arterioles and pre-capillary sphincters to the muscles dilate, those to the gut and kidneys constrict. Muscles get about 20% of Q at rest and 80% in hard exercise (of a much bigger Q).

Investigation 4: Trained against untrained

What to look for: Training enlarges the heart (bigger SV), so the same output needs fewer beats: bradycardia at rest (about 50 bpm), lower HR at the same pace, faster recovery.

Investigation 5: Which receptor? A level

What to look for: Proprioceptors respond within seconds of movement; chemoreceptors follow as CO₂ and H⁺ build up and stay active into the recovery. The CCC sends impulses down the sympathetic accelerator nerve to the SA node.

Investigation 6: Standing still after a sprint A level

What to look for: Without the muscle pump, blood pools in the legs, venous return falls, SV and blood pressure drop: dizziness. An active cool-down keeps the pump working and clears lactate faster.

Investigation 7: Cardiovascular drift A level

What to look for: Sweating reduces plasma volume and more blood goes to the skin, so venous return and SV fall; HR drifts upwards to keep Q up. Drinking reduces the drift.

Batch experiment 1: Heart rate and intensity

Close to a straight line from resting to maximum heart rate: the basis of training zones (Karvonen).
Result from a test run: 0: 77.4 ± 1.31 · 20: 93.2 ± 1.07 · 40: 118 ± 0.933 · 60: 143 ± 1.01 · 80: 181 ± 1.83 · 100: 204 ± 1.65 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Trained and untrained hearts

About 20 bpm lower at rest and at moderate work: a bigger stroke volume means fewer beats for the same output.
Result from a test run: 0: 19.2 ± 1.95 · 20: 14.2 ± 1.9 · 40: 14.3 ± 2.43 · 60: 12.4 ± 1.12 · 80: 6.7 ± 1.88 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Cardiovascular drift and temperature

The hotter it is, the more sweat, the smaller the plasma volume and the bigger the drift.
Result from a test run: 5: 3.05 ± 0.902 · 10: 2.4 ± 0.984 · 15: 2.95 ± 1.05 · 20: 5.36 ± 0.658 · 25: 15.6 ± 0.983 · 30: 18.6 ± 0.906 · 35: 20.2 ± 0.971 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Anticipatory rise and anxiety

Adrenaline released before the start raises HR more in anxious athletes.
Result from a test run: 0: 8.12 ± 0.984 · 0.25: 11.8 ± 1.13 · 0.5: 17.6 ± 0.887 · 0.75: 22.1 ± 0.539 · 1: 24.9 ± 0.397 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)
