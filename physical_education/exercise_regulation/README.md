# Heart, Lungs and Blood in Exercise

A single, self-contained HTML file: a training session second by second, showing how the cardiac and respiratory control centres respond: receptors, nerves and adrenaline, stroke volume and venous return, the vascular shunt and cardiovascular drift. It needs no install and no
internet: **open `exercise-regulation.html` in Chrome or Edge**. Printable worksheets (one per
investigation and per batch experiment, with teacher notes) are in
`worksheets.html`.

Kit-built: made from `tools/kit/src/exercise_regulation.js` by `node build.js` (run inside
`tools/kit/`). Do not edit `exercise-regulation.html` by hand.

- **Standard (GCSE):** OCR GCSE (9–1) Physical Education (J587) 1.1: heart rate, stroke volume and cardiac output, breathing, vascular shunting, short-term effects of exercise.
- **Advanced (A level):** OCR A level Physical Education (H555) 1.1: the cardiac control centre and its receptors, adrenaline, Starling's law and venous return, the vascular shunt, a-vO₂ difference, cardiovascular drift and the respiratory control centre.

## What it shows

Three sessions (a steady run, intervals, a sprint then standing still) for a trained or untrained athlete of any age, in hot or cold air, drinking or not. The picture shows the receptor inputs to the cardiac control centre, a beating heart (HR, SV, Q), breathing lungs (VE = TV × f) and where the blood goes. A level adds a beta-blocker switch and the skeletal muscle pump.

## Batch run

| Experiment | Result when tested (theory) |
|---|---|
| Heart rate and intensity | 77 bpm at rest to 143 at 60% and 204 at 100% of VO₂max |
| Trained and untrained hearts | the trained heart beats 19 bpm slower at rest and 12 bpm slower at 60% |
| Cardiovascular drift and temperature | 3 bpm drift at 5–15 °C, 19 bpm at 30 °C |
| Anticipatory rise and anxiety | 8 bpm with no anxiety, 25 bpm at maximum |
