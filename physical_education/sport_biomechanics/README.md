# Sport Biomechanics

A single, self-contained HTML file about the physics of the moving body,
with an athlete built from 14 body segments of standard length and mass. It
needs no install and no internet: **open `sport-biomechanics.html` in Chrome
or Edge**. Printable worksheets (one per investigation and per batch
experiment, with teacher notes) are in `worksheets.html`.

★ Enhanced: built from `tools/kit/src/sport_biomechanics.js` by `node build.js`
(run inside `tools/kit/`). Do not edit `sport-biomechanics.html` by hand. The
earlier version (with drag and lift flow pictures) and its worksheets are kept
in [`original/`](original/).

- **Standard (GCSE):** OCR J587 1.1.d: lever systems and mechanical
  advantage; planes and axes of movement.
- **Advanced (A level):** OCR H555 1.3 biomechanics: stability, impulse and
  force–time graphs, projectiles and spin, angular momentum; also WJEC Eduqas
  A level Physics option C (I = Σmr², ½Iω², coefficient of restitution).

## Seven views

Levers (five sporting examples, effort from moments, MA) · Planes and axes (a
3D athlete you can turn: running, curl, somersault, star jump, cartwheel, full
twist, golf swing) · Stability (standing, wide stance, low crouch, arabesque,
handstand; push to topple from the body's own base and centre of mass) ·
Impulse (countermovement jump on a force plate, landing with stiff or bent
knees, sprint start) · Projectiles (shot put, long jump, javelin, free kick,
tennis, badminton; drag, spin, "real athletes") · Rotation (diver in layout,
pike or tuck; skater's spin) · Bounce (coefficient of restitution).
