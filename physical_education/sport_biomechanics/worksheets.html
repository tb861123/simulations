Teacher notes

Investigation 1: A third-class lever

What to look for: Fulcrum (elbow), effort (biceps, 4 cm from the elbow), load (dumbbell, 33 cm away). The effort arm is much shorter, so MA < 1: the biceps pulls about 8 times the load, but the hand moves 8 times further and faster. Most levers in the body are third class.

Investigation 2: A second-class lever

What to look for: Second class: load (body weight) between the fulcrum (ball of the foot) and effort (calf muscles at the heel). The effort arm is longer than the load arm, so MA > 1: a big load can be moved with a smaller effort.

Investigation 3: Planes and axes

What to look for: Running: sagittal plane, transverse axis. Star jump: frontal plane, frontal axis. Full twist: transverse plane, longitudinal axis.

Investigation 4: Stability A level

What to look for: The push needed = weight × (distance from the line of gravity to the edge of the base) ÷ height of the centre of mass. A wide base and a low centre of mass are most stable (the crouch); the arabesque balances on one small foot, so a small push topples it. A sprinter in the set position deliberately leans so the line of gravity is near the front edge, ready to move.

Investigation 5: Impulse in a jump A level

What to look for: Dipping down needs a net downward force (force < weight). The net impulse (area above body weight minus area below) = m × take-off velocity, and height = v² ÷ 2g. More force for longer = more impulse = higher jump.

Investigation 6: Bend your knees A level

What to look for: The impulse needed to stop is the same (m × landing speed), so a longer contact time means a smaller average force: the peak falls from many times body weight to about two to three times.

Investigation 7: Shot put: the best angle A level

What to look for: Because the shot is released 2 m above the ground, the best angle is about 42°. Real athletes can push harder at flatter angles, so their best angle is lower still: about 37°.

Investigation 8: Spin and swerve A level

What to look for: Topspin makes the air flow faster under the ball (lower pressure below), so a Magnus force pulls it down: it dips and lands sooner. Backspin lifts it and it floats.

Investigation 9: Tuck to spin A level

What to look for: L = Iω is constant in the air. Tucking cuts I to about a third, so ω triples: about 2 somersaults in tuck against about 1 in layout.

Investigation 10: Coefficient of restitution A level

What to look for: Tennis ball on concrete: e ≈ 0.75 (bounces to about 55% of the drop height). A warm squash ball is bouncier than a cold one. Kinetic energy falls by a factor of e² at each bounce.

Batch experiment 1: Shot put: best angle and release height

45° only from ground level. The higher the release, the lower the best angle: about 42° for a shot putter.
Theory (arctan(v ÷ √(v² + 2gh))): 0: 45 · 0.5: 44.2 · 1: 43.5 · 1.5: 42.9 · 2: 42.2 · 2.5: 41.6 · 3: 41
Result from a test run: 0: 45 ± 0 · 0.5: 44.2 ± 0 · 1: 43.5 ± 0 · 1.5: 42.9 ± 0 · 2: 42.2 ± 0 · 2.5: 41.6 ± 0 · 3: 41 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Tuck, pike or layout?

The tuck turns about twice as many somersaults as the layout: same L, smaller I, bigger ω.
Result from a test run: 1: 0.899 ± 0 · 2: 1.8 ± 0 · 3: 1.97 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Impulse and jump height

More force for the same time means more net impulse, a faster take-off (v = J ÷ m) and a higher jump: height grows with v², so it rises faster than the force.
Theory (h = (J/m)² ÷ 2g): 1.8: 0.0471 · 2.1: 0.143 · 2.4: 0.29 · 2.7: 0.489 · 3: 0.74
Result from a test run: 1.8: 0.0471 ± 1.03e-17 · 2.1: 0.143 ± 2.73e-17 · 2.4: 0.29 ± 3.51e-17 · 2.7: 0.489 ± 5.36e-17 · 3: 0.74 ± 1.27e-16 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Push to topple

Halve the height of the centre of mass and the push needed doubles: crouch to be stable (a rugby scrum, a wrestler).
Theory (W × d ÷ h): 0.4: 258 · 0.6: 172 · 0.8: 129 · 1: 103 · 1.2: 85.8
Result from a test run: 0.4: 258 ± 0 · 0.6: 172 ± 0 · 0.8: 129 ± 0 · 1: 103 ± 0 · 1.2: 85.8 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 1 runs each)

Batch experiment 6: Measuring e by eye

Each reading gives e within about ±0.01; averaging five drops shrinks the error by √5.
Theory (true e): 0.75
Result from a test run: mean 0.75, SD 0.00913, SE 5.27e-4 from 300 runs
