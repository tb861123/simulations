Teacher notes

Investigation 1: One neuron, one straight line

What to look for: A single neuron's output is σ(w₁x + w₂y + b), so its boundary (output = 0.5) is the straight line w₁x + w₂y + b = 0. Gradient descent nudges the weights after each example until the line separates the classes, usually within a few epochs.

Investigation 2: Learning rate

What to look for: Too small: steady but very slow progress. Too large: the weights overshoot, so the loss jumps about. A good learning rate is a compromise.

Investigation 3: XOR defeats one neuron

What to look for: No straight line can separate XOR, so a single neuron gets stuck around 50–75% accuracy, however long it trains. This (Minsky and Papert, 1969) is why hidden layers are needed.

Investigation 4: A hidden layer solves XOR A level

What to look for: Each hidden neuron draws its own line; the output neuron combines them into a bent boundary that separates XOR completely (usually within a couple of hundred epochs).

Investigation 5: Circles and spirals A level

What to look for: A circle needs at least 3 straight pieces (a triangle-ish boundary), so 2 hidden neurons is not enough. The spiral twists several times and needs many neurons and many epochs: a deeper or wider network can represent more complicated boundaries.

Investigation 6: Overfitting A level

What to look for: Training accuracy keeps rising, but test accuracy stalls or falls: the network learns the noise in the few training points (overfitting). More data, fewer neurons or stopping early all help.

Batch experiment 1: How fast does one neuron learn a straight line?

Nearly always above 95%: a straight boundary is exactly what one neuron can represent. The few errors are points close to the line.
Result from a test run: mean 0.969, SD 0.0112, SE 0.00144 from 60 runs

Batch experiment 2: Single neuron against XOR

Usually around 50%, never much above 75%: any straight line gets at least one quadrant wrong. A hidden layer is needed.
Theory (best possible for a straight line: about ¾): 0.75
Result from a test run: mean 0.514, SD 0.0832, SE 0.0107 from 60 runs

Batch experiment 3: Hidden neurons and the circle (A level)

One neuron is no better than guessing well; accuracy climbs as hidden neurons are added (at least 3 straight pieces are needed to wrap round a circle) and levels off around 4–6. Single runs vary because the starting weights are random.
Result from a test run: 0: 0.606 ± 0.072 · 1: 0.684 ± 0.0175 · 2: 0.682 ± 0.0102 · 3: 0.803 ± 0.0493 · 4: 0.887 ± 0.0217 · 6: 0.895 ± 0.0215 · 8: 0.842 ± 0.0373 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Overfitting and training-set size (A level)

With little data the network memorises the training points and does worse on new ones (a big gap). More data shrinks the gap.
Result from a test run: 20: 0.178 ± 0.0583 · 40: 0.112 ± 0.0828 · 80: 0.0481 ± 0.0608 · 160: -9.72e-4 ± 0.00376 · 320: -0.00479 ± 0.0396 (mean ± SE, 3 runs each)
