Teacher notes

Investigation 1: A vending machine

What to look for: Any sequence totalling at least 30p reaches the accepting state: TTT, TF, FT, FF… The machine only 'remembers' the total so far (its state), not the coins themselves.

Investigation 2: A door lock

What to look for: 21123 opens (it ends with 1, 2, 3); 1213 doesn't. A 1 is always a possible start of the code, so the machine keeps it.

Investigation 3: Divisible by 3

What to look for: 6 and 9 are accepted (ending in S0, remainder 0); 7 is not. Each state is the remainder so far. A machine with just 3 states can check numbers of any length.

Investigation 4: A Turing machine adds 1 A level

What to look for: It moves right to the blank, then goes back left turning trailing 1s into 0s until it turns a 0 into 1: 1011 → 1100 (12). The rule table is the whole 'program'.

Investigation 5: Unary addition A level

What to look for: In unary, 3 + 2 is 111 then 11. The machine replaces the + with a 1 (now six 1s) and erases one 1 from the end, leaving five.

Investigation 6: Busy beaver A level

What to look for: It halts after 14 steps leaving six 1s in a row: the most any 3-state machine of this kind can write. Whether an arbitrary machine ever halts cannot be decided by any program: the halting problem.

Batch experiment 1: Random binary strings: divisible by 3?

Close to 1/3 for longer strings, as expected for multiples of 3. The machine's answer matches the arithmetic for every string.
Theory (exact count): 1: 0.5 · 2: 0.5 · 3: 0.375 · 4: 0.375 · 6: 0.344 · 8: 0.336 · 12: 0.333 · 16: 0.333
Result from a test run: 1: 0.482 ± 0.0104 · 2: 0.532 ± 0.0239 · 3: 0.371 ± 0.0187 · 4: 0.368 ± 0.0129 · 6: 0.351 ± 0.0152 · 8: 0.342 ± 0.0138 · 12: 0.333 ± 0.0126 · 16: 0.352 ± 0.0154 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Random strings ending in 01

The last two bits are 01 with probability ½ × ½ = ¼, whatever the length.
Theory (¼): 2: 0.25 · 3: 0.25 · 4: 0.25 · 6: 0.25 · 8: 0.25 · 12: 0.25
Result from a test run: 2: 0.246 ± 0.00551 · 3: 0.243 ± 0.0161 · 4: 0.262 ± 0.0188 · 6: 0.267 ± 0.00991 · 8: 0.245 ± 0.00687 · 12: 0.25 ± 0.00828 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Steps for the Turing machine to add 1 (A level)

Linear in n: it must walk to the end of the number. The carry usually stops quickly (on average after about 2 bits).
Theory (≈ n + 3 (n moves right, then about 2 back)): 2: 5 · 4: 7 · 6: 9 · 8: 11 · 12: 15 · 16: 19
Result from a test run: 2: 5.05 ± 0.0412 · 4: 6.96 ± 0.0432 · 6: 8.96 ± 0.0911 · 8: 10.9 ± 0.0746 · 12: 15 ± 0.0655 · 16: 19 ± 0.136 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)
