Teacher notes

Investigation 1: Asch's line study

What to look for: About a third of answers conform (Asch: 36.8%); about a quarter never conform and three-quarters conform at least once. Big individual differences.

Investigation 2: Group size

What to look for: It rises sharply up to about 3 confederates, then levels off: adding more makes little difference (Asch, 1955).

Investigation 3: A dissenter

What to look for: It falls to about a quarter of its level (Asch found 5.5%). Social support breaks the group's unanimity and frees the participant from normative pressure: a key way to resist social influence.

Investigation 4: Harder task, private answers

What to look for: Harder tasks increase conformity: informational influence (people look to others when unsure). Private answers reduce it: much of the conformity was normative (compliance, not changed belief).

Investigation 5: Milgram's obedience study

What to look for: About 65% (26 of 40) gave the maximum shock; all went to at least 300 V in Milgram's study. Each sample of 40 differs a little by chance.

Investigation 6: Situational variables

What to look for: Touch (30%) and an ordinary-clothed or phoning experimenter (20%) reduce obedience most. Closeness to the victim and the legitimacy and presence of the authority matter more than the building.

Investigation 7: Minority influence A level

What to look for: A consistent minority slowly gains converts, and then faster as each convert adds pressure (the snowball effect). An inconsistent minority makes little headway: consistency is the key (Moscovici's blue-green slides).

Batch experiment 1: Asch: group size

Rises steeply to 3 then plateaus, as Asch found. Each point is one study of 40 people, so the points scatter around the model's average.
Theory (model average): 1: 3.32 · 2: 14.7 · 3: 32.4 · 4: 34.6 · 6: 36.8 · 8: 36.8 · 12: 36.8 · 15: 36.8
Result from a test run: 1: 3.49 ± 0.131 · 2: 14.3 ± 0.733 · 3: 36.2 ± 1.97 · 4: 35.2 ± 3.34 · 6: 37 ± 3.06 · 8: 39.1 ± 2.23 · 12: 36.7 ± 2.52 · 15: 33.2 ± 1.51 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Asch: who never conforms?

Close to a quarter on average: people differ a lot in their tendency to conform. Samples of 40 give anything from about 10% to 40%.
Theory (Asch: about 25%): 0.25
Result from a test run: mean 0.249, SD 0.0701, SE 0.00405 from 300 runs

Batch experiment 3: Milgram's variations

Samples of 40 vary by about ±7 percentage points, so small differences between real variations (each run with 40 people) should be interpreted carefully.
Theory (Milgram's percentages): 1: 65 · 2: 40 · 3: 30 · 4: 20.5 · 5: 47.5 · 6: 20 · 7: 10
Result from a test run: 1: 71.3 ± 2.6 · 2: 38.8 ± 3.75 · 3: 33.1 ± 3.59 · 4: 21.9 ± 5.14 · 5: 49.4 ± 4.38 · 6: 12.5 ± 1.77 · 7: 7.5 ± 1.77 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Consistency and minority influence (A level)

Little change until the minority is quite consistent; then conversions snowball. The model's rates are chosen to show the pattern, not fitted to data.
