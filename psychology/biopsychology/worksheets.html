Teacher notes

Investigation 1: One EPSP is not enough

What to look for: One EPSP lifts the membrane only about 8 mV, well short of the 15 mV needed to reach threshold (−55 mV). Then it leaks back to −70 mV.

Investigation 2: Spatial and temporal summation

What to look for: Both can: spatial summation adds EPSPs from different synapses arriving together; temporal summation adds EPSPs from one synapse arriving in quick succession before each has leaked away.

Investigation 3: Inhibition

What to look for: IPSPs push the membrane further from threshold, so the output rate falls or stops. Whether a neuron fires depends on the balance of excitation and inhibition (e.g. GABA is inhibitory).

Investigation 4: An SSRI

What to look for: The transmitter stays in the cleft longer, so each EPSP is bigger and lasts longer, and the postsynaptic neuron fires more. SSRIs (antidepressants) do this for serotonin.

Investigation 5: Fight or flight

What to look for: Adrenaline (the SAM pathway) rises within a minute and is gone in about 10. Cortisol (the HPA axis) rises slowly, peaks about 20 minutes after the threat and takes over an hour to fall back. The parasympathetic system then restores rest-and-digest.

Investigation 6: Chronic stress

What to look for: Cortisol stays raised. Short-term it helps (glucose, less inflammation), but long-term high cortisol suppresses the immune system and is linked to heart disease and poor memory.

Investigation 7: Siffre's cave A level

What to look for: It drifts later each day: the endogenous pacemaker (SCN) free-runs at about 24.5 h here. Siffre's sleep–wake cycle settled at a little over 24 hours. Without zeitgebers the clock is not reset.

Investigation 8: Jet lag A level

What to look for: Flying east needs the clock to ADVANCE, which is harder for a clock that naturally runs slow (τ > 24 h): here it takes well over a week, and longer than the same flight west. Flying west needs a delay, which the clock does more easily. Light is the zeitgeber doing the resetting.

Batch experiment 1: Temporal summation

Low rates do nothing (each EPSP fades before the next arrives). Above a few tens of Hz EPSPs pile up and the neuron starts firing: a threshold effect created by temporal summation.
Result from a test run: 10: 0.2 ± 0.122 · 25: 2 ± 0.354 · 50: 8.4 ± 1.25 · 75: 16.6 ± 1.13 · 100: 25.5 ± 1.39 · 150: 43.3 ± 2.16 · 200: 60.4 ± 2.07 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Inhibition

Inhibitory input subtracts from excitation. Enough inhibition silences the neuron completely.
Result from a test run: 0: 29.6 ± 2.02 · 20: 25.8 ± 1.01 · 40: 23.5 ± 1.06 · 60: 19.3 ± 0.86 · 80: 15.8 ± 1.44 · 100: 15.4 ± 1.59 · 140: 10.5 ± 0.548 (mean ± SE, 5 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Beta-blockers and heart rate

Beta-blockers stop adrenaline acting on the heart, so the body still releases adrenaline but the heart does not race: used for performance anxiety.
Theory (model, typical person): 0: 130 · 0.2: 118 · 0.4: 105 · 0.6: 92.9 · 0.8: 80.5
Result from a test run: 0: 130 ± 0.115 · 0.2: 118 ± 0.086 · 0.4: 105 ± 0.0663 · 0.6: 92.9 ± 0.0429 · 0.8: 80.4 ± 0.0182 (mean ± SE, 6 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Jet lag: days to re-entrain (A level)

Eastward flights take longer to recover from than westward ones of the same size: the clock runs slightly slow, so delaying (west) is easier than advancing (east).
Result from a test run: -9: 11 ± 0 · -6: 7 ± 0 · -3: 3 ± 0 · 3: 4 ± 0 · 6: 9 ± 0 · 9: 13 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 4 runs each)
