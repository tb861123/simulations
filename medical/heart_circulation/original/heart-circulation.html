Pressure–volume loop (left ventricle)

Each beat goes round the loop anticlockwise: filling (bottom), squeezing with valves shut (right side), ejecting (top), relaxing (left side). The width of the loop is the stroke volume; its area is the work done by the heart.

How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Compartments: 4 heart chambers and 5 vessel sections (aorta, body arteries, body veins, pulmonary arteries, pulmonary veins). Capillaries are the resistance between arteries and veins. Total blood volume is shared between them.
  • Chambers (time-varying elastance): P = E(t)·(V − V0). E rises smoothly from its relaxed value Emin to Emax as the muscle contracts, then falls again. Left ventricle Emax 2.6, Emin 0.075 mmHg/mL; right ventricle 0.55 and 0.035; atria about 0.3 and 0.15.
  • Timing: the atria contract first (P wave); after the AV-node delay (about 0.15 s) the ventricles contract for 0.16 + 0.13T seconds and relax for 55% of that (T = time per beat). At fast rates this leaves very little filling time.
  • Vessels: P = (V − V0)/C. Compliance C: aorta 0.3, body arteries 1.25 (both divided by the stiffness setting), veins 110, pulmonary arteries 4, pulmonary veins 10 mL/mmHg.
  • Flow: Q = ΔP/R between compartments. Body (systemic) resistance 0.95 mmHg·s/mL (about 16 mmHg per L/min); lungs 0.085 — about a tenth, which is why lung pressures are low.
  • Valves: open only when pressure pushes forwards. Flow through an opening of area A follows Bernoulli/Gorlin: ΔP = (Q / 44.3A)². Stenosis shrinks A (aortic 3.5 cm² → 0.35 cm²). A leaky valve lets blood flow backwards through a small hole.
  • Hole in the heart: a ventricular septal defect lets blood flow between the ventricles, mostly left to right because the left side is at higher pressure.
  • Exercise: heart rate rises towards 185/min, contractility ×1.8, body resistance falls by 62% (muscle vessels widen), and the muscle pump and tightened veins return about 800 mL of extra blood to the heart.
  • Heart failure: the left ventricle's Emax falls by up to 70%, it stiffens when relaxed and enlarges (V0 grows).
  • Reflexes (optional): a simple baroreceptor reflex compares mean pressure with 93 mmHg and, over a few seconds, changes heart rate, resistance, squeeze and vein tone.
  • Conduction system and rhythms (Advanced). The SAN, AVN, bundle of His and Purkyne fibres are drawn lighting up in time with the model's beat clock (the SAN fires, the atria contract, the AVN delays the signal, then the ventricles contract from the apex upwards). They are a picture of the timing, not a model of electrical cells. Atrial fibrillation: the atria only quiver (no atrial squeeze, no P wave, small fibrillation waves) and the ventricles beat at irregular intervals (each gap 0.55–1.45 × the set gap, never shorter than one squeeze-and-relax). Ectopic beats: every 5th beat starts early in the ventricle (no P wave, a wide QRS, an inverted T wave), followed by a compensatory pause. These rhythms are in the live view only; batch runs always use a normal rhythm.
  • ECG and heart sounds are drawn from the timing, not calculated from electrical currents. "Lub" (S1) is drawn when the mitral valve shuts, "dub" (S2) when the aortic valve shuts.
  • Blood pressure shown is the aortic pressure. A cuff on the arm reads a few mmHg higher systolic.
  • Not modelled: the momentum of blood (so no proper dicrotic notch), the pericardium, breathing, gravity and posture, the coronary blood supply, the kidneys' slow control of blood volume, hormones, valve leaflet mechanics, and the different branches of the circulation (brain, gut, kidneys, muscles) — the body is one combined circuit. Real hearts also change size slowly (athletes' hearts grow). The heart picture is a simplified cut-away and not to scale.
