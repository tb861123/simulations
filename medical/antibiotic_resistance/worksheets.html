Teacher notes

Investigation 1: A full course

What to look for: Each dose pushes the drug above the MIC for a few hours and kills sensitive bacteria fast; between doses the level falls and survivors start dividing again. Over a week the numbers fall below about a thousand, and the immune system finishes them off. A few partly resistant mutants may appear but are killed too.

Investigation 2: Stopping when you feel better

What to look for: Feeling better means the numbers have fallen below the 'feels ill' level, not that every bacterium is dead. If the survivors are too many for the immune system, they grow back, and the most resistant ones were the most likely to survive: they make up more of the relapse. Run it several times: chance decides each patient.

Investigation 3: Missed doses

What to look for: Missed doses leave long gaps with the drug below the MIC, when bacteria multiply freely. Treatment often fails, and partly resistant strains get a head start. The batch run 'Missed doses' shows how the chance of cure falls.

Investigation 4: Antibiotics for a cold

What to look for: No: antibiotics act on bacterial cell walls, ribosomes and enzymes, which viruses do not have. The immune system clears the virus in about a week either way. Using antibiotics for viral infections only gives bacteria elsewhere in the body more chances to evolve resistance.

Investigation 5: Resistance caught from someone else

What to look for: The sensitive majority is killed, but the resistant ones are hardly touched by the drug and soon take over: the infection returns, now almost entirely resistant. A different antibiotic is needed. This is how resistant strains like MRSA spread in hospitals.

Investigation 6: The mutant selection window A level

What to look for: When the level sits above the sensitive MIC but below the partly resistant MIC for long periods (the mutant selection window), sensitive bacteria die while partly resistant mutants grow: ideal conditions for selecting resistance. High enough doses keep levels above the resistant mutants' MIC, closing the window.

Investigation 7: Half-life and dosing A level

What to look for: A short half-life gives high peaks but long troughs below the MIC; a long half-life gives steadier levels. What matters for many antibiotics is the time above the MIC (or the area under the curve above it), which is why dosing intervals differ between drugs.

Batch experiment 1: Stopping early

Stopping in the first few days usually fails, even though the patient may feel better: the survivors grow back. Each point is a proportion of 25 patients, so it scatters by about ±10%.
Result from a test run: 1: 0 ± 0 · 2: 0 ± 0 · 3: 100 ± 0 · 4: 100 ± 0 · 5: 100 ± 0 · 6: 100 ± 0 · 7: 100 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 25 runs each)

Batch experiment 2: Low doses select resistance

Low doses sit in the mutant selection window: the drug holds back the sensitive bacteria but not the rare mutants, which take over. Adequate doses kill the mutants too.
Result from a test run: 0.25: 100 ± 0 · 0.5: 95 ± 5 · 0.75: 50 ± 11.5 · 1: 15 ± 8.19 · 1.5: 0 ± 0 · 2: 0 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 3: Missed doses

A few missed doses are tolerated; many are not.
Result from a test run: 0: 100 ± 0 · 10: 88 ± 6.63 · 20: 68 ± 9.52 · 30: 24 ± 8.72 · 40: 0 ± 0 · 50: 0 ± 0 · 60: 4 ± 4 (mean ± SE, 25 runs each)

Batch experiment 4: Resistance already present

With 10⁸ bacteria, a share of 10⁻⁸ is about one resistant cell; occasionally the immune system catches it, but almost always it survives the drug and multiplies, and the infection returns, resistant. Once resistance is in an infection, the same antibiotic will not cure it.
Result from a test run: -8: 100 ± 0 · -7: 100 ± 0 · -6: 100 ± 0 · -5: 100 ± 0 · -4: 100 ± 0 · -3: 100 ± 0 · -2: 100 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)

Batch experiment 5: Dose and cure

Too low a dose keeps the drug in the mutant selection window: cure is unreliable and resistance is selected. Higher doses cure more reliably (but real drugs have side effects that limit the dose).
Result from a test run: 0.25: 0 ± 0 · 0.5: 0 ± 0 · 0.75: 55 ± 11.4 · 1: 75 ± 9.93 · 1.5: 100 ± 0 · 2: 100 ± 0 · 3: 100 ± 0 (mean ± SE, 20 runs each)
