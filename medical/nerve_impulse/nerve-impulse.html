Nerve Impulses

How an action potential is made by sodium and potassium channels, and how it travels along an axon — faster in thick or myelinated fibres, slower in the cold or with damaged myelin.

Presets

The axon (changes restart the run)

Real myelinated fibres have nodes about 100 fibre-diameters apart.
Squid live in cool sea water; human nerves work at 37 °C.

The stimulus

Current pushed into the membrane at the left-hand end, in µA per cm² of membrane.

Disease and drugs

Damages the myelin on three internodes in the middle of a myelinated axon.
A local anaesthetic (like lidocaine at the dentist) or TTX (puffer-fish poison), put on one patch of the axon.
Like TEA: blocks the K⁺ channels that end each impulse, all along the axon.
Each channel really flicks open and shut at random. In thin axons with few channels this matters near threshold.

What's happening

Measurements

Ion currents at electrode 1

Na⁺ flowing in is drawn downwards (inward current); K⁺ flowing out is drawn upwards. The Na⁺ current comes first and is quick; the K⁺ current is slower and ends the impulse.
How the model works (and what it leaves out)
  • Cable: the axon is cut into 200 short pieces (unmyelinated: about 25 length-constants long, shorter when warm) or 22 internodes of 6 pieces plus a node (myelinated). Batch runs use 100 pieces, or 14 internodes of 4 pieces, to run faster. Neighbouring pieces are joined by the resistance of the axoplasm (35.4 Ω·cm).
  • Membrane: capacitance 1 µF/cm². Hodgkin–Huxley channels: INa = gNam³h(V − ENa), IK = gKn⁴(V − EK), plus a small leak. Squid values gNa = 120, gK = 36, gL = 0.3 mS/cm²; ENa = +45 mV, EK = −82 mV. All of HH's voltages are shifted down 5 mV so rest is the textbook −70 mV.
  • Gates: dx/dt = α(V)(1 − x) − β(V)x for x = m, h, n, using Hodgkin and Huxley's own α and β formulas.
  • Temperature: gate rates speed up 3× for every 10 °C (Q10 = 3) and channel currents 1.5× (Q10 = 1.5), from HH's 6.3 °C. Human axons use the same channel equations at 37 °C — real mammalian channels differ in detail.
  • Myelin: fibre diameter D, axon 0.7D inside it. The sheath is many wraps of membrane (each 17 nm), so the internode's capacitance and leak are divided by twice the number of wraps. Nodes of Ranvier are 1 µm long with 5× the squid Na⁺ channel density. Damaged myelin (multiple sclerosis) has its capacitance and leak raised by up to 41×.
  • Drugs: the sodium blocker removes that fraction of Na⁺ channels in the patch from 45% to 60% along the axon. The potassium blocker removes that fraction of K⁺ channels everywhere.
  • Noise (optional): each piece has about 60 Na⁺ and 18 K⁺ channels per µm²; the gates get random kicks whose size depends on how few channels there are (Fox's Langevin method).
  • Maths: gates by exponential Euler; voltages by the implicit (backward) Euler method with a tridiagonal solver. Time step 0.001–0.025 ms, smaller when warm or myelinated.
  • Measurements: speed = distance between the electrodes (30% and 80% along) ÷ difference in the times the voltage passes −20 mV going up. Amplitude = peak minus resting voltage.
  • Not modelled: ion concentrations changing (the pumps are assumed to keep up), the outside of the axon (assumed to be a perfect conductor), other channel types (e.g. fast and slow K⁺ channels in mammals), branching, synapses, and the details of the paranodes around each node. Real squid axons fail above about 30–35 °C (heat block); this model does not. The colours and the drawing of ions are illustrations, not to scale: a real impulse moves only a tiny number of ions.
