1 · Is the impulse all-or-nothing?

Is the impulse all-or-nothing? Below threshold nothing reaches the far electrode; above it the spike is always the same size, however hard you stimulate. The step happens at the threshold found by bisection (white line). The model's spike size changes by less than 1% above threshold.

2 · Unmyelinated: does speed ∝ √diameter?

Unmyelinated: does speed ∝ √diameter? Cable theory: a wider axon has less resistance inside, so current spreads further ahead and speed ∝ √d. On log–log axes that is a straight line with gradient 0.5. To double the speed, an unmyelinated axon has to be 4 times wider — which is why squid needed giant axons.
Gradient of the log–log graph: 0.500 (theory 0.5, difference -0.0%), R² = 1.0000. So conduction speed ∝ axon diameter0.50.

3 · Myelinated: does speed ∝ diameter?

Myelinated: does speed ∝ diameter? With the gap between nodes in proportion to the diameter (100 × D) and the same g-ratio, theory says speed ∝ d: gradient 1 on log–log axes. Real human fibres follow about 6 m/s per µm (Hursh's rule); the model gives about 5.8 m/s per µm.
Gradient of the log–log graph: 0.999 (theory 1, difference -0.1%), R² = 1.0000. So conduction speed ∝ axon diameter1.00.

4 · How does temperature change the speed?

How does temperature change the speed? Warmer channels open and close faster, so the impulse speeds up. If channel currents sped up as much as the gates, speed would go as √(gate rate), Q10 = 1.73. In the model the currents speed up less (Q10 1.5), so above about 18 °C speed rises more slowly than that line. Real squid axons stop working above roughly 30–35 °C (heat block); this model keeps conducting up to 42 °C, so it disagrees with reality there.
Fitted Q10 of conduction speed (up to 30 °C): 1.53 — speed is multiplied by this for every 10 °C warmer (theory √3 = 1.73; real nerves about 1.5–1.9).

5 · Do noisy channels make firing a matter of chance?

Do noisy channels make firing a matter of chance? A thin C fibre has only a few thousand channels in each piece, so random openings matter. Near threshold the same stimulus sometimes fires the axon and sometimes doesn't. The probability of firing follows an S-shaped curve (green, a fitted cumulative normal) instead of the sharp step of the noise-free model (pink). Each point is the fraction of 16 runs that fired, so it scatters by about ±12% from chance alone.
Fitted S-curve: 50% firing at 84.25 µA/cm², spread (SD) 4.59 µA/cm² — the 10% to 90% range is 78.37 to 90.13 µA/cm².

6 · How soon can a second impulse follow?

How soon can a second impulse follow? Two pulses, each about twice threshold. If the gap is shorter than the refractory period the Na⁺ channels are still inactivated and the second pulse makes no impulse (amplitude 0). Once they have recovered a full-size second impulse travels — all-or-nothing again. There is no simple formula here: the refractory period comes out of the channel equations.
